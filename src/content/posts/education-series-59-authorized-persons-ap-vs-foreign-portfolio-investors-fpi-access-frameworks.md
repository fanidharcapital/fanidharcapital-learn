Welcome to **Lesson 59** of **Module 02: Exchanges & Operations** (**Lessons 31–60**) in the **Shree Fanidhar Capital Education Series**.

In **Lesson 58**, you saw how SEBI and the stock exchanges regulate registered stock brokers through net worth standards, internal audits, and strict client-fund segregation. Now look at the **two primary distribution and participation gateways** that connect millions of domestic investors and global institutions to **NSE** and **BSE**:

1. **Authorized Persons (`APs`):** The regulated local distribution network through which stock brokers reach retail investors across Indian cities and towns.
2. **Foreign Portfolio Investors (`FPIs`):** The regulated institutional gateway through which sovereign wealth funds, global pension funds, mutual funds, and hedge funds invest in Indian equities and debt.

Understanding how both channels are structured—and where their strict legal boundaries lie—protects retail investors from local franchise fraud while clarifying how foreign capital flows into Indian markets.

---

## 1. Authorized Persons (APs): How Local Broker Franchises Operate

Following SEBI's abolition of the legacy "sub-broker" category, **Authorized Persons (`APs`)** serve as the sole exchange-approved local representatives of a SEBI-registered stock broker.

* **Exchange Approval & Broker Vicarious Liability:** A stock broker can appoint an AP only after obtaining **prior approval from the relevant stock exchange** for specific market segments. Crucially, the **principal stock broker is legally liable for all acts of omission and commission** of its APs and their employees.
* **Exclusivity & Conflict-of-Interest Rules:** An AP registered with one broker for a given exchange segment **cannot** register as an AP with another broker—nor even trade as a client of another broker—for that same segment on that exchange.
* **Mandatory Office & Communication Transparency:** Every AP office must prominently display the principal broker's name, official bank account details, SEBI/exchange registration numbers, investor Do's & Don'ts, and the broker's **Grievance Escalation Matrix**.

### The Golden Rule for Retail Investors Dealing with an AP
Under SEBI and exchange rules, **an Authorized Person is strictly a facilitation and client-service channel—never a custodian of money or shares**:

| Activity | Permitted for an Authorized Person (`AP`)? | Operational Rule for Investors |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Client Onboarding & Terminal Access** | **Yes** (under broker supervision) | KYC, Demat opening, and trading terminals operate directly on the principal broker's systems. |
| **Receiving/Paying Money or Shares in AP's Name** | **Strictly Prohibited** | **Never transfer funds or shares to an AP's personal or firm bank/Demat account**—every rupee must go directly to the principal broker's designated **Client Bank Account (`USCNBA`)**. |
| **Collecting Cash from Clients** | **Strictly Prohibited** | Zero cash handling is allowed anywhere in the broking chain. |
| **Charging Extra Fees or Promising Assured Returns** | **Strictly Prohibited** | An AP receives commission solely from the principal broker and cannot charge separate fees or run "profit-sharing / portfolio handling" schemes on client accounts. |

## 2. Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs): How Global Capital Enters India

On the institutional side, overseas investors access Indian listed securities under the **SEBI (Foreign Portfolio Investors) Regulations, 2019** (as amended). Rather than registering separately with SEBI, tax authorities, and banks, FPIs onboard through a streamlined single-window architecture:

### FPI Categories
1. **Category I FPIs (Low-Risk / Well-Regulated Entities):** Sovereign wealth funds, central banks, government agencies, international/multilateral organizations, appropriately regulated pension funds, university funds, and regulated funds/asset managers from **FATF-compliant jurisdictions**. Category I FPIs enjoy simplified KYC/documentation and broader investment flexibility.
2. **Category II FPIs (Other Eligible Foreign Investors):** Broad-based funds not eligible under Category I, endowments, foundations, charitable organizations, corporate bodies, family offices, and individuals—subject to more detailed **Ultimate Beneficial Owner (`UBO`)** and PMLA disclosures.

### The 3-Pillar FPI Onboarding & Settlement Architecture
1. **Designated Depository Participant (`DDP`):** SEBI authorizes **DDPs** (operating via **NSDL** and **CDSL** custodians) to conduct KYC/AML due diligence and grant the **FPI Registration Certificate** on SEBI's behalf within a statutory **30-day** disposal window.
2. **Common Application Form (`CAF`):** Through a single digital filing on the **CAF portal**, an overseas applicant simultaneously applies for **SEBI FPI Registration**, **Income Tax PAN allotment**, **Demat account opening**, and **Special Non-Resident Rupee (`SNRR`) / Custodian bank accounts**.
3. **Custodian-Backed Institutional Settlement:** Unlike retail clients who settle through a single broker, FPIs execute trades across multiple institutional brokers and settle all trades through their **SEBI-registered Custodian**—where **NSDL** and **CDSL** continuously monitor **sectoral foreign investment caps** (`FPI Group` and `Company-level` foreign ownership limits) in real time.

## 3. How Foreign Investment Limit Monitoring Protects Market Integrity

Every Indian listed company has statutory foreign ownership ceilings (sectoral caps and aggregate FPI limits monitored by **NSDL** and **CDSL** alongside **RBI**):
* **Caution Limit (Within 3% of Sectoral Cap):** When aggregate FPI holding in a stock comes within **3% of the permissible foreign investment limit**, depositories trigger a **Caution Alert** to custodians and exchanges.
* **Breach / Red-Flag Restriction:** Once the aggregate FPI limit is reached, **fresh FPI purchases are automatically blocked** on the exchange trading system—FPIs can only sell shares to domestic buyers until headroom reopens.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **Authorized Persons (`APs`) extend broker reach locally under exchange approval and broker liability**, but are **strictly barred** from accepting cash, receiving client funds/shares in their own accounts, or managing client accounts on a profit-sharing basis.
* **Foreign Portfolio Investors (`FPIs`) onboard via Designated Depository Participants (`DDPs`) and the single-window Common Application Form (`CAF`)**, combining SEBI registration, PAN, Demat, and custodial bank setup.
* **NSDL and CDSL monitor FPI ownership caps in real time**, automatically halting fresh foreign buying when a company's foreign investment ceiling is reached.
