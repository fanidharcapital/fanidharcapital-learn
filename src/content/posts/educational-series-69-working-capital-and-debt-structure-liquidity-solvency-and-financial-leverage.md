## 1. Net Working Capital: Cash Tied Up in Daily Operations

**Working Capital** is the day-to-day operational money required to buy raw materials, hold inventory, extend credit to customers, and pay suppliers.

$$\text{Net Working Capital (NWC)} = \text{Current Assets} - \text{Current Liabilities}$$

For pure operational analysis, analysts exclude cash and short-term debt to measure **Core Operating Working Capital**:

$$\text{Operating Working Capital} = \text{Inventories} + \text{Trade Receivables} - \text{Trade Payables}$$

* **Positive Working Capital (`> 0`):** The company pays suppliers *before* collecting cash from customers, meaning growth requires tying up additional cash in inventory and receivables.
* **Negative Working Capital (`< 0`):** Customers pay upfront (e.g., FMCG leaders, QSR chains, telecoms, exchanges) while suppliers are paid 30–60 days later—meaning **suppliers effectively fund the company’s growth**.

## 2. The Cash Conversion Cycle (`CCC`): Measuring Speed of Cash Recovery

To compare working capital efficiency across years or peers of different sizes, convert rupee balances into **days**:

$$\text{Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)} = \text{Inventory Days} + \text{Receivable Days} - \text{Payable Days}$$

| Component | Formula | What It Measures | Analytical Signal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Inventory Days (`DIO`)** | $\frac{\text{Average Inventory}}{\text{COGS}} \times 365$ | How many days stock sits in warehouses before being sold. | Rising `DIO` warns of slowing demand or obsolete goods. |
| **Receivable Days (`DSO`)** | $\frac{\text{Average Receivables}}{\text{Revenue}} \times 365$ | How many days customers take to pay invoices after delivery. | Rising `DSO` signals weak bargaining power or aggressive revenue booking. |
| **Payable Days (`DPO`)** | $\frac{\text{Average Payables}}{\text{COGS}} \times 365$ | How many days the company takes to pay its own suppliers. | Higher `DPO` conserves cash—unless stretched due to liquidity stress. |

**Worked Example:** If a manufacturer holds inventory for **`55 days`**, collects from distributors in **`45 days`**, and pays raw-material suppliers in **`40 days`**, its **`CCC` is `60 days`** (`55 + 45 − 40`). Shaving `CCC` down to `40 days` releases 20 days of sales directly back into free cash!

## 3. Liquidity vs. Solvency: Short-Term Survival vs. Long-Term Resilience

| Ratio | Formula | Healthy Benchmark | What It Tests |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Current Ratio** *(Liquidity)* | $\frac{\text{Current Assets}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}$ | `1.2x – 2.0x` | Ability to meet obligations due within the next 12 months. |
| **Quick Ratio (`Acid-Test`)** *(Liquidity)* | $\frac{\text{Current Assets} - \text{Inventories}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}$ | `≥ 1.0x` | Immediate liquidity without relying on selling inventory. |
| **Debt-to-Equity (`D/E`)** *(Solvency)* | $\frac{\text{Total Debt}}{\text{Shareholders’ Equity}}$ | `< 0.5x` *(non-financials)* | Proportion of lender funding vs. owner capital. |
| **Net Debt / `EBITDA`** *(Solvency)* | $\frac{\text{Total Debt} - \text{Cash \& Liquid Investments}}{\text{EBITDA}}$ | `< 1.5x – 2.0x` | How many years of operating cash profit are needed to pay off net debt. |
| **Interest Coverage** *(Solvency)* | $\frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Finance Costs}}$ | `> 4.0x – 5.0x` | Cushion of operating profit available to pay annual interest. |

*(Note: Banks and NBFCs operate on financial leverage as their raw material, so `D/E` and Current Ratio do not apply to financial institutions—they are evaluated using Capital Adequacy (`CRAR`) and `NIM`, covered later in Module 03.)*

## 4. Examining Debt Structure Beyond the Headline Number

Two companies can both report `₹500 Crore` of debt yet carry vastly different risk profiles. Always inspect the **Borrowings Note** in the Annual Report for four details:

1. **Short-Term vs. Long-Term Maturity:** Funding long-term factory capex with short-term commercial paper creates **refinancing risk** if credit markets tighten.
2. **Fixed vs. Floating Interest Rates:** High floating-rate debt exposes earnings immediately when the RBI raises repo rates.
3. **Currency Denomination:** Unhedged foreign-currency loans (`ECB` / USD debt) create sudden mark-to-market losses if the Indian Rupee depreciates.
4. **Debt Covenants & Asset Pledges:** Check whether core factories or promoter shares are pledged as collateral to lenders.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **Working capital determines how much cash growth consumes**—a shorter or negative **Cash Conversion Cycle (`CCC`)** turns revenue growth directly into bank cash.
* **Liquidity ratios (`Current`, `Quick`) measure 12-month survival**, while **solvency ratios (`Net Debt / EBITDA`, `D/E`, `Interest Coverage`) measure long-term balance-sheet durability**.
* **Always look inside the Borrowings Note** to check maturity schedules, floating-rate exposure, and currency risk rather than relying on headline debt alone.
