## 1. The Four Core Futures Risk Vectors

| Risk Vector | How It Operates in Futures | Why It Hurts Unprepared Traders |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Leverage Amplification** | Upfront margin covers only a fraction of notional contract value (`Price × Lot Size`). | A routine `2%–3%` move in the underlying causes a `10%–15%+` swing against blocked margin—and wipes out over-sized accounts quickly. |
| **2. Overnight & Session Gap Risk** | News, global cues, or earnings hit while NSE/BSE cash and F&O markets are closed. | Price opens at 9:15 AM IST far past your resting stop-loss trigger, turning a planned `1R` stop into a `2R–4R` realized loss. |
| **3. Liquidity & Spread Risk** | Far-month contracts, lower-volume stock futures, or volatile sessions have thinner order books. | Wide bid-ask spreads and shallow depth increase entry/exit slippage and make fast exits costly. |
| **4. Event & Volatility Shocks** | Earnings surprises, RBI policy announcements, Union Budget, or regulatory/corporate shocks. | Triggers sudden price spikes, widened basis, margin hikes, or circuit/operating-range halts. |

---

## 2. How Risks Compound Together

In live trading, these risks rarely show up one at a time:
*   An oversized position (**leverage amplification**) held overnight into an earnings announcement (**event shock**) on a mid-tier stock future (**liquidity/gap risk**) combines all four hazards at once.
*   Surviving in derivatives comes from ensuring no single trade can combine excessive leverage with unhedged event or gap exposure.

---

## 3. Key Takeaways

*   Futures carry four primary structural risks: **leverage amplification, gap risk, liquidity/spread risk, and event shocks**.
*   When multiple risk factors overlap on the same trade, realized losses can far exceed your planned `1R` stop.
*   Control position size first, respect liquidity, and avoid carrying unbuffered overnight exposure across major event catalysts.
