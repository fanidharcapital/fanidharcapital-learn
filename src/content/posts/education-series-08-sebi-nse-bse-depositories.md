![SEBI, NSE, BSE & Depositories — Who Does What?](/images/lessons/lesson-08.png)

When you buy shares on a broker app, it feels like a single instant tap. Behind the scenes, however, your money and shares pass through a tightly regulated chain of institutions—each with a distinct, legally separated job.

In India, the institutions that form the backbone of capital markets are known as **Market Infrastructure Institutions (MIIs)**—Stock Exchanges, Clearing Corporations, and Depositories—all supervised by the regulator, **SEBI**. Understanding who does what shows why your shares remain safe even if a stockbroker shuts down.

---

## 1. SEBI — The Market Regulator (Rulemaker & Referee)

The **Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)** is the statutory regulator established under the SEBI Act, 1992, headquartered in Mumbai. SEBI does not execute trades or hold your shares; instead, it governs everyone who does.

### Core Responsibilities of SEBI
* **Investor Protection:** Enforces strict KYC norms, risk disclosures, and grievance redressal mechanisms (such as **SCORES** and **SMART ODR**).
* **Market Regulation & Surveillance:** Registers and regulates stockbrokers, mutual funds, FPIs, investment advisers, research analysts, exchanges, and depositories—while monitoring insider trading, front-running, and price manipulation.
* **Systemic Risk Control:** Sets upfront margin rules, position limits, circuit breakers, and peak margin reporting so excessive leverage cannot destabilize the market.
* **Primary Market Oversight:** Reviews Draft Red Herring Prospectuses (DRHPs) and sets listing and corporate governance norms for IPOs and listed companies.

> **Think of SEBI as the traffic authority and rulebook:** It builds the rules of the road, licenses drivers (brokers), and penalizes violations.

## 2. NSE & BSE — The Stock Exchanges (The Marketplace)

Stock exchanges provide the electronic trading platforms where buyers and sellers meet to discover fair prices transparently. India has two primary nationwide equity exchanges:

* **BSE (Bombay Stock Exchange):** Established in **1875**, BSE is Asia's oldest stock exchange. Its flagship benchmark index is the **SENSEX** (tracking 30 large, well-established companies).
* **NSE (National Stock Exchange):** Incorporated in **1992** and operational since 1994, NSE pioneered nationwide screen-based electronic trading in India. Its flagship benchmark index is the **NIFTY 50** (tracking 50 major companies across sectors).

### Core Responsibilities of Stock Exchanges
* **Order Matching:** Run high-speed electronic limit order books that match buy and sell orders based on **price-time priority** (best price first, earliest order first at the same price).
* **Price Discovery & Indices:** Publish real-time quotes, corporate announcements, circuit limits, and benchmark indices (Nifty 50, Sensex, Nifty Bank, etc.).
* **Listing & Compliance Monitoring:** Enforce continuous listing obligations on companies—such as quarterly financial results, shareholding patterns, and board disclosures.

## 3. Clearing Corporations — The Settlement Guarantor

Many investors assume the exchange itself guarantees trade settlement, but that role belongs to **Clearing Corporations**—such as **NSE Clearing Limited (NCL)** and **Indian Clearing Corporation Limited (ICCL)**.

### Core Responsibilities of Clearing Corporations
* **Central Counterparty (Novation):** Once a trade is matched on NSE or BSE, the clearing corporation steps in as the legal buyer to every seller and the legal seller to every buyer. Even if one broker defaults, your trade still settles.
* **Netting & Risk Management:** Calculates each broker's net pay-in and pay-out obligations at the end of the trading session and collects upfront margins to cover default risk.
* **T+1 Settlement Execution:** Ensures funds and securities are exchanged on the **T+1 cycle** (one working day after the trade date).

## 4. NSDL & CDSL — The Depositories (Digital Vaults)

Before 1996, shares were issued as physical paper certificates—which carried risks of theft, forgery, bad delivery, and weeks of transfer delays. Today, shares are held electronically in **Demat (Dematerialized)** form inside India's two SEBI-registered depositories:

* **NSDL (National Securities Depository Limited):** India's first electronic depository, established in **1996**.
* **CDSL (Central Depository Services (India) Limited):** Established in **1999**, widely used across retail broking platforms.

### Depositories vs. Depository Participants (DPs)
You cannot open a Demat account directly with NSDL or CDSL. Instead, you access them through a registered agent called a **Depository Participant (DP)**—which is typically your stockbroker or bank (for example, Zerodha, Kotak Securities, or HDFC Bank).

* **Where your shares actually live:** Your shares are recorded directly in **your own PAN-linked Demat account** at NSDL or CDSL—**not** on your broker's balance sheet.
* **Corporate Actions:** Depositories maintain the official electronic record of shareholders so dividends, bonus shares, stock splits, and rights entitlements are credited directly to your bank or Demat account.

## 5. Quick Comparison: Who Does What?

| Institution | Category | Primary Role | Real-World Analogy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **SEBI** | Regulator | Frames rules, licenses intermediaries, monitors fraud, protects investors. | Financial Traffic Police & Rulemaker |
| **NSE & BSE** | Stock Exchanges | Match buy and sell orders electronically; enable transparent price discovery. | Regulated Digital Marketplace |
| **NCL & ICCL** | Clearing Corporations | Guarantee trade settlement, manage margins, net funds and shares on T+1. | Escrow & Settlement Guarantor |
| **NSDL & CDSL** | Depositories | Hold shares safely in electronic Demat form and record legal ownership. | Digital Bank Locker for Shares |

## 6. How They Work Together When You Buy a Share

Suppose you buy 25 shares of an NSE-listed company at ₹500 each:
1. **Your Broker** checks your funds and routes the order to **NSE**.
2. **NSE** matches your buy order with a seller's order at ₹500.
3. **NSE Clearing (NCL)** guarantees the trade, debits ₹12,500 from your broker's clearing pool, and collects 25 shares from the seller's side.
4. **CDSL / NSDL** credits those 25 shares directly into your Demat account on **T+1**, and sends you an official SMS/email confirmation directly from the depository.
5. **SEBI** oversees every link in this chain through strict audits, segregation of client funds, and real-time surveillance.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **Separation of powers keeps investors safe:** Trading (Exchanges), settlement guarantee (Clearing Corporations), custody of shares (Depositories), and regulation (SEBI) are handled by separate institutions.
* **Your shares are ring-fenced:** Because shares sit in your personal Demat account at NSDL or CDSL, they remain yours even if your broker faces financial trouble.
* **Verify directly with your depository:** Always check the official SMS/email alerts and Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) sent directly by **NSDL or CDSL** to confirm your holdings.
