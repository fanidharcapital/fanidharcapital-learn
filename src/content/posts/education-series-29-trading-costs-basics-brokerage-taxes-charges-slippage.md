When a trader buys a stock at ₹1,000 and sells it at ₹1,002, it is tempting to assume a ₹2 per share profit. In reality, after statutory taxes, exchange fees, brokerage, and execution friction are deducted, that trade can actually close at a net loss.

In the Indian stock market (**NSE** and **BSE**), every buy and sell leg carries explicit charges—plus an invisible execution cost called **slippage**. Knowing your exact cost structure is essential for calculating your true **breakeven price**.

---

## 1. Explicit Costs: Brokerage & Statutory Charges

Every equity trade executed through a SEBI-registered broker generates a **Contract Note** detailing two broad groups of explicit costs: what your broker charges, and what the government and market institutions collect.

### 1. Brokerage (Broker's Fee)
* **What It Is:** The fee charged by your stockbroker for providing the trading platform, order routing, and execution infrastructure.
* **Pricing Models in India:**
  * **Discount Brokers:** Typically charge ₹0 on Equity Delivery and a flat fee (such as up to ₹20 per executed order) on Intraday and F&O trades.
  * **Full-Service Brokers:** Usually charge a percentage of turnover (e.g., 0.10% to 0.50% on delivery) in exchange for research reports, relationship managers, and advisory support.

### 2. Securities Transaction Tax (STT)
* **What It Is:** A direct central government tax levied on the value of securities bought or sold on recognized Indian stock exchanges.
* **How It Applies:**
  * **Equity Delivery:** Charged on **both** the buy and sell sides (currently 0.1% on buy value + 0.1% on sell value).
  * **Equity Intraday:** Charged **only on the sell side** (currently 0.025% on sell value).

### 3. Exchange Transaction Charges (NSE / BSE)
* **What It Is:** Fees collected by **NSE** or **BSE** for using their electronic matching engines.
* **How It Applies:** Calculated as a small percentage of total trade turnover (both buy and sell legs).

### 4. SEBI Turnover Fees
* **What It Is:** A regulatory fee collected by the **Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)** to fund market regulation and investor protection.
* **How It Applies:** Currently ₹10 per crore (0.0001%) on both buy and sell turnover.

### 5. Stamp Duty
* **What It Is:** A central government duty levied on the transfer of financial securities.
* **How It Applies:** Charged **only on the buy side**—0.015% (₹1,500 per crore) for Equity Delivery and 0.003% (₹300 per crore) for Equity Intraday.

### 6. Goods and Services Tax (GST)
* **What It Is:** An 18% indirect tax applied **not** on your share value, but on the **sum of Brokerage + Exchange Transaction Charges + SEBI Turnover Fees**.

### 7. Depository Participant (DP) Charges (Delivery Sells Only)
* **What It Is:** A flat per-scrip, per-day fee charged by the depository (**CDSL** or **NSDL**) and your broker whenever shares are debited from your Demat account upon selling delivery holdings (typically around ₹13–₹16 + GST per company sold on that day, regardless of quantity).

## 2. Quick Reference: Who Charges What?

| Cost Component | Paid To | Delivery (Buy / Sell) | Intraday (Buy / Sell) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Brokerage** | Stockbroker | Often ₹0 (Discount) or % | Flat per order or % |
| **STT** | Central Govt | Both Buy & Sell | Sell side only |
| **Exchange Charges** | NSE / BSE | Both Buy & Sell | Both Buy & Sell |
| **SEBI Fees** | SEBI | Both Buy & Sell | Both Buy & Sell |
| **Stamp Duty** | Govt (via Exchange) | Buy side only | Buy side only |
| **GST (18%)** | Central & State Govt | On Brokerage + Exch + SEBI | On Brokerage + Exch + SEBI |
| **DP Charges** | CDSL / NSDL + Broker | Sell side only (per scrip/day) | Not applicable |

## 3. The Hidden Cost: Slippage

Unlike brokerage and taxes—which appear cleanly on your contract note—**Slippage** is the hidden difference between the price you *expected* to get when clicking Buy/Sell and the actual average price at which your order filled.

* **Why Slippage Happens:**
  * Placing **Market Orders** in stocks with wide **Bid-Ask spreads**.
  * Trading large quantities in a thin order book where your order consumes multiple price levels.
  * Fast-moving market conditions (such as the 9:15 AM opening minutes or major news releases).
* **Example:** You see an LTP of ₹500.00 and place a Market Buy order for 1,000 shares. Because only 200 shares are available at ₹500.10 and the remaining 800 shares fill at ₹501.20, your average fill price is ₹500.98—costing you ₹980 in immediate execution slippage before the trade even moves.

---

## 4. How Costs Define Your Breakeven Point

Your **Breakeven Price** is the minimum price movement required just to recover all round-trip charges:

$$\text{Net Profit / Loss} = (\text{Sell Value} - \text{Buy Value}) - (\text{Total Statutory Charges} + \text{Brokerage} + \text{Slippage})$$

* **Why Overtrading Hurts:** Even if a strategy wins 55% of the time, taking dozens of tiny 0.15% intraday scalps allows taxes, exchange fees, and slippage to steadily erode account equity.
* **Why Delivery Holds Have Higher One-Time Friction:** Because Equity Delivery carries 0.1% STT on both sides plus DP charges on exit, frequent short-term churning in delivery is far costlier per rupee of turnover than disciplined swing or long-term holding.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **Always calculate net P&L, not gross P&L:** Review your broker's **Contract Note** regularly to see the exact breakdown of STT, stamp duty, exchange fees, GST, and DP charges.
* **Use Limit Orders to control slippage:** Except in urgent exits on ultra-liquid large caps, Limit Orders protect you from unexpectedly wide Bid-Ask spreads.
* **Match your trade horizon to your cost structure:** Larger, well-planned price targets easily absorb transaction costs, while frequent low-edge trades get eaten alive by friction.
