## Overview

Once you understand how Indian equity trades clear and settle under **T+1**, the next foundational question is: **what are you actually looking at when a share or index moves?**

In **Lesson 02** of **Module 01: Market Foundations**, we break down how equity shares represent fractional ownership, how the **primary market** differs from the **secondary market**, and how **market-cap-weighted indices** like **NIFTY 50** and **SENSEX** summarize broader market behavior.

---

## 1. Equity Shares: Fractional Ownership, Not a Lottery Ticket

When a company issues equity shares, it divides its ownership into standardized units:

* **Ownership Claim:** Each ordinary share represents a proportionate stake in the company after senior obligations are met.
* **Primary Market (Capital Raising):** Companies issue new shares (such as via an IPO or rights issue) to raise capital for business growth, debt reduction, or expansion.
* **Secondary Market (Price Discovery):** Investors buy and sell existing shares among one another on exchanges like **NSE** and **BSE**—the company itself does not receive funds from routine secondary-market trades.

---

## 2. How Order-Book Liquidity Shapes Price

On an electronic limit order book, prices do not move in a vacuum—they update where **buyers (bids)** and **sellers (asks)** agree to transact:

1. **Bid vs. Ask:** The highest price a buyer is willing to pay is the **best bid**; the lowest price a seller is willing to accept is the **best ask**.
2. **Bid-Ask Spread:** The gap between the best ask and best bid reflects immediate liquidity and transaction friction. Tighter spreads typically appear in liquid, high-turnover stocks.
3. **Last Traded Price (LTP):** Represents the most recent matched transaction—not a guaranteed fill price for the next order.

---

## 3. Market Indices: Why NIFTY 50 & SENSEX Don't Equal Every Stock

A benchmark index is a curated basket designed to track a specific segment of the market rather than every listed company:

* **Free-Float Market Capitalization:** Major Indian benchmarks weight constituents using **free-float market cap** (share price × shares readily available for public trading, excluding promoter/locked-in holdings as defined by index methodology).
* **Index Movement ≠ Uniform Stock Movement:** Because larger-weight constituents contribute more points to an index move, a headline index can rise or fall even when individual stocks move in mixed directions.
* **Role as a Reference Yardstick:** Indices serve as transparent benchmarks for tracking broad market direction, sector performance, and derivative underlyings—not as single stocks.

## Key Takeaways

* **Shares** represent ownership units; **secondary markets** provide liquidity and continuous price discovery after issuance.
* **Liquidity and spread** determine how smoothly orders match without unnecessary slippage.
* **Benchmark indices** reflect weighted baskets of selected companies, so headline index moves should always be read alongside breadth and liquidity.
