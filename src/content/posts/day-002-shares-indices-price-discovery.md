## Introduction

Once you understand how market participants, exchanges, clearing corporations, and depositories work together under T+1 settlement, the next core question is straightforward: **what are you actually buying or selling when a trade happens?**

In Indian equity markets, trading centers on **shares** (ownership units in listed companies), **market indices** (benchmarks that summarize how baskets of stocks move), and **price discovery** (the continuous matching of buyer bids and seller offers on exchanges).

## 1. What is a Share?

A **share** represents a fractional unit of ownership in a company's equity capital. When a company lists its shares on exchanges such as the **NSE** or **BSE**, public investors can buy and sell partial ownership stakes through a registered stockbroker into their demat account.

* **Unit of Ownership:** Holding shares makes you a part-owner (shareholder) of the underlying business in proportion to your shareholding.
* **Shareholder Rights:** Depending on the share class and company rules, equity shareholders may participate in corporate actions such as dividends, bonus/rights issues, and voting on key resolutions.
* **Capital Formation:** Issuing equity allows companies to raise growth capital without taking on fixed debt repayment obligations.
* **Return Potential & Risk:** Shares offer long-term capital appreciation and potential dividend income, while carrying market price fluctuations and business risk.

## 2. What is a Market Index?

A **market index** (such as **NIFTY 50** or **SENSEX**) tracks the combined price movement of a selected basket of representative stocks rather than a single company.

* **Measures Segment or Market Performance:** It gives a consolidated view of how a specific index basket—or a sector/theme—is performing over time.
* **Market Barometer:** Traders and investors watch benchmark indices as a quick pulse check on broader equity market sentiment and institutional activity.
* **Foundation for Index Products:** Indices serve as benchmarks for active funds and as underlying references for index funds, ETFs, and exchange-traded derivatives.
* **How Indices Are Constructed:** Most major equity indices select liquid, large companies that meet eligibility rules and weight them (commonly using free-float market capitalization) so larger tradable floats have a proportionate effect on index movement.

## 3. How Price Discovery Works

**Price discovery** is the process by which buyers and sellers arrive at a transaction price through order matching on an exchange.

* **Buyers (Demand / Bids):** Buyers enter bid prices indicating the maximum price they are willing to pay for a quantity of shares. Generally, higher bids compete more aggressively for immediate execution.
* **Sellers (Supply / Offers / Asks):** Sellers enter offer prices indicating the minimum price they are willing to accept to sell shares. Generally, lower offers are more competitive for immediate execution.
* **Order Matching & Market Price:** When a buyer's bid price and a seller's offer price align according to exchange matching rules, a trade executes at that agreed price—establishing the latest traded price (LTP).
* **Continuous Repricing:** As fresh news, earnings, macroeconomic data, order flow, and liquidity arrive, bids and offers shift continuously, moving the market price from tick to tick.
