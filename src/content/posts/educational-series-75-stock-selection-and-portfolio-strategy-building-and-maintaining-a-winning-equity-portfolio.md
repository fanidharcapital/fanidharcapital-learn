## 1. The 5-Point Fundamental Stock Screening Framework

Before adding any stock to your watchlist or portfolio, run it through five foundational filters[span_2](start_span)[span_2](end_span)[span_3](start_span)[span_3](end_span):

| Screening Pillar | Core Question | What to Look For in Practice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Durable Competitive Moat**[span_4](start_span)[span_4](end_span)[span_5](start_span)[span_5](end_span) | Can competitors easily steal market share or erode margins? | Brand pricing power, high switching costs, network effects, or structural cost leadership. |
| **2. Consistent High `ROCE` & `ROIC`**[span_6](start_span)[span_6](end_span)[span_7](start_span)[span_7](end_span) | Does the business compound capital well above its cost of capital? | Multi-year `ROCE` and `ROIC` consistently `> 15%–18%` without relying on excessive debt. |
| **3. Strong Free Cash Flow (`FCF`)**[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span)[span_9](start_span)[span_9](end_span) | Do reported accounting profits convert into real cash? | Healthy `CFO / PAT` (`~80%–100%+` over 3–5 years) and positive `FCF` after maintenance capex. |
| **4. Prudent Capital Allocation**[span_10](start_span)[span_10](end_span)[span_11](start_span)[span_11](end_span) | Does management reinvest surplus cash wisely? | High-return organic expansion, low leverage (`Net Debt / EBITDA < 1.5x`), or sensible dividends/buybacks. |
| **5. Honest & Capable Management**[span_12](start_span)[span_12](end_span)[span_13](start_span)[span_13](end_span) | Are minority shareholders treated as true partners? | Zero promoter share pledging, clean audit opinions, minimal related-party leakage, and candid disclosures. |

## 2. Five Rules of Portfolio Construction

Even great individual stock picks can underperform if position sizing and risk controls are haphazard. Build your portfolio around five structural principles[span_14](start_span)[span_14](end_span)[span_15](start_span)[span_15](end_span):

1. **Diversification (Not Over-Diversification):**[span_16](start_span)[span_16](end_span)[span_17](start_span)[span_17](end_span)
   * Holding **12 to 20 well-researched businesses** across 5–7 uncorrelated sectors captures most of the risk-reduction benefits of diversification without turning your portfolio into a closet index fund.
   * Owning 50+ stocks dilutes your best ideas and makes it impossible to track quarterly earnings and annual reports thoroughly.
2. **Sizing Based on Conviction:**[span_18](start_span)[span_18](end_span)[span_19](start_span)[span_19](end_span)
   * Allocate larger initial weights (`6%–10%`) to proven compounders with wide moats, clean balance sheets, and high earnings visibility.
   * Keep emerging turnarounds, cyclical plays, or smaller-cap bets smaller (`2%–4%`) until execution proves itself over multiple quarters.
3. **Risk Management (`Margin of Safety`):**[span_20](start_span)[span_20](end_span)[span_21](start_span)[span_21](end_span)
   * Always leave room between your estimate of **intrinsic value** and the **market price paid** so unexpected sector slowdowns or valuation compression do not cause permanent capital loss.
   * Cap single-stock exposure (`10%–12%` ceiling) and single-sector exposure (`25%–30%` ceiling) so one macro shock cannot derail the entire portfolio.
4. **Regular Review & Rebalancing:**[span_22](start_span)[span_22](end_span)[span_23](start_span)[span_23](end_span)
   * Conduct a structured **quarterly thesis check** (after `Q1–Q4` results) and an **annual deep review** (when the Annual Report is released).
   * Trim positions only when valuations become detached from fundamentals, position weights swell far beyond risk limits, or the **original investment thesis breaks** (moat erosion, governance red flag, or structural `ROIC` decline).
5. **Long-Term Holding Mindset:**[span_24](start_span)[span_24](end_span)[span_25](start_span)[span_25](end_span)
   * Compounding requires uninterrupted time—interrupting high-`ROIC` businesses solely because of short-term market noise or routine `10%–15%` corrections forfeits multi-year wealth creation.

## 3. Sample Core-Satellite Portfolio Blueprint

| Portfolio Bucket | Target Allocation | Stock Profile | Role in Portfolio |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Core Compounders** *(8–12 stocks)* | `65%–75%` | Market leaders with durable moats, `ROIC > 18%`, net-cash/low-debt balance sheets, and steady `FCF`. | Primary compounding engine and downside stabilizer. |
| **Growth & Emerging Leaders** *(4–6 stocks)* | `20%–25%` | Mid-sized businesses reinvesting `FCF` at high incremental `ROIC` into large addressable markets. | Accelerates long-term portfolio `CAGR`. |
| **Tactical / Value Opportunities** *(1–3 stocks)* | `5%–10%` | Cyclical recoveries or temporarily mispriced businesses with clear catalysts and strong balance sheets. | Opportunistic alpha with strict position caps. |

## Key Takeaways

* **Screen rigorously before buying:** Combine competitive moat durability, high `ROCE`/`ROIC`, cash conversion (`FCF`), capital allocation, and clean governance[span_26](start_span)[span_26](end_span)[span_27](start_span)[span_27](end_span).
* **Concentrate with discipline:** A focused portfolio of **12–20 stocks** sized by conviction balances meaningful upside with sensible single-stock and sector risk caps[span_28](start_span)[span_28](end_span)[span_29](start_span)[span_29](end_span).
* **Sell on broken fundamentals or extreme overvaluation, not short-term price volatility**—let high-return businesses compound over multi-year horizons[span_30](start_span)[span_30](end_span)[span_31](start_span)[span_31](end_span).
