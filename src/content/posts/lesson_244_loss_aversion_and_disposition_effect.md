In **Lesson #243**, we saw how revenge trading and overtrading compound losses. In **Lesson #244**, we examine **Loss Aversion** and the **Disposition Effect**—cognitive tendencies that flip healthy trade management upside down by **trimming winners prematurely** and **holding losers too long**.

![Educational Series #244: Loss Aversion & Disposition Effect](/images/lessons/lesson-244.png)

---

## 1. Loss Aversion & The Disposition Effect Explained

*   **Loss Aversion:** Psychologically, a loss hurts noticeably more than an equal-sized gain feels rewarding. To avoid feeling or admitting a loss, traders often cling to underwater positions hoping price will "come back to breakeven."
*   **The Disposition Effect:** When a trade moves into green territory, relief and fear of giving back unrealized gains tempt you to exit early for a small win; when a trade turns red, reluctance to lock in the loss tempts you to hold past your stop or average down into weakness.

---

## 2. How This Skews Your Payoff Ratio

*   If you routinely book **`+0.5R` to `+0.8R`** wins out of fear while letting occasional losers run to **`-2R` or `-3R`** out of hope, your **average win shrinks below your average loss**.
*   Even a decent win rate can struggle to stay profitable when **average loss > average win** and winners are cut before reaching structured targets.
*   **Fix With Pre-Set Exits:** Anchor targets to **market structure / volatility / spread rules** and honor **hard stop-losses** automatically rather than bargaining with price tick by tick.

---

## 3. Key Takeaways

*   **Loss aversion** makes taking a planned loss feel painful, while the **disposition effect** pushes traders to cut winners early and hold losers too long.
*   Pre-defined **stop-losses** and **objective target/management rules** keep your payoff profile intact.
