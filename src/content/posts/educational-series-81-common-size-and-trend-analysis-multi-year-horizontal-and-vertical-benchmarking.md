## 1. Vertical (Common-Size) Analysis: Structural Proportions

**Vertical (Common-Size) Analysis** expresses every line item on a financial statement as a **percentage of a single base figure** in the same period:

* **Common-Size Income Statement (`P&L`):** Every revenue, cost, and profit line is divided by **Total Revenue / Revenue from Operations (`100%`)**.
  $$\text{Vertical P\&L \%} = \frac{\text{Line Item (e.g., COGS, Employee Cost, EBITDA, PAT)}}{\text{Revenue from Operations}} \times 100$$
* **Common-Size Balance Sheet:** Every asset, liability, and equity line is divided by **Total Assets / Total Equity & Liabilities (`100%`)**.
  $$\text{Vertical Balance Sheet \%} = \frac{\text{Balance Sheet Line Item}}{\text{Total Assets}} \times 100$$

### Why Vertical Analysis Helps
1. **Apples-to-Apples Peer Comparison:** Comparing a `₹800 Cr` mid-cap against a `₹6,500 Cr` market leader in rupee terms hides cost structure differences—common-size percentages immediately show who spends a higher share on raw materials, marketing, or R&D.
2. **Balance-Sheet Composition Shift:** Shows whether a company's asset base is becoming more `receivable-heavy`, `inventory-heavy`, or `cash-rich`, or whether liabilities are shifting toward `borrowings`.

## 2. Horizontal (Trend) Analysis: Multi-Year Trajectory

**Horizontal (Trend) Analysis** tracks how line items move **over time across multiple periods** (typically `3–10` years or consecutive quarters), either as **period-over-period growth (`YoY %` / `QoQ %`)** or as an **index relative to a base year (`Base Year = 100`)**:

$$\text{Trend Index (Year } t\text{)} = \frac{\text{Value in Year } t}{\text{Value in Base Year}} \times 100$$

### Why Horizontal Analysis Helps
1. **Spot Divergence Early:** If `Revenue` grows at a `12%` trend index over four years while `Trade Receivables` climbs to a `185` index (nearly doubling) or `Interest Expense` rises faster than `EBIT`, trend analysis flags working-capital strain or debt pressure before headline profit collapses.
2. **Separate Structural Change From One-Offs:** Comparing multi-year trend lines shows whether a jump in margins or cash flow is repeatable or a single-year anomaly.

## 3. Worked Example: Multi-Year Trend & Common-Size Snapshot (`₹ Crore`)

Consider **Kaveri Consumer & Home Care Ltd.** across `FY24`, `FY25`, and `FY26` (`FY24 = Base 100` for trend index):

| Line Item | `FY24` (`₹ Cr`) | `FY25` (`₹ Cr`) | `FY26` (`₹ Cr`) | `FY26` Common-Size (`% of Sales`) | `FY26` Trend Index (`FY24 = 100`) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Revenue from Operations** | `₹500 Cr` | `₹560 Cr` | `₹625 Cr` | **`100.0%`** | **`125.0`** |
| **Cost of Goods Sold (`COGS`)** | `₹240 Cr` | `₹263.2 Cr` | `₹287.5 Cr` | **`46.0%`** | **`119.8`** |
| **Gross Profit** | `₹260 Cr` | `₹296.8 Cr` | `₹337.5 Cr` | **`54.0%`** | **`129.8`** |
| **Employee & Marketing Expenses** | `₹160 Cr` | `₹176.4 Cr` | `₹193.75 Cr` | **`31.0%`** | **`121.1`** |
| **`EBITDA` (`OPM`)** | `₹100 Cr` (`20.0%`) | `₹120.4 Cr` (`21.5%`) | `₹143.75 Cr` (`23.0%`) | **`23.0%`** (`OPM`) | **`143.8`** |
| **Profit After Tax (`PAT` / `NPM`)** | `₹65 Cr` (`13.0%`) | `₹81.2 Cr` (`14.5%`) | `₹100 Cr` (`16.0%`) | **`16.0%`** (`NPM`) | **`153.8`** |

* **What Vertical & Horizontal Analysis Reveal Together:** Revenue grew `25%` (`index 125.0`), while `COGS` rose slower (`index 119.8`)—lifting `Gross Margin` from `52.0%` (`260/500`) to `54.0%` (`337.5/625`) and expanding `OPM` by `+300 bps` (`20.0%` to `23.0%`). `PAT` grew `53.8%` (`index 153.8`) as operating leverage and lower interest/tax drag compounded.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **Vertical (Common-Size) analysis** turns `P&L` and `Balance Sheet` items into percentages of a common base (`Revenue` or `Total Assets`), making cross-size peer comparisons and structural checks clear.
* **Horizontal (Trend) analysis** tracks multi-year growth rates and base-year indices (`100`), highlighting early divergences between sales, costs, working capital, and profits.
* **Combine both lenses with ratios and cash flows** so you understand both *how the business is structured* and *how its trajectory is evolving*.
