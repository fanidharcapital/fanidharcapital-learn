---

## 1. Why Quantitative and Qualitative Factors Need Each Other

* **Quantitative Without Qualitative:** Numbers tell you *what* happened historically (`revenue growth`, `OPM`, `ROE/ROIC`, `CFO`, multiples), but cannot by themselves tell you *why* it happened or whether it will last if customer habits, competition, or governance shift.
* **Qualitative Without Quantitative:** Great stories about moats, leadership, or sector tailwinds can mask weak unit economics, cash burn, equity dilution, or an expensive valuation multiple.

---

## 2. Core Pillars of a Practical Equity Research Checklist

| Research Pillar | Quantitative Checks | Qualitative Checks |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Business Model & Competitive Edge** | Multi-year revenue trend, `Gross Margin` & `OPM` stability, working-capital cycle (`CCC`), and asset/capital intensity. | How the business earns and keeps money; pricing power, switching costs, scale economics, and competitive threats. |
| **2. Earnings & Cash Flow Quality** | `CFO / EBITDA` and `FCF / PAT` conversion, receivable/inventory growth vs. sales, share of non-operating/other income. | Revenue recognition practices, footnote disclosures, auditor notes/resignations, and cyclical vs. structural drivers. |
| **3. Balance Sheet & Capital Allocation** | `Debt-to-Equity`, net debt, `Interest Coverage`, `ROE / ROCE / ROIC` vs. cost of capital, payout ratio & buybacks/dilution. | Promoter ownership & share pledging, related-party transactions, board oversight, and track record of capital deployment. |
| **4. Valuation & Margin of Safety** | Trailing/forward `P/E`, `PEG`, `P/B`, `P/S`, `EV/EBITDA` vs. peers/history; conservative `DCF` or cash-flow valuation range. | Whether current market expectations match realistic business growth and risk—and whether there is a sensible margin of safety. |

## 3. Sample Stock Evaluation Scorecard (`Illustrative Template`)

Use a clear scorecard to force honest trade-offs before making a decision:

| Checklist Area | Key Question Answered | Status (`Pass / Watch / Red Flag`) | Notes / Key Observations |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Business & Moat** | Are unit economics and competitive advantages durable? | `Pass` | Steady margins and repeat demand across cycles |
| **Cash & Forensics** | Do cash flows and filings back reported profits? | `Watch` | Check receivable days in Q3–Q4; clean auditor report |
| **Governance & Capital** | Is capital allocated prudently without heavy dilution/pledge? | `Pass` | Zero promoter pledge; healthy `ROIC` on reinvested cash |
| **Valuation & Cushion** | Is valuation reasonable with adequate margin of safety? | `Watch` | Fair multiple on base case; wait for better entry if stretched |

---

## 4. Disciplined Workflow When Using Your Checklist

1. **Screen First, Deep-Dive Second:** Use basic financial and quality filters to narrow a wide universe down to candidates worth serious study.
2. **Respect Deal-Breakers:** Severe forensic red flags or persistent governance concerns should stop an idea early—don't try to "value-adjust" away fundamental trust issues.
3. **Update as Facts Change:** Re-check your thesis whenever quarterly results, capex plans, promoter disclosures, or industry dynamics shift materially.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **A strong equity research checklist pairs quantitative evidence** (margins, returns, cash flows, leverage, multiples) **with qualitative judgment** (moat durability, governance, business model, and cycle context).
* **Structured scorecards reduce bias** by forcing you to verify cash conversion, balance-sheet health, and valuation discipline consistently.
* **Keep the checklist living and practical**—use it to filter ideas, spot risks early, and revisit assumptions as new data arrives.
