Macro headlines and institutional flow numbers only become actionable when they agree with what price is actually doing on the chart. A bullish RBI liquidity stance or strong DII buying does not justify buying a breakdown below major weekly support, just as a hawkish global cue does not automatically mean shorting an index holding higher lows.

## The Three-Layer Top-Down Filter

1. **Layer 1 — Macro & Liquidity Regime:** Identify whether interest rates, inflation prints, crude oil, and USD/INR create a tailwind, headwind, or neutral backdrop for Indian equities.
2. **Layer 2 — Institutional Flow & Sector Leadership:** Check where FII/DII money is rotating—such as rate-sensitive banks and autos during easing expectations, or defensives during global risk-off sessions.
3. **Layer 3 — Chart Structure & Trigger Levels:** Map higher-timeframe support, resistance, swing highs/lows, and VWAP on Nifty, Bank Nifty, or liquid F&O stocks before risking capital.

## Alignment Matrix: When to Press vs. When to Pass

| Macro & Flow Bias | Chart Structure | Execution Decision |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bullish** | Higher highs & higher lows holding above key support | **Full-conviction long setups** on pullbacks or clean breakouts |
| **Bullish** | Below major resistance or breaking swing lows | **Wait or size down**; let price reclaim structure first |
| **Bearish** | Lower highs & lower lows breaking support with volume | **High-probability short / bearish spread** setups |
| **Bearish** | Holding strong multi-week demand zone | **Avoid chasing shorts**; wait for support failure |

## Practical Execution Checklist

* Never enter a trade solely because a macro narrative sounds convincing; demand a defined invalidation level on the chart.
* Use the macro bias to decide **which side of the market deserves larger risk allocation**, and use price action to decide **exact entry and stop-loss placement**.
* When macro cues and chart structure conflict, treat the environment as tactical range trading with smaller position sizes.
