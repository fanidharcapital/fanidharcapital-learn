## 1. GDP, PMI & IIP: Measuring Economic Momentum

*   **`GDP` (Gross Domestic Product):** Measures overall economic output across quarterly releases—helping frame broad domestic growth momentum, nominal vs. real expansion, and medium-term earnings backdrop.
*   **`PMI` (Purchasing Managers' Index):** Monthly survey-based readings for **Manufacturing** and **Services** where **`50.0` is the neutral dividing line**—readings **above `50`** generally indicate business expansion (new orders, output, hiring), while readings **below `50`** signal contraction. Because `PMI` arrives monthly and ahead of quarterly `GDP`, traders watch it for timely momentum shifts.
*   **`IIP` (Index of Industrial Production):** Tracks monthly physical output across industrial segments (such as manufacturing, mining, and electricity) to show factory-floor activity trends.

---

## 2. Liquidity Trends & Credit Transmission

*   **System Liquidity Matters:** Even when growth is steady, tight banking liquidity can raise short-term money-market rates and slow credit flow, whereas comfortable liquidity supports smoother transmission of policy rates into loans and business activity.
*   **Avoid Over-Reading Any Single Print:** A single softer `IIP` or `PMI` reading can be revised or reversed next month—always weigh the **multi-month trend** against current price structure and earnings expectations.

---

## 3. Key Takeaways

*   **`GDP`** gives the big-picture growth pace, while monthly **`PMI`** (`above/below 50`) and **`IIP`** provide more frequent activity and industrial clues.
*   Track **banking liquidity** alongside activity data and confirm signals with actual **price structure**.
