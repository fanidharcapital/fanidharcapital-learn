## 1. Contango vs. Backwardation

Across expiry dates (or versus spot):

*   **Contango (Upward-Sloping / Carry Structure):** Later-dated futures trade **higher** than near-dated futures or spot (`Far > Near > Spot`), typically reflecting positive net cost of carry (financing cost above dividend yield) under normal conditions.
*   **Backwardation (Downward-Sloping / Inverted Structure):** Near-dated or spot prices trade **higher** than later-dated futures (`Spot / Near > Next / Far`), occurring when dividends/carry are negative or when immediate hedging, shorting, or near-term supply/demand pressure outweighs carry.

---

## 2. Reading Shifts Between Contango & Backwardation

Markets are not static—a stock or index future can shift between contango and backwardation as conditions change:

1.  **Dividend & Corporate Event Windows:** Upcoming ex-dividend dates or special payouts can compress premiums or push futures into backwardation.
2.  **Hedging & Positioning Pressure:** Heavy short hedging, event protection, or rollover imbalances can pull near-month futures below fair value or below next-month contracts.
3.  **Check Liquidity Before Acting:** Next-month and far-month quotes can look distorted when order books are thin—always check depth and bid-ask spreads on later expiries before reading far-month pricing as a clean signal.

---

## 3. Practical Takeaways

*   **Contango** generally reflects normal positive carry across expiries; **backwardation** reflects inverted/negative carry or near-term pressure/dividend effects.
*   Track **changes** in the spread/structure over time rather than treating a single snapshot as a standalone trade trigger.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

*   In **contango**, later expiries trade above earlier expiries/spot; in **backwardation**, the curve slopes downward/inverts.
*   Shifts between contango and backwardation reflect **carry changes, dividends, hedging flows, and positioning**.
*   Always verify **liquidity** across contract months before drawing conclusions from far-month prices.
