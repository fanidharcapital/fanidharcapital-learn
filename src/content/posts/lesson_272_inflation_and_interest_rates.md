In **Lesson #271**, we saw how economic releases move indices, yields, currency, and volatility through surprises. In **Lesson #272**, we examine two foundational macro levers for Indian markets: **Inflation** (`CPI` and `WPI`) and **Interest Rates** (`RBI Repo Rate` and monetary policy signals).

---

## 1. CPI, WPI & Core vs. Volatile Components

*   **`CPI` (Consumer Price Index):** Tracks retail price changes faced by households and serves as the primary inflation anchor for **Reserve Bank of India (`RBI`)** monetary policy decisions.
*   **`WPI` (Wholesale Price Index):** Tracks wholesale-level price movements across manufactured goods, primary articles, and fuel/power—offering a complementary lens on producer cost pressures.
*   **Core vs. Headline / Food & Fuel:** Sharp jumps in volatile food or energy items can swing headline prints temporarily, while **core inflation** (excluding food and energy) helps gauge broader, stickier demand pressures.

---

## 2. RBI Repo Rate, MPC Stance & Rate-Sensitive Sectors

*   **Repo Rate & Policy Stance:** The **repo rate** is the key policy rate at which the `RBI` lends to commercial banks, influencing short-term funding costs and borrowing rates across the economy. Traders pay close attention not only to rate moves (`cut`, `hold`, `hike`), but also to the **Monetary Policy Committee (`MPC`) stance** and liquidity commentary (`system liquidity`, `CRR`, `OMO` operations).
*   **Sector Sensitivity:** Shifts in interest rate and yield expectations hit **banks, NBFCs, real estate, and rate-sensitive auto/consumer cyclicals** directly—changing net interest margins, credit demand, and valuation multiples.

---

## 3. Key Takeaways

*   **`CPI`** anchors `RBI` inflation policy alongside **`WPI`** wholesale cost signals; look past one-off food/fuel spikes to see underlying trend and policy intent.
*   Watch **repo rate decisions, `MPC` stance, and banking liquidity** together when reading rate-sensitive sectors and indices.
