Textbook chart levels—like a flat three-touch resistance line or a clean ascending trendline—are visible to everyone, which makes the area just beyond them a prime spot for **stop-loss clusters and breakout traps**.

## Step-by-Step Trap Anatomy

1. **Crowded Resistance Level:** Price tests the same horizontal ceiling three times over two weeks, encouraging breakout buy-stop orders just above the high and protective stops just under the range floor.
2. **The Trap Push:** Price pierces resistance by a small margin on a sudden burst, drawing in late breakout buyers and covering short sellers.
3. **Snap-Back & Reversal:** Buying dries up immediately, price closes back below the breakout line, and trapped buyers rush to exit—accelerating a move back toward range support.

## Key Execution Lessons

* **Don't Buy the Spike Without Acceptance:** Wait for a sustained hold or clean retest rather than chasing an emotional poke above obvious resistance.
* **Confirm Reversal Structure Before Fading:** Require a clear close back inside structure and a defined invalidation level above the trap high.
