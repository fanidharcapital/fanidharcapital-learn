As an investor in the Indian stock market (**NSE** and **BSE**), you are a part-owner of the companies whose shares you hold. From time to time, the management of these companies makes decisions that directly affect the number of shares you own and the value of your investment.

These events are called **Corporate Actions**. While some are complex, the four most common actions—Dividends, Stock Splits, Bonus Shares, and Rights Issues—are fundamental to understand.

A crucial concept underlying all these actions is that they are **value-neutral** to the company itself; the intrinsic value of the business does not change simply because shares are split or cash is paid out.

---

## 1. Dividends — A Share of Profits

A **Dividend** is a direct payout of a portion of a company's accumulated profits to its shareholders. It is the company's way of sharing its success with its owners.

*   **How It Works:** The board of directors declares a dividend per share (e.g., ₹5 per share). If you own 100 shares, you receive a direct credit of ₹500 to your bank account.
*   **Why It Matters:** For income-focused investors, dividends provide a regular cash return independent of the stock price. For growth investors, high dividends *might* signal that the company has fewer high-return growth opportunities internally.
*   **Key Terms:**
    *   **Interim Dividend:** Paid *before* the financial year results are finalized.
    *   **Final Dividend:** Paid *after* the full-year results are declared (requires shareholder approval at the AGM).
    *   **Dividend Yield:** The annual dividend per share expressed as a percentage of the current stock price ($\frac{\text{Dividend}}{\text{Price}} \times 100$).

---

## 2. Stock Split — Making Shares More Affordable

A **Stock Split** is the division of existing shares into multiple, smaller shares. The primary goal is to increase liquidity by making the stock price more affordable for retail investors.

*   **How It Works:** The company splits the face value of the share. A common split is 1-for-2 (often written as 1:2). If you own 1 share with a face value of ₹10, you now own 2 shares with a face value of ₹5 each.
*   **Value Neutrality:** The **Market Capitalization** (Total Value) remains identical. If one share was trading at ₹1,000, after a 1:2 split, the two new shares will trade at roughly ₹500 each. You own twice as many shares, but each is worth half as much.

---

## 3. Bonus Shares (Scrip Issue) — Free Shares

**Bonus Shares** are additional shares issued by the company to its existing shareholders completely free of cost. These are issued out of the company's reserves or retained earnings.

*   **How It Works:** The company declares a Bonus Issue in a specific ratio (e.g., 1:1). If you own 100 shares, the company gives you 100 *more* shares for free. You now own 200 shares.
*   **Value Neutrality:** Similar to a split, the total value of your holding does not increase on Day 1. Because the number of shares doubled, the stock price is adjusted downward proportionally. If it was ₹2,000, it becomes ₹1,000 after a 1:1 bonus.

*Companies often declare bonus shares to reward long-term shareholders and capitalize their reserves without affecting their cash position.*

---

## 4. Rights Issue — Raising Capital from Existing Owners

A **Rights Issue** is an offer to existing shareholders to purchase *additional* new shares in the company at a discounted price compared to the current market price.

*   **How It Works:** The company offers shares in a ratio (e.g., 1:5) to existing investors. If you own 100 shares, you get the *right* to buy 20 additional shares at a set price (the "Subscription Price").
*   **Why It Matters:** Companies use rights issues to raise capital (often for expansion or debt reduction) without taking on expensive loans.
*   **Action Required:**
    1.  **Exercise the Rights:** Accept the offer and buy the discounted shares.
    2.  **Let Rights Lapse:** Do nothing (the rights expire worthless).
    3.  **Renounce Rights:** Sell your "right" to purchase the shares to another investor in the market (if the exchange permits).

*Unlike Splits and Bonus shares, a Rights Issue requires you to invest *new* capital to maintain your percentage ownership.*

---

## Key Concept: Ex-Date and Record Date

To determine *who* gets the dividend, split, or bonus shares, the company sets specific dates.

1.  **Record Date:** The date by which you must legally own the shares in your Demat account to be eligible for the corporate action.
2.  **Ex-Date (Ex-Dividend Date):** This is the critical trading date. Due to India's T+1 settlement cycle, you must buy the stock **at least one trading day BEFORE the Ex-Date** to have the shares settle by the Record Date.
    *   *If you buy on or after the Ex-Date, you are NOT eligible for the benefit.*
    *   On the Ex-Date, the exchange automatically adjusts the stock price downward by the value of the dividend, or proportionally for a split or bonus.

---

## Key Takeaways

*   **Corporate Actions are value-neutral:** Splits, bonus shares, and dividends do not change the total value of your investment on the day they are adjusted; they simply alter the structure of that value.
*   **Ex-Date drives price adjustments:** Understand that the stock price will drop on the Ex-Date; this is not a loss, but a reflection of the corporate action payout.
*   **Bonus and Splits increase liquidity:** By creating more shares at a lower price, companies encourage trading activity.
*   **Rights issues require cash:** Be prepared to evaluate if you want to invest more capital during a Rights Issue or sell your rights in the market.
