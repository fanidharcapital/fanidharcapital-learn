## 1. What Drives FII vs. DII Flows

*   **`FIIs` (Foreign Institutional Investors / `FPIs`):** Global pension funds, sovereign funds, emerging-market funds, and hedge funds allocating capital across countries. Their flows respond heavily to **US Treasury yields, dollar strength (`USD/INR`), global risk-on/risk-off appetite, valuation relative to other emerging markets, and global index weights**.
*   **`DIIs` (Domestic Institutional Investors):** Indian mutual funds, insurance companies, pension funds, and domestic banks/institutions deploying **recurring domestic savings and monthly `SIP` inflows**. `DIIs` often provide steady structural demand and absorb volatility when foreign funds rotate out.

---

## 2. Reading Multi-Day Rolling Flows Instead of One Daily Number

*   **Why Single-Day Prints Mislead:** Evening `FII`/`DII` provisional cash figures reflect combined exchange activity—including one-off block deals, primary market/QIP allocations, or basket adjustments—so a single red or green day rarely tells the whole story.
*   **Track `5`-Day & `20`-Day Rolling Trends:** Watch **multi-day cumulative net flows** alongside index trend structure. When **both `FIIs` and `DIIs` buy together**, breakouts tend to have stronger follow-through; when one buys while the other sells, indices often consolidate or rotate across sectors.

---

## 3. Key Takeaways

*   **`FIIs`** reflect global liquidity and emerging-market risk appetite, while **`DIIs`** channel steady domestic savings and absorb supply.
*   Focus on **multi-day rolling net flow trends** paired with actual **price structure**, never one isolated evening print.
