Welcome to **Lesson 56** of **Module 02: Exchanges & Operations** (**Lessons 31–60**) in the **Shree Fanidhar Capital Education Series**.

Across **Module 02**, you have seen how orders match, how **T+1 settlement** transfers Demat shares, and how **SEBI surveillance** monitors market fairness. But what happens when something goes wrong in your own account—such as an unauthorized trade, delayed payout, erroneous margin penalty, missing dividend/corporate action credit, or a broker default?

Rather than leaving retail investors stuck in endless customer-support loops, **SEBI, NSE, BSE, NSDL, and CDSL** operate a fast, digital, time-bound **three-tier investor protection and dispute resolution ecosystem**: **Entity Escalation $\rightarrow$ SCORES 2.0 $\rightarrow$ SMART ODR**, backed by the **Exchange Investor Protection Fund (IPF)**.

---

## 1. The Mandatory 4-Step Escalation Ladder

Under SEBI's grievance redressal framework, an investor follows a clear, sequential escalation path so complaints are resolved quickly at the lowest possible cost:

| Stage | Forum / Platform | Mandatory Timeline & Operational Rules | Cost to Investor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Step 1: Direct Entity Complaint** | Broker / DP / Listed Company / RTA Grievance Officer | You must **first lodge a written complaint directly with the concerned SEBI-registered entity** (using their official Grievance Redressal ID / Investor Charter escalation matrix). | **₹0 (Free)** |
| **Step 2: SEBI SCORES 2.0** (`scores.sebi.gov.in`) | SEBI Complaint Redressal System (automated routing + 2-level review) | If the entity rejects your complaint, fails to reply, or gives an unsatisfactory reply, lodge a complaint on **SCORES 2.0 within 1 year** of the cause of action. The entity must file an **Action Taken Report (ATR) within 21 calendar days**. | **₹0 (Free)** |
| **Step 3: Two-Level Review on SCORES** | **1st Review:** Designated Body (`NSE` / `BSE` / `CDSL` / `NSDL` / `AMFI`)<br>**2nd Review:** **SEBI** | If unsatisfied with the entity's ATR, you have **15 calendar days** to request **1st-Level Review** by the Designated Body (auto-triggered if the entity misses the 21-day ATR deadline). If still unresolved, request **2nd-Level Review by SEBI within 15 calendar days**. | **₹0 (Free)** |
| **Step 4: SMART ODR** (`smartodr.in`) | Securities Market Approach for Resolution through Online Dispute Resolution | For monetary claims, binding awards, or disputes unresolved after SCORES, initiate **online Conciliation** followed (if needed) by **online Arbitration** through exchange/depository-empaneled ODR institutions. | **₹0 for Conciliation** (paid by intermediary); slab-based fee for **Arbitration** (refunded if you win) |

## 2. Deep Dive: How SCORES 2.0 Enforces Accountability

SEBI upgraded its complaint portal to **SCORES 2.0** to eliminate delays and automate regulatory oversight:

1. **Instant KYC-Linked Registration:** Investors register using **PAN and Date of Birth**, pulling verified KYC details directly from the **KRA** database.
2. **Strict 21-Calendar-Day Clock:** Once a complaint is lodged, the intermediary or listed company has **21 calendar days** to investigate and upload its **Action Taken Report (ATR)**.
3. **Automatic Escalation to Market Infrastructure Institutions (MIIs):** If an intermediary fails to upload an ATR within 21 days, SCORES **automatically routes** the complaint to the relevant **Designated Body** (such as **NSE, BSE, NSDL, CDSL, AMFI, or APMI**) for **First-Level Review** without requiring the investor to chase anyone.
4. **Public Monthly Grievance Disclosure:** SEBI requires registered intermediaries (brokers, RAs, IAs, PMS) to publish their monthly complaint receipts, disposals, and pending counts on their websites by the **7th of every month**—making service quality publicly auditable.

## 3. SMART ODR: Fast-Track Digital Conciliation & Arbitration

When a dispute involves **financial compensation** (for instance, recovering losses from an unauthorized order or wrongful square-off) and remains unresolved on SCORES, **SMART ODR** provides legally binding resolution without visiting a physical court:

* **Phase 1 — Online Conciliation (Zero Cost to Investor):** An independent, neutral conciliator conducts virtual sessions to help both parties reach a mutually agreed settlement. The **market participant bears 100% of the conciliation fee**—the investor pays **₹0**.
* **Phase 2 — Online Arbitration (Binding Award):** If conciliation does not produce a settlement, either party can escalate to **binding online Arbitration** within one month.
  * Both sides deposit standardized, slab-based arbitration fees (for example, starting from **₹5,400 + taxes** per side for claims up to ₹1 lakh, scaling by claim slab).
  * **Winner Recovers Fees:** Upon passing of the arbitral award, the **successful party receives a full refund of its deposited arbitration fees**, shifting the ultimate cost onto the losing party.
  * **15-Day Payment Enforcement:** If the award goes against a broker or intermediary, they must pay the awarded amount within **15 calendar days**, monitored directly by the stock exchange or depository.

*(Note: Once you initiate proceedings on **SMART ODR**—or file before a court or consumer forum—any parallel complaint on SCORES for the same dispute is automatically closed to prevent duplicate proceedings.)*

## 4. What If a Stock Broker Defaults or Is Expelled? (IPF Protection)

In **Lesson 43** and **Lesson 45**, you learned how your Demat shares stay ring-fenced in **NSDL/CDSL** and how idle client cash is upstreamed daily to Clearing Corporations. As an additional safety net, if a trading member ever defaults or is expelled by an exchange, eligible clients can file claims against the exchange's **Investor Protection Fund (IPF)**:

| Exchange / Segment | Maximum IPF Compensation Ceiling per Investor | Key Eligibility Condition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **NSE (National Stock Exchange)** | Up to **₹35 Lakh** per investor per claim | Must be a valid, bonafide exchange-traded / ledger-verified claim against a declared defaulter or expelled member (excluding off-market loans or assured-return schemes). |
| **BSE (Bombay Stock Exchange)** | Up to **₹16 Lakh** per client of defaulter | Verified eligible claim arising from trades/balances on BSE against a declared defaulter member. |

> **What IPF Does *Not* Cover:** IPF strictly protects **bonafide securities market transactions** executed through registered brokers. It **never** covers unregulated "assured-return" side agreements, personal loans lent to a sub-broker/Authorized Person, or losses arising from normal market price movements.

## 5. Investor Best Practices to Protect Your Rights

1. **Always Preserve Digital Audit Trails:** Download and archive your **Contract Notes, Monthly Demat CAS, Margin Statements, and Recorded Order Confirmation Logs**—these are primary evidence in SCORES and SMART ODR.
2. **Never Share Login Credentials or TPINs:** Sharing your broker password, TPIN, or OTP with a relationship manager or third party weakens your claim in an unauthorized-trading dispute.
3. **Act Within Statutory Timelines:** Always escalate to your broker immediately upon spotting a discrepancy, lodge on **SCORES within 1 year**, and request **1st/2nd reviews within 15 calendar days** of an ATR.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **Always escalate in sequence:** First complain directly to the **entity**, then lodge free on **SEBI SCORES 2.0** (where entities must respond within **21 calendar days**), and use **SMART ODR** for free online conciliation or binding arbitration.
* **SCORES 2.0 automates oversight** by routing unanswered complaints directly to **NSE, BSE, NSDL, or CDSL** for review and requiring public monthly grievance disclosures.
* **Exchange Investor Protection Funds (IPF)** provide last-resort default compensation (up to **₹35 Lakh on NSE** and **₹16 Lakh on BSE** per eligible investor) strictly for legitimate, exchange-recorded transactions.
