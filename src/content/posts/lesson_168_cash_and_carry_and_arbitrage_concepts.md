## 1. Cash-and-Carry Arbitrage (Intuition)

When a futures contract trades **significantly above** its theoretical fair value (an unusually rich premium relative to financing and dividend carry):

*   Institutional/arbitrage participants can theoretically **buy the underlying in the cash/spot market** and **sell the overpriced futures contract**, locking in the spread until expiry/convergence (subject to capital, margin, and execution friction).
*   Selling futures and buying cash adds supply pressure on futures relative to spot, helping pull an over-rich futures price back toward fair value.

---

## 2. Reverse Arbitrage & Why a "No-Arbitrage Band" Exists

When futures trade **deeply below** fair value (an unusually steep discount):

*   The opposite trade—**selling/unwinding cash exposure and buying the discounted futures** (or reverse cash-and-carry where permissible)—tends to support futures relative to spot.
*   **Why Prices Aren't Locked to the Exact Paisa:** Real-world arbitrage is not free. **Brokerage, STT, exchange fees, stamp duty, GST, margin capital costs, and execution slippage** create a **no-arbitrage band (transaction-cost corridor)** around fair value. Small deviations within that band are not worth the friction for arbitrageurs to trade away.

---

## 3. What This Means for Retail Traders

*   Arbitrage keeps futures tethered to spot over time and guarantees convergence at expiry—it does **not** give retail traders a "free money" button, because institutional desks operate with specialized execution, lower friction, and tight risk/capital limits.
*   Understanding fair value and the no-arbitrage band simply helps you recognize when a premium or discount is **normal carry** versus **stretched/event-driven**.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

*   Arbitrage activity helps keep **futures priced near spot + carry** and drives **convergence at expiry**.
*   **Transaction costs, taxes, margin funding, and slippage** create a **no-arbitrage band** where minor mispricings persist.
*   Treat arbitrage concepts as a **pricing lens**, not a shortcut to risk-free retail profits.
