## 1. Basis Swings Around Earnings & Major Events

Around scheduled **earnings announcements**, corporate actions, or major macro/policy events:

*   **Surge in Premium or Discount:** Traders positioning for a big move or hedging existing cash/option exposure can push futures noticeably higher or lower relative to spot as implied/event risk builds.
*   **Post-Event Normalization:** Once uncertainty clears after an announcement or event, extreme basis stretches often ease back toward normal carry/convergence paths—sometimes even while price continues trending or consolidating.

---

## 2. Expiry-Week Swings & Rollover Effects

As covered in **Lessons #156 and #164**, **expiry week** brings distinct basis behavior:

*   Near-month basis must converge to zero at expiry, while rollover orders shift liquidity and spread dynamics between near and next month.
*   A sudden jump or drop in near-month basis during expiry week can reflect **rollover execution, squaring off, or delivery-margin adjustments** rather than a brand-new directional trend.

---

## 3. Putting Basis, Price & OI Together

Never trade a basis swing in isolation:

1.  **Structure First:** Let support, resistance, trend, and invalidation levels (from Modules 04–05) define your trade idea.
2.  **Layer OI & Basis as Context:** Use **Open Interest** (Lesson #157) to see whether fresh positions or unwinding/covering are driving the move, and use **basis/carry** (Lessons #164–#169) to spot event/rollover distortions or crowded sentiment.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

*   **Earnings, major events, and expiry week** frequently cause sharp swings in futures premium or discount.
*   Basis swings can reflect **hedging, event positioning, or rollover mechanics**—not just pure bullish/bearish conviction.
*   Use basis as **supporting context** alongside **price structure** and **Open Interest**, never as a standalone signal.
