In **Lesson #103**, you introduce the **Relative Strength Index (RSI)**—a momentum oscillator bounded between `0` and `100` that measures the speed and magnitude of recent price gains relative to recent price losses (most commonly over a **14-period** lookback).

## 1. Rethinking Overbought (>70) and Oversold (<30)

Retail traders frequently make a classic mistake with RSI: shorting immediately whenever RSI crosses above `70`, or buying blindly whenever RSI drops below `30`. Momentum behaves differently depending on whether a stock is **ranging** or **trending**:

| Market Context | RSI > 70 Behavior | RSI < 30 Behavior | Practical Takeaway |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sideways / Range-Bound** | Often marks the upper end of a swing; price frequently reverts toward range mid/support | Often marks the lower end of a swing; price frequently bounces toward range resistance | Classic `70/30` mean-reversion levels work best when price is oscillating inside a horizontal box |
| **Strong Bullish Uptrend** | RSI can stay elevated above `70` ("overbought" can stay overbought for weeks) as price trends strongly | Pullbacks often find support around **40–50** rather than dropping all the way to `30` | **Do not short a strong uptrend just because RSI > 70**; buy pullbacks when RSI holds above `40–50` |
| **Strong Bearish Downtrend** | Relief bounces often stall around **40–60** before rolling over again | RSI can stay depressed below `30` during sharp sell-offs | **Do not catch a falling knife solely because RSI < 30**; wait for structure and momentum confirmation |

---

## 2. RSI Momentum Regimes & Trend Pullbacks

Rather than treating `50` as a rigid buy/sell trigger, read it as the **momentum centerline**:

* **Bullish Momentum Regime (`40–80` Band):** In a healthy uptrend on NSE/BSE, RSI typically surges above `60–70` on impulse legs and cools off into the **40–50** zone during orderly pullbacks. When RSI bounces off `40–50` alongside a bullish candle at a moving average or support zone, momentum is refreshing rather than reversing.
* **Bearish Momentum Regime (`20–60` Band):** In a downtrend, RSI frequently drops below `30–40` on sell legs and fails to reclaim `50–60` on counter-trend bounces.

## 3. RSI Divergences: Early Warning, Not Standalone Trigger

A **divergence** occurs when price makes a new swing high or low that is **not** confirmed by a corresponding swing high or low on the RSI:

1. **Regular Bearish Divergence (Momentum Losing Steam at Highs):**
   * **Price:** Prints a **Higher High (HH)**.
   * **RSI:** Prints a **Lower High (LH)**—often from a lower peak than the prior rally.
   * **Meaning:** Upward thrust is weakening even as price pushes slightly higher. Treat it as a **caution/profit-booking warning** near resistance—not an automatic short while price structure remains intact.
2. **Regular Bullish Divergence (Selling Pressure Fading at Lows):**
   * **Price:** Prints a **Lower Low (LL)**.
   * **RSI:** Prints a **Higher Low (HL)**.
   * **Meaning:** Downward momentum is decelerating. Look for price structure confirmation (such as a break of a local swing high or support reclaim) before entering long.

### Crucial Rule on Divergences
Divergences can persist for multiple swings in strong trends (a stock can print two or three RSI divergences while still climbing). Always require **price structure confirmation**—never short a runaway leader solely because RSI shows bearish divergence.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

* **RSI (`0–100`)** measures internal price momentum speed—not whether a stock is "cheap" or "expensive" fundamentally.
* In **strong uptrends**, RSI `> 70` reflects trend power, while **40–50** often acts as a momentum pullback floor.
* **Divergences** warn of fading momentum early, but **price action and key levels** always govern final execution.
