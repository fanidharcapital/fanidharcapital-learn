Access to daily FII/DII cash numbers, participant-wise OI tables, and real-time macro feeds is an advantage only if you avoid misusing the data. Most losses around macro and flow analysis come from a handful of repeatable mistakes.

## Five High-Cost Macro & Flow Traps

1. **Fighting Strong Institutional Trends Because Price Looks "Too High":** Shorting an index or sector solely because valuations look stretched or FIIs sold for two days ignores sustained domestic SIP inflows and strong price structure. Never fade a trend without a confirmed breakdown on the chart.
2. **Treating Daily FII/DII Numbers as a Standalone Signal:** End-of-day cash figures reflect *past* execution, not guaranteed tomorrow morning direction. Use multi-day rolling flows alongside price action rather than reacting blindly to one day's print.
3. **Over-Leveraging Binary Events:** Sizing up ahead of Union Budget, RBI policy, or earnings hoping for a windfall turns disciplined trading into a coin flip. Binary events have non-linear gap risk that can jump right over stop-loss orders.
4. **Ignoring Hedged Institutional Positioning:** Large FII index futures shorts are often hedges against large cash equity portfolios—not an outright bet on a market crash.
5. **Changing Strategy Mid-Trade After a Headline:** Entering a swing trade based on daily chart structure and then panic-exiting—or doubling down—because of an intraday news flash destroys statistical expectancy.

## Corrective Rules to Enforce

* Require **price confirmation** before acting on any flow or macro thesis.
* Cap risk per event trade strictly below your normal swing-trade risk unit.
* Separate **portfolio hedges** from **directional signals** when reading participant-wise derivatives data.
