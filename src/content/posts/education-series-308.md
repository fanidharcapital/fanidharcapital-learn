Markets alternate between **contraction** (tight ranges, shrinking candles, low volatility) and **expansion** (directional trend legs and wider ranges). Spotting **volatility coils** early lets you prepare clean levels and risk parameters ahead of the expansion—without jumping the gun inside the chop.

## Signs of a Volatility Coil

* **Contracting Candle Ranges & Lower ATR:** Daily or intraday Average True Range drifts lower as buyers and sellers reach temporary equilibrium.
* **Converging Highs and Lows:** Lower highs meet higher lows in a tightening coil or narrow sideways box, squeezing intraday noise into a smaller band.
* **Suppressed Implied Volatility:** Option premiums often cool or coil ahead of a catalyst or structural resolution.

## Preparing for Expansion Without Predicting Direction

1. **Mark the Coil Boundaries:** Define the upper resistance ceiling and lower support floor of the compression zone—plus your structural invalidation if price breaks out and fails.
2. **Wait for Expansion Confirmation:** Coils can break up or down; let price close decisively outside the coil rather than guessing which side wins in advance.
3. **Adjust Targets & Stops to Volatility:** Once expansion starts, widen trailing stops reasonably to match the new range and size positions so a fast move does not violate your risk budget.

## Key Takeaways

* Tight consolidation and contracting volatility often precede **larger directional expansion**, but direction itself is only confirmed once price exits the coil.
* Avoid overtrading small noise inside a narrow squeeze; save your risk for the clean structural break or confirmed rejection.
