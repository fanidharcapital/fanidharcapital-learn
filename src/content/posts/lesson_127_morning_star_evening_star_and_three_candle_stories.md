## 1. The Three-Act Auction Story

Trends rarely turn on a dime without a transition phase. Three-candle star patterns organize that transition into three clear acts:

| Sequence | Act 1: Exhaustion Push | Act 2: Equilibrium Star | Act 3: Confirmation Drive |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Morning Star** (Bullish Reversal at Support) | Wide red candle driving price into a support/demand zone as late sellers capitulate. | Small-bodied candle (Spinning Top, Doji, or Hammer) near the lows showing selling pressure has stalled. | Strong green candle closing **at least 50% into Candle 1's red real body**, proving buyers have seized control. |
| **Evening Star** (Bearish Reversal at Resistance) | Wide green candle pushing price into a resistance/supply zone as late buyers chase highs. | Small-bodied candle (Spinning Top, Doji, or Shooting Star) near the highs showing buying momentum has dried up. | Strong red candle closing **at least 50% into Candle 1's green real body**, proving sellers have taken control. |

*   **Daily vs. Intraday Gaps:** In classical textbook diagrams, Candle 2 gaps away from Candle 1 and Candle 3. On continuous intraday NSE timeframes (15-min, 1-hour) and liquid Daily charts, gaps are not mandatory—what matters is the **contraction in Candle 2's body** followed by **decisive penetration by Candle 3**.

## 2. Related Three-Candle Exhaustion & Continuation Stories

Beyond the Morning and Evening Star, three-candle rhythm appears in two other high-signal price-action structures:

*   **Three White Soldiers / Three Black Crows:** Three consecutive wide-range candles closing near their extremes with minimal wicks. At the start of a breakout, they signal strong institutional initiation; after a prolonged rally or decline, three oversized candles often mark **climactic exhaustion** right before a pullback.
*   **Three-Bar Play (Continuation Pause):** A wide expansion candle, followed by a tight, quiet second candle holding in the upper/lower third of the first bar, followed by a third candle breaking out in the original trend direction.

## 3. Filtering High-Probability Star Patterns on NSE / BSE

1.  **Require >50% Penetration on Candle 3:** If Candle 3 barely closes above Candle 2 and fails to reclaim at least half of Candle 1's real body, buyers/sellers lack follow-through conviction.
2.  **Anchor to Structural Extremes:** Look for a **Morning Star** at a higher-timeframe swing low, horizontal demand zone, or rising 50-EMA—and an **Evening Star** at a multi-week resistance shelf or supply zone.
3.  **Check Volume Expansion on Candle 3:** Ideal reversals on NSE stocks show quieter volume on the Candle 2 pause and expanding volume/delivery on the Candle 3 confirmation bar.
4.  **Set Clear Invalidation:** For a Morning Star long, place the stop-loss just below the lowest wick of the three-candle cluster (usually Candle 2's low). For an Evening Star short, place the stop-loss just above the highest wick of the cluster.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

*   **Morning Star** and **Evening Star** tell a complete three-step story: directional exhaustion, equilibrium stall, and confirmed reversal.
*   Candle 3 must close **more than halfway into Candle 1's body** to validate the shift in control.
*   Trading three-candle reversals only at pre-identified support and resistance levels prevents getting caught in mid-range noise.
