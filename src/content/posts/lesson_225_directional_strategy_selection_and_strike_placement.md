In **Lessons #219–#224**, you learned the mechanics of debit spreads, credit spreads, and ratio/backspreads. In **Lesson #225**, we wrap up **Part 2** with a practical decision framework for **directional strategy selection and strike placement**—aligning strikes and spread width with **market structure, `DTE`, and `1R` risk**.

![Educational Series #225: Directional Strategy Selection & Strike Placement](/images/lessons/lesson-225.png)

---

## 1. Matching Strikes & Spread Width to Market Structure

*   **Start With Chart Structure First:** Identify trend bias, key swing highs/lows, breakout/retest levels, and obvious support/resistance zones before picking strikes.
*   **For Credit Spreads (`Bull Put` / `Bear Call`):** Place the short strike with a **structural buffer**—such as below a valid support shelf or outside a key resistance zone—rather than right in the middle of noisy price action. Keep spread width sized so max loss fits your `1R` risk budget.
*   **For Debit Spreads (`Bull Call` / `Bear Put`):** Anchor strikes around your **expected move/target zone** and invalidation level so the spread width reflects a realistic price objective rather than an unreachable pipe dream.

---

## 2. Factoring in `DTE` and Risk/Reward

*   **`DTE` Shapes Time vs. Move Trade-Off:** Short `DTE` sees faster **Theta** and **Gamma** swings—leaving less time for a slow trend to develop on debit spreads, while accelerating decay if price stays safely outside a credit spread. Longer `DTE` gives more breathing room for a thesis to unfold, at higher upfront cost/time exposure.
*   **Size Every Spread to `1R`:** Whether paying a net debit or risking max loss on a credit spread, check worst-case loss **before** entry so one adverse move never breaches your risk rule.

---

## 3. Key Takeaways

*   Let **market structure** (support, resistance, trend, invalidation) guide strike placement—never pick strikes mechanically without chart context.
*   Match **spread width and `DTE`** to your expected move and **`1R` risk limit**, ready to move into **Part 3: Non-Directional, Volatility & Range-Bound Strategies**.
