Welcome to **Lesson 78** of **Module 03: Fundamental Analysis** (**Lessons 61–90**) in the **Shree Fanidhar Capital Educational Series**.

In **Lessons 74–77**, we mastered general valuation multiples (`P/E`, `P/B`, `EV/EBITDA`, `PEG`), **Discounted Cash Flow (`DCF`)**, **Dividend Discount Models (`DDM`)**, and **Owner Earnings**. However, applying the exact same valuation ratio across every industry leads to serious analytical errors. Today, we learn how seasoned analysts adapt their **valuation multiples and operating KPIs by sector** in Indian equities.

---

## 1. Why One Valuation Yardstick Never Fits Every Sector

Each industry has a distinct balance-sheet structure, capital intensity, and earnings cycle:
* In **Banks and NBFCs**, money is the raw material—so debt (`deposits` and `borrowings`) is not a financing side-issue, and `EBITDA` or `Operating Cash Flow` have no analytical meaning.
* In **IT Services and FMCG**, minimal physical plant is required—so `Price-to-Book (P/B)` looks deceptively high (`8x–20x`) even when a stock is reasonably valued on `P/E` and `FCF Yield`.
* In **Metals, Cement, and Commodities**, earnings swing violently with global prices—so buying at a "cheap" `6x P/E` at the top of a boom is often the worst possible entry point.

| **3. Pharma & Specialty Chemicals** | **`EV / EBITDA`**, **`P/E`**, and **`DCF` / `SOTP`** | R&D spend (`% of sales`), `USFDA` plant compliance (`OAI`/`WL` status), `API` vs. branded formulation mix, and complex molecule pipeline. | Extrapolating one-off windfall generic exclusivity profits (`gRevlimid`-style spikes) into perpetuity. |

## 3. Deep Dive: Valuing Banks & NBFCs (`P/ABV` & `RoA`)

For lenders, **Book Value** represents the equity capital buffer that absorbs loan losses and supports credit growth:

$$\text{Adjusted Book Value (ABV)} = \text{Shareholders’ Equity} - \text{Net Non-Performing Assets (NNPA)}$$

* **Why `RoA` (Return on Assets) Matters Alongside `ROE`:** A bank can temporarily boost `ROE` by running higher leverage, but **`RoA` (`PAT / Average Total Assets`)** measures pure lending profitability and underwriting discipline. Top Indian private banks typically deliver **`RoA` of `1.6%–2.0%+`**, justifying `P/B` multiples of `2.2x–3.5x`, whereas lenders with `RoA < 0.8%` and rising `NNPA` often trade below `1.0x P/B`.

---

## 4. Deep Dive: Valuing Cyclicals With Replacement Cost (`EV / Tonne`)

In homogeneous commodity industries like **Cement or Steel**, analysts compare a company's **`EV / Tonne`** (Enterprise Value divided by installed annual production capacity in million tonnes) against the **Replacement Cost** of building a brand-new greenfield plant from scratch:

* **Below Replacement Cost:** If an efficient cement producer trades at `$75/tonne` when building a new plant costs `$110–$120/tonne`, down-cycle pessimism may be offering a strong margin of safety.
* **Far Above Replacement Cost:** Trading at `$220/tonne` at peak margins signals that high profitability will invite new capacity additions and future price competition.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **Match your valuation multiple to the sector's economics:** Use **`P/B` and `P/ABV`** for lenders, **`P/E` and `FCF Yield`** for asset-light compounders, **`EV/EBITDA`** for pharma/chemicals, and **Mid-Cycle `EV/EBITDA` or `EV/Tonne`** for cyclicals.
* **Never trust headline `P/E` on commodity cyclicals**—low `P/E` at peak spreads often marks cyclical tops rather than bargains.
* **Always combine valuation multiples with sector-specific operating KPIs** (`NIM` & `NNPA` in banking, volume growth in FMCG, `USFDA` status in pharma, and capacity utilization in industrials).
