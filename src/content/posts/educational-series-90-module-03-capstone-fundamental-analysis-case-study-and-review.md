## 1. End-to-End Case Study: Narmada Precision Valves Ltd. (`Hypothetical`)

Suppose you are evaluating **Narmada Precision Valves Ltd.** (`NSE/BSE: NARMADAVLV`), a manufacturer of industrial flow-control valves used in water infrastructure, process industries, and power/energy projects.

### A. Business Model, Moat & Cycle Context
* **What It Does:** Supplies engineered valves and replacement spares across domestic and export industrial/utility clients. Replacement spare parts provide recurring revenue once valves are installed in critical pipelines or plants.
* **Competitive & Cycle Check:** Customer switching costs exist on mission-critical installations where valve failure causes downtime, though competitive bidding in large project tenders keeps pricing disciplined. Order inflows show moderate cyclicality tied to industrial and infrastructure capex.

### B. Multi-Year Financial & Ratio Snapshot (`₹ Crore`, Illustrative)

| Metric | `FY24` | `FY25` | `FY26` | Analytical Readout |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Revenue from Operations** | `₹600 Cr` | `₹690 Cr` | `₹780 Cr` | Steady `~14–15%` annual top-line growth |
| **Operating Profit (`EBITDA`) & `OPM`** | `₹108 Cr` (`18.0%`) | `₹127.7 Cr` (`18.5%`) | `₹148.2 Cr` (`19.0%`) | Gradual `+50 bps/yr` operating margin expansion |
| **Profit After Tax (`PAT`) & `NPM`** | `₹66 Cr` (`11.0%`) | `₹80.0 Cr` (`11.6%`) | `₹95.2 Cr` (`12.2%`) | Healthy profit growth backed by clean tax & interest lines |
| **Cash Flow from Operations (`CFO`)** | `₹62 Cr` | `₹76 Cr` | `₹91 Cr` | `CFO / PAT` at `~0.96x` in `FY26`—solid cash conversion |
| **Capital Expenditure (`Capex`)** | `₹24 Cr` | `₹28 Cr` | `₹30 Cr` | Moderate `Capex` (`~3.8%` of sales) |
| **Free Cash Flow (`FCF = CFO − Capex`)** | `₹38 Cr` | `₹48 Cr` | `₹61 Cr` | Positive and growing `FCF` (`~64%` of `PAT` in `FY26`) |
| **Return on Equity (`ROE`) / `ROCE`** | `17.5%` / `20.2%` | `18.8%` / `21.6%` | `19.6%` / `22.8%` | Strong capital efficiency well above typical cost of capital |
| **Net Debt / Equity** | `0.12x` | `0.06x` | `Net Cash` (`−0.04x`) | Conservative balance sheet; low leverage risk |

### C. Forensics, Governance & Valuation Check
* **Forensics & Governance:** Receivable days held steady around `54 days` (no unexplained spike), inventory turnover stayed healthy, promoter holding is `62.5%` with `0%` pledged shares, and auditor reports carry no qualifications or unusual related-party advances.
* **Valuation & Margin of Safety:** At a market cap of **`₹2,665 Cr`** (`FY26` `P/E` ≈ **`28.0x`**, `EV/EBITDA` ≈ **`18.0x`**), the stock prices in continued mid-teens growth and high returns. Under a conservative `DCF` / valuation check, `28x` trailing earnings leaves a **thin margin of safety** if industrial order growth slows or margins plateau—making it a **quality business to track on a watchlist or scale into only at better valuation support** rather than chasing blindly.

## 3. Module 03 Complete Recap (`Lessons 61–90`)

1. **Business & Financial Statement Foundations (`61–73`):** Understanding business economics, annual reports, `P&L`, revenue recognition, balance sheet, working capital, `Capex`/depreciation, debt, cash flows, `FCF`, consolidated vs. standalone statements, and footnotes/contingent liabilities.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **Fundamental analysis is a system, not a single formula:** Combine business understanding, financial statement & cash-flow quality, ratio trends, governance/forensic checks, and valuation discipline.
* **Great businesses and fair prices are two separate questions:** A company can pass every quality and governance test yet still be expensive if the current price leaves no margin of safety.
* **Keep a repeatable process** so every investment decision is grounded in evidence, risk awareness, and clear reasoning.
