In **Lesson #279**, we compared `FII` and `DII` flows. In **Lesson #280**, we look across **market segments**—comparing **cash market delivery** against **index/stock futures and options (`F&O`) positioning** so you don't misread institutional hedging as a pure directional bet.

## 1. Three Institutional Footprints Across Segments

| Market Segment | Primary Institutional Purpose | How to Read It Cleanly |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cash Market (Delivery)** | Outright capital deployment into or out of specific companies and sectors. | Sustained cash buying with strong **delivery volume** signals genuine medium-to-long-term accumulation. |
| **Index & Stock Futures** | Tactical directional exposure, index arbitrage (`cash-futures`), or temporary beta hedging. | Watch changes in **net long/short futures positioning and Open Interest (`OI`)** alongside spot price direction. |
| **Index & Stock Options** | **Portfolio downside hedging (buying Puts)**, covered writing (selling Calls), or volatility strategies. | Large `Put` buying or `Call` writing by institutions frequently **protects an existing long cash portfolio** rather than betting on a market crash. |

---

## 2. Avoiding the "Derivatives-Only" Misread

Traders who look only at daily `FII` index option numbers without checking **cash market trends and price structure** often assume institutions are aggressively bearish simply because they bought index Puts to hedge billions of rupees in cash equities. Always read **cash + futures + price action together**.

---

## 3. Key Takeaways

*   **Cash delivery** shows true equity ownership shifts, while **futures and options** serve both directional and **portfolio-hedging** roles.
*   Never mistake routine institutional **option hedging** for a standalone bearish bet without cash and chart confirmation.
