In **Lesson #244**, we explored how loss aversion and the disposition effect distort exits. In **Lesson #245**, we focus on two perception biases that cloud chart reading: **Confirmation Bias** and **Anchoring**.

![Educational Series #245: Confirmation Bias & Anchoring](/images/lessons/lesson-245.png)

---

## 1. Confirmation Bias: Cherry-Picking Only What You Want to See

*   Once you form a bias—or enter a position—**confirmation bias** leads you to highlight every indicator, candle, or news snippet that supports your view while ignoring **opposing price structure, resistance/support breaks, volume clues, or invalidation signals**.
*   *Counter-Check:* Actively look for the **strongest reason your thesis could be wrong** right now. If structure breaks against you, respect the break rather than arguing with the chart.

---

## 2. Anchoring: Obsessing Over Your Entry Price or Past Levels

*   **Entry-Price Anchoring:** Treating your fill price (or a prior high/low) as a sacred reference point—refusing to exit a broken trade until price "gets back to my entry," or hesitating to take a valid setup because price "feels higher/lower than earlier."
*   **The "Flat-Screen" Question:** The market does not know or care where you entered. Ask yourself neutrally: *"If I were completely flat right now looking at current price action and structure, would I enter this trade here?"*

---

## 3. Key Takeaways

*   **Confirmation bias** makes you filter out inconvenient market evidence; **anchoring** ties your decisions to entry prices or stale reference levels.
*   Anchor your decisions to **current market structure and invalidation levels**, not your fill price or desired bias.
