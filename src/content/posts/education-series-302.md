Financial markets operate as continuous two-way auctions searching for fair price between buyers and sellers. **Auction Market Theory (AMT)** classifies price action into **balance** (acceptance within a value range) and **imbalance** (directional repricing away from fair value).

## Key Auction Market Reference Levels

* **Point of Control (POC):** The price level with the highest traded volume (or time) within a session or profile—representing the market's strongest consensus of fair value.
* **Value Area High (VAH) & Value Area Low (VAL):** The upper and lower bounds of the range where roughly **70%** of recent traded volume occurred.
* **Balanced Auction:** Price rotates back and forth between VAL and VAH around the POC; breakout attempts fail and revert toward the mean.
* **Imbalanced Auction:** Strong one-sided aggression drives price outside the Value Area with acceptance, leaving low-volume nodes behind as price discovers a new equilibrium.

## Balance vs. Imbalance Playbook

1. **In Balance (Rotation Inside VAH–VAL):** Favor mean-reversion setups near VAL and VAH with tight stops outside the range rather than chasing mid-range entries.
2. **Transition to Imbalance (Acceptance Outside VAH or VAL):** Look for a clean break and hold outside the Value Area accompanied by expanding range and volume, then trade pullbacks toward the broken boundary.
3. **Return to Value:** If price pierces VAH or VAL and quickly falls back inside, the auction rejected higher/lower prices—often setting up a rotation back toward the POC.

## Key Takeaways

* Never place a breakout trade right in the middle of a high-volume **POC** zone where two-way trading is heaviest.
* Use **VAH and VAL** as objective boundaries to separate range rotation from directional trend discovery.
