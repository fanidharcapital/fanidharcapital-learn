In **Lesson #129**, we established how to spot valid **Swing Highs** and **Swing Lows** without getting distracted by single-candle noise. Now, in **Lesson #130**, we connect those pivots into the three primary states of market structure: **Uptrends**, **Downtrends**, and **Sideways Ranges**. Knowing which regime an NSE/BSE stock or index is currently in sets your directional bias and prevents you from forcing trend setups into range-bound tape.

## 1. The Three Structural Market Regimes

Once you have marked clean swing pivots on your chosen timeframe, classify the structure by comparing each new swing high and swing low against the previous one:

| Market Regime | Swing Sequence | What It Tells You About Supply & Demand |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Uptrend** | **Higher Highs (HH)** and **Higher Lows (HL)** | Buyers are willing to bid higher prices on each advance and step in earlier on each pullback. Demand consistently absorbs available supply. |
| **Downtrend** | **Lower Highs (LH)** and **Lower Lows (LL)** | Sellers are willing to accept lower prices on each breakdown and defend lower levels on each bounce. Supply consistently overwhelms demand. |
| **Sideways Range** | Overlapping highs and lows bounded between a horizontal ceiling and floor | Neither side holds sustained control. Buying near the floor meets selling near the ceiling, creating balance or rotation. |

## 2. Practical Nuances on Real NSE / BSE Charts

Clean textbook zig-zags rarely appear every single day. In live Indian equity charts, watch for three real-world details:

### A. It Takes at Least Two Swings to Define a Trend
A single bounce off a low does not make an uptrend. You need at least a **Swing Low $\rightarrow$ Swing High $\rightarrow$ Higher Low $\rightarrow$ Higher High** sequence before treating a move as an established uptrend.

### B. Trend Continuation vs. Early Stall
*   In a healthy **uptrend**, pullbacks form **Higher Lows** and do not slice cleanly below the prior confirmed Swing Low.
*   When an uptrend stops making **Higher Highs** and instead prints a **Lower High**, momentum is slowing—even before the structure officially reverses (which we cover in detail in Lesson #131).

### C. Mid-Range Traps in Sideways Markets
When Nifty or a stock is rotating inside a horizontal box (say `₹24,200` to `₹24,700` on an index or `₹780` to `₹840` on a stock), the middle of the range is a low-edge zone. Many false breakouts and choppy stop-outs happen right around the midpoint where structure has no clear directional anchor.

## 3. Adapting Your Bias by Regime

Your execution mindset should shift with the structure you are looking at:

1.  **In an Uptrend (HH + HL):** Favor long setups on pullbacks into support, demand zones, or broken resistance (polarity flips) and breakout continuations. Avoid habitually shorting strong uptrends just because price "looks high."
2.  **In a Downtrend (LH + LL):** Favor short/bearish setups on rallies into resistance or supply zones, and avoid trying to "catch every falling knife" without structural confirmation.
3.  **In a Sideways Range:** Either fade extremes (buying near proven support, selling near proven resistance) with tight targets, or wait patiently for a confirmed breakout and retest out of the range.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

*   **Uptrends** are defined by **Higher Highs and Higher Lows**; **downtrends** are defined by **Lower Highs and Lower Lows**.
*   **Sideways ranges** occur when price rotates between horizontal boundaries with overlapping swings and no sustained directional progression.
*   Align your directional bias with the active swing structure on NSE/BSE charts instead of fighting dominant order flow.
