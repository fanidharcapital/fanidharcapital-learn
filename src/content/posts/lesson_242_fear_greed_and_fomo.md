In **Lesson #241**, we established probabilistic thinking over outcome bias. In **Lesson #242**, we examine the three classic emotional triggers that hijack judgment during live NSE/BSE sessions: **Fear**, **Greed**, and **FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out)**.

![Educational Series #242: Fear, Greed & FOMO](/images/lessons/lesson-242.png)

---

## 1. How Fear, Greed & FOMO Show Up on the Screen

*   **Fear (Hesitation, Freezing & Early Cutting):** Often surfaces after a loss or drawdown. You hesitate on valid A+ setups, pull stop-losses wider out of dread of admitting being wrong, or yank winners out for tiny scraps at the first minor pullback.
*   **Greed (Chasing Extended Moves & Size Creep):** Often surfaces after green trades or strong momentum. You skip profit targets hoping a rally or drop never ends, add unplanned contracts mid-move, or inflate lot size past your risk rule.
*   **FOMO (Late Entries Without Structure):** Triggered when a fast candle or index spike is already underway and you feel left behind—causing you to buy tops or sell bottoms late with poor risk-reward and no clear invalidation level.

---

## 2. Catching Emotional Triggers Before Execution

*   **Watch Physical & Behavioral Clues:** Tight chest, hurried mouse clicks, switching timeframes rapidly to justify an entry, or feeling urgency to "catch this candle right now" are classic early warnings.
*   **Name the Emotion First:** Pause for `10–15` seconds and ask: *"Am I acting on a pre-defined setup and risk plan, or am I reacting to fear, greed, or FOMO?"* If a move has already run far from structure, letting it pass is often the highest-expectancy choice.

---

## 3. Key Takeaways

*   **Fear** makes you shrink or freeze; **greed** pushes you to overreach or over-size; **FOMO** makes you chase late moves without structure.
*   Recognizing emotional arousal *before* order entry lets you step back and stick to your plan.
