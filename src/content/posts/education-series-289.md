Large institutions cannot deploy thousands of crores in a single session without moving prices. Instead, they accumulate leading sectors over weeks while trimming underperforming ones. Trading stocks inside a weak sector because they "look cheap" often traps capital in sideways chop, whereas focusing on relative-strength leaders puts institutional momentum behind your setups.

## How to Measure Relative Strength Objectively

1. **Sector Ratio Chart ($\text{Sector Index} \div \text{Nifty 50}$):** When the ratio line makes higher highs and stays above its rising 20-day and 50-day moving averages, the sector is outperforming the broader market.
2. **Pullback Resilience:** During a 1%–2% Nifty dip, institutional leaders hold above prior swing lows or bounce first when selling pressure eases.
3. **Breakout Participation:** Leading sectors break out to fresh multi-week highs *before* the benchmark index clears its own resistance.

## Typical Indian Market Rotation Themes

* **Rate-Cut / Liquidity Tailwind:** Private & PSU Banks, NBFCs, Autos, and Real Estate attract early institutional inflows.
* **Global Risk-Off / Rupee Depreciation:** IT Services, Pharma, and Healthcare often act as defensive or currency-beneficiary shelters.
* **Capex & Domestic Growth Cycle:** Capital Goods, Power, Metals, and Infrastructure lead earnings upgrades and relative strength rankings.

## Practical Workflow for Weekly Watchlists

* Rank major NSE thematic indices (Nifty Bank, IT, Auto, Pharma, Metal, FMCG, Energy, Realty) every weekend by 20-day and 60-day relative performance against Nifty 50.
* Restrict **long breakout and pullback trades** to the top two or three leading sectors, and look for **short setups** in the weakest lagging sectors when the broader market turns down.
