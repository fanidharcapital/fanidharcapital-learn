Welcome to **Lesson 66** of **Module 03: Fundamental Analysis** (**Lessons 61–90**) in the **Shree Fanidhar Capital Educational Series**.

With **Lessons 61–65** behind us, we now transition from qualitative business analysis into the **Three Core Financial Statements** (**Lessons 66–73**). We start with the **Income Statement**—commonly called the **Statement of Profit and Loss (`P&L`)** in Indian Annual Reports—which measures how much money a company earned, spent, and retained over a quarter or financial year (`FY`).

---

## 1. What Does the Income Statement Show?

Unlike a Balance Sheet (which is a single-day photograph), the **Income Statement is a video recording** of business performance across a specific period (for example, `1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026`).

It follows a logical **top-down waterfall**:

$$\text{Total Revenue} - \text{Operating Costs} - \text{Non-Operating Costs \& Taxes} = \text{Net Profit (PAT)}$$

Every line item answers a distinct question about operational efficiency and earnings quality.

---

## 2. Line-by-Line Anatomy of an Indian `P&L` Statement

| `P&L` Line Item | What It Represents | Why Analysts Track It |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Revenue from Operations** *(Top Line)* | Core sales generated from selling the company’s primary goods or services (net of GST). | Measures real customer demand, volume expansion, and pricing power. |
| **2. Other Income** | Non-core income such as fixed deposit interest, mutual fund gains, dividends, or profit on asset sales. | Must be separated from core operations so one-time gains do not mask weak business sales. |
| **3. Cost of Goods Sold (`COGS`)** | Direct manufacturing/procurement expenses: raw materials consumed, purchase of stock-in-trade, and changes in inventory. | Reveals **Gross Margin** (`Revenue − COGS`), the clearest indicator of raw material pass-through ability. |
| **4. Employee Benefit Expenses** | Salaries, wages, bonuses, ESOP charges, and provident fund contributions. | Critical in people-intensive sectors (IT services, banking, healthcare, retail). |
| **5. Other Operating Expenses** | Power & fuel, freight, advertising, rent, repairs, legal fees, and SG&A overheads. | Shows operating discipline and whether fixed overheads scale efficiently as sales grow. |
| **6. `EBITDA` / Operating Profit** | Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation & Amortization (`Revenue from Operations − Operating Expenses`). | Measures pure core operational cash-earning power before financing and tax structures. |
| **7. Depreciation & Amortization (`D&A`)** | Non-cash annual wear-and-tear charge on tangible plants/machinery (`Depreciation`) and intangible assets/software (`Amortization`). | Reflects ongoing capital intensity required to maintain productive assets. |
| **8. Finance Costs (`Interest`)** | Interest paid on term loans, working capital facilities, debentures, and lease liabilities. | Reveals how much operating profit goes toward servicing lenders rather than shareholders. |
| **9. Profit Before Tax (`PBT`) & Tax** | `EBIT + Other Income − Finance Costs`, followed by current and deferred corporate income tax. | Checks whether the company’s effective tax rate aligns with standard statutory rates (`~25.17%` in India). |
| **10. Profit After Tax (`PAT`)** *(Bottom Line)* | Final net profit attributable to equity shareholders after all costs, interest, and taxes. | Drives **Earnings Per Share (`EPS`)**, dividends, and retained earnings for future growth. |

## 3. Worked Example: Reading a `P&L` Waterfall (`₹ Crore`)

Consider **Aarav Specialty Chemicals Ltd.** for `FY26`:

| Line Item | Amount (`₹ Cr`) | Calculation / Margin Insight |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Revenue from Operations** | `₹1,000` | Core top-line sales |
| **Less: Cost of Goods Sold (`COGS`)** | `(₹580)` | Direct raw materials & inventory adjustment |
| **Gross Profit** | **`₹420`** | **Gross Margin = `42.0%`** (`420 / 1,000`) |
| **Less: Employee & Other Operating Expenses** | `(₹220)` | Salaries (`₹90 Cr`) + Power, Freight & SG&A (`₹130 Cr`) |
| **Operating Profit (`EBITDA`)** | **`₹200`** | **Operating Margin (`OPM`) = `20.0%`** (`200 / 1,000`) |
| **Less: Depreciation & Amortization** | `(₹40)` | Non-cash plant & machinery charge |
| **`EBIT` (Operating Profit after `D&A`)** | **`₹160`** | **`EBIT` Margin = `16.0%`** |
| **Add: Other Income** | `₹15` | Treasury interest income |
| **Less: Finance Costs (Interest)** | `(₹15)` | Interest on term debt |
| **Profit Before Tax (`PBT`)** | **`₹160`** | `160 + 15 − 15` |
| **Less: Tax Expense (`25%`)** | `(₹40)` | Corporate income tax |
| **Profit After Tax (`PAT`)** | **`₹120`** | **Net Profit Margin (`NPM`) = `12.0%`** (`120 / 1,000`) |

## 4. Three Essential Checks When Scanning Any `P&L`

1. **Core Operating Profit vs. Other Income:** If a company reports `₹100 Cr` in `PAT` but `₹65 Cr` came from **Other Income** (such as selling land or one-time tax refunds), its core business actually earned very little. Always evaluate **operating profit excluding Other Income**.
2. **Accrual Accounting Reality:** Under Indian Accounting Standards (`Ind AS`), revenue is recorded when goods are dispatched and billed—**not necessarily when cash hits the bank account**. A fast-growing `P&L` must always be verified against the **Cash Flow Statement** (covered in **Lesson 70**).
3. **Exceptional Items:** Watch for a separate line between `PBT` and `Tax` labeled **Exceptional Items** (restructuring costs, plant impairment, or one-off gains). Strip out one-time items to calculate **normalized recurring profit**.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **The Income Statement (`P&L`) tracks performance over a period**, flowing from **Revenue from Operations (Top Line)** down to **Profit After Tax (Bottom Line)**.
* **Three milestone profit lines matter most:** **Gross Profit** (product pricing power), **`EBITDA` / `EBIT`** (core operational efficiency), and **`PAT`** (final shareholder earnings).
* **Always separate core operating performance from `Other Income` and `Exceptional Items`** to judge true recurring earning power.
