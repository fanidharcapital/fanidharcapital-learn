## Introduction

When you place an order on the **NSE** or **BSE**, you are buying shares from another investor—not directly from the company. **Lesson 06** explains the difference between the **primary market**, where shares are created and issued for the first time, and the **secondary market**, where listed shares trade continuously among market participants.

## 1. The Primary Market & IPOs

The primary market is where companies raise capital directly from investors by issuing new securities:

* **Initial Public Offering (IPO):** When an unlisted company offers its shares to the public for the first time and lists on a stock exchange.
* **Fresh Issue vs. Offer for Sale (OFS):** A **Fresh Issue** creates new shares and sends funds directly to the company for expansion, debt reduction, or working capital, while an **OFS** allows existing promoters or early investors to sell their stake to public applicants.
* **Book Building & Price Band:** Instead of a single fixed price, most Indian IPOs announce a **price band** (for example, ₹480–₹505), allowing Qualified Institutional Buyers (QIBs), Non-Institutional Investors (NIIs), and Retail Individual Investors (RIIs) to bid within that range before the final cut-off price is discovered.

## 2. The Secondary Market

Once IPO allotment is completed and shares list on the exchange, trading shifts to the secondary market:

* **Investor-to-Investor Trading:** Buyers and sellers exchange existing shares through registered stockbrokers on the **NSE** and **BSE**; the issuing company neither receives money from daily trades nor sets the daily share price.
* **Continuous Price Discovery:** Supply and demand from retail participants, mutual funds, domestic institutions, and foreign portfolio investors update share prices tick by tick during market hours.
* **Liquidity & Exit Flexibility:** A deep secondary market lets investors enter or exit positions quickly using **Limit**, **Market**, or **Stop-Loss** orders, with shares held electronically in **NSDL** or **CDSL** Demat accounts.

## 3. From IPO Application to Exchange Listing

Every listed company follows a structured regulatory journey before appearing on your trading terminal:

1. **DRHP & SEBI Review:** The company files a **Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP)** disclosing financials, business risks, and fund usage for regulatory review.
2. **Bidding & ASBA:** Investors apply during the IPO window using **ASBA** (Application Supported by Blocked Amount) or UPI mandate, keeping funds blocked in their own bank account until allotment.
3. **Allotment & Listing Day:** Allotted shares are credited to successful applicants' Demat accounts, and on listing day the stock opens for continuous secondary market trading on the exchange.
