## 1. Case Study 1: Stopping "Spiral Days" With Circuit Breakers & Journal Tags

*   **What Went Wrong:** An index option trader had a solid month until two red sessions turned a minor `-1.5R` morning dip into a `-7R` loss by `3:15 PM`—driven by revenge entries, wider stops, and double lots in afternoon chop.
*   **Journal Diagnosis:** Tagging every trade by **Setup Grade** and **Rule Adherence** showed that `A+` trades with clean execution actually broke even or made modest gains, while **100% of the severe losses** came from off-plan trades taken *after* an initial loss.
*   **The Turnaround:** Enforced a hard **Daily Circuit Breaker (`-2R` max daily loss)**, removed afternoon chop entries, and capped risk at `1R`. Drawdowns stopped spiraling immediately, and equity stabilized over the next sample.

---

## 2. Case Study 2: Resolving Regime Mismatch & Scaling Back to Rebuild Edge

*   **What Went Wrong:** A swing and spread trader experienced a steady multi-week drawdown after a quiet trending market shifted into choppy, event-driven swings—continuing to trade full `1R` size on low-conviction setups while ignoring rising volatility.
*   **Journal Diagnosis:** Monthly review of `R`-multiples, `MFE`/`MAE`, and regime tags revealed that `B`-grade setups and unhedged/tight-stop entries were dragging expectancy down during the regime shift.
*   **The Turnaround:** Cut `1R` position size by **`50%`** during the drawdown, restricted entries strictly to `A+` setups aligned with current structure and `IV`, and stepped size back up gradually only after clean execution returned across a fresh sample.

---

## 3. Key Takeaways

*   Drawdowns become dangerous when **emotion, over-sizing, or regime mismatch** compound normal variance—journal tags pinpoint the exact leak.
*   Enforcing **circuit breakers**, **cutting size during drawdowns**, and **returning strictly to `A+` rules** rebuilds both capital and confidence.
