## 1. The Three-Phase Event Volatility Cycle

Ahead of major scheduled catalysts—such as **`RBI` policy**, **Union Budget**, **key inflation/growth prints**, or **quarterly earnings**—option pricing typically follows a recognizable pattern:

1.  **Pre-Event `IV` Build-Up:** As event risk approaches, uncertainty rises and demand for protection or straddles/strangles pushes **Implied Volatility (`IV`) higher**, making option premiums more expensive.
2.  **Event-Day Realized Swings:** When the announcement hits, price may gap or whip around sharply. For net **long-volatility / long-option** trades, the **actual realized move** needs to exceed what option premiums were already pricing in (plus time decay).
3.  **Post-Event `IV` Crush:** Once the uncertainty event passes and the outcome is known, fear/anticipation often drops quickly—causing **`IV` to contract ("crush")**. Falling `IV` creates **negative Vega headwinds** for net long-vega positions, while helping **short-vega / defined-risk credit structures** if price stays inside planned bounds—ready for **Part 2: Institutional Flows, Market Breadth & Smart-Money Footprints**.

---

## 2. Practical Event Discipline

Never look only at *direction* before a major macro or earnings event—check **how rich `IV` already is**, size within **`1R`**, and prefer **defined-risk structures** when gap and volatility swings can be sharp.

---

## 3. Key Takeaways

*   **`IV` often builds up before major events and crushes afterward** once uncertainty resolves.
*   Compare **expected volatility / `IV` context** and **defined risk (`1R`)** before putting on event-sensitive option trades.
