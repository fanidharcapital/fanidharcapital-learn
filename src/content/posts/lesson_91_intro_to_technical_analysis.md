## 1. What Is Technical Analysis?

Technical analysis is the structured evaluation of historical **price** and **volume** data on charts to assess supply-demand imbalances, trend direction, and high-probability reaction zones.

* **Fundamental Analysis** looks at earnings, cash flows, balance sheets, and intrinsic value.
* **Technical Analysis** looks at real-time market positioning—where buyers are stepping in (demand) and where sellers are offloading supply (resistance).
* **Combined Edge:** Institutional and disciplined retail traders often use fundamentals to build a watchlist of quality stocks and technicals to time entries and cap downside risk.

---

## 2. The Three Core Premises of Technical Analysis

1. **Price Discounts Everything**
   * Every known factor—earnings expectations, macro policy, RBI rate decisions, FII/DII flows, sector rotation, and retail sentiment—is immediately reflected in the traded price.
   * Instead of guessing how news *should* affect a stock, technicians observe how price *actually* responds.

2. **Price Moves in Trends**
   * Markets do not move in purely random steps over medium horizons; once institutional accumulation or distribution establishes a directional trend, price tends to persist in that direction until a structural shift occurs.
   * Trading with the dominant trend aligns your position with active capital flows rather than fighting them.

3. **History Tends to Repeat Itself**
   * Market participants are driven by recurring human emotions—**fear**, **greed**, **anchoring**, and **herd behavior**.
   * Because collective psychology remains consistent across market cycles, price action forms recognizable structures (breakouts, pullbacks, double tops, bases) that recur across decades and asset classes.

## 3. The Two Raw Inputs: Price & Volume

| Input | What It Measures | Analytical Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Price Action** | The actual transaction levels (Open, High, Low, Close) over a chosen timeframe | Defines trend direction, support/resistance zones, momentum shifts, and stop-loss levels |
| **Volume** | Total number of shares or contracts traded during that period | Measures institutional participation and conviction behind a price move |

### Practical Rule on Price + Volume (Indian Equities Context)
* **Breakout on Rising Volume:** Indicates genuine institutional accumulation (e.g., an NSE stock clearing a multi-month resistance zone with 2x–3x its 20-day average volume).
* **Breakout on Weak Volume:** Signals lack of broad participation and carries a higher probability of a false breakout ("bull trap").

---

## 4. Scope & Limitations: What Technical Analysis Can and Cannot Do

### What It Does Well
* **Timing & Execution:** Identifies objective entry triggers instead of buying blindly after a sharp rally.
* **Risk Definition:** Gives exact invalidation points where a trade thesis is proven wrong (stop-loss levels).
* **Multi-Timeframe Clarity:** Works consistently across intraday, swing, and multi-year positional horizons.

### Key Limitations to Respect
* **Probabilities, Not Certainties:** A clean technical setup gives a statistical edge (e.g., 55%–65% win rate with favorable risk-reward), never a 100% guarantee.
* **Subjectivity:** Two traders can draw trendlines differently; consistent rules and confirmation filters are essential.
* **Event & Gap Risk:** Sudden overnight news, earnings surprises, or regulatory announcements can cause stocks to gap past planned levels before technical indicators adjust.

---

## 5. Key Takeaways

* **Technical analysis** is a framework for reading **supply, demand, and market psychology** through price and volume—not a crystal ball.
* **Price** shows direction and structure; **volume** validates whether big money is backing the move.
* Technicals deliver their highest value when paired with **strict position sizing** and **pre-defined stop-losses**.
