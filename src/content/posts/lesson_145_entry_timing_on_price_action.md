In **Lesson #144**, we defined the three primary high-probability setups. In **Lesson #145**, we focus on the execution: **Entry Timing**. A correct analysis of a support level becomes a losing trade if you enter too early (before the level is confirmed) or too late (by chasing the breakout). Mastering timing is the final step that bridges the gap between knowing *what* will happen and profiting from *when* it happens on NSE/BSE charts.

## 1. The Golden Rule: Wait for the Candle Close

The single most important rule in entry timing is to wait for the **completed formation of the trigger candle**.

*   **The Trap of Anticipation:** At 14:30 IST, a stock is crashing into support, and you see a beautiful Hammer forming with 10 minutes left in the session. You place a buy order at `₹1,000`. Between 14:30 and 15:30, aggressive sellers step in, and the stock collapses, closing at `₹985` as a full red Marubozu. Your buy order was filled at `₹1,000`, and you are instantly deep underwater.
*   **The Discipline of the Close:** If you waited until 15:30 (the Daily Close), the Hammer never existed. The Marubozu candle told you not to buy.
*   **Reversal Footprints (Hammer, Engulfing, Doji):** Always wait for the specified time-bar (M15, H1, or D1) to **close** to confirm that the rejection or engulfment was successful.

## 2. The Two Primary Entry Execution Methods

Once the trigger candle closes, you have two choices for execution:

| Entry Method | When to Use It | Entry Price Point | Pros / Cons |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A. Candle Close Entry (Aggressive)** | High-momentum moves, strong breakouts, or when a level absolutely must hold (e.g., earnings reaction). | You enter at the **market open of the *next* candle** (or the very close of the trigger candle). | **Pro:** Ensures you get in (you won't miss the train).<br>**Con:** Usually gives a wider stop-loss (below the entire trigger wick), reducing Reward-to-Risk. |
| **B. Retest / Limit Entry (Conservative)** | Range reversals, deep pullbacks, or high-confluence setups where you have patience. | You set a **limit order** somewhere inside the trigger candle's range (e.g., at the 50% midpoint of the Hammer, or just inside the broken resistance level for a retest). | **Pro:** Gives a much tighter stop-loss and superior Reward-to-Risk ratio.<br>**Con:** Often, the stock simply "gaps and goes" (runs without you), and you miss the trade entirely. |

## 3. How to Avoid Chasing Breakouts

Chasing (buying into strength that has already moved significantly) is the primary cause of retail losses.

*   **The Chaser's Problem:** An NSE stock breaks resistance at `₹200` on a massive green candle. You panic and buy at `₹205`. The stock moves to `₹208` and then begins a routine pullback. By `₹201`, your emotions are frayed, and you panic-sell near the original breakout point, just before the real trend resumes.
*   **The Solution:**
    1.  **Enter on the Initial Breakout Close:** Only if the breakout candle itself is compact enough to allow for a `1R` stop-loss beneath its base.
    2.  **Wait for the Retest:** This is the superior approach. Let the breakout happen, let it pullback to the *original breakout level* (now polarity flip support), and enter only when it rejects that level (Retest Entry, Lesson #144).
    3.  **Ignore It:** If the stock just "runs" (e.g., gaps up `10%` and closes at the high), accept it and move on. There will always be another setup.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

*   **Never enter a trade *while* the trigger candle is still forming.** Wait for the **timeframe close** (M15, H1, D1) to confirm the footprint.
*   **Aggressive Entry:** Enter on the next candle's open. (Less risk of missing the move, more risk on stop placement).
*   **Conservative Entry:** Enter via limit order on a shallow retest of the trigger candle or breakout zone. (Superior R/R, risk of missing the move).
*   **Avoid chasing.** Buy pullbacks or breakout-retests, not breakout spikes that are already extended.
