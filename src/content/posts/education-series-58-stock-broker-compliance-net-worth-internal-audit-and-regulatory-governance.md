In **Lesson 56** and **Lesson 57**, you saw how investors escalate grievances via **SCORES 2.0** and **SMART ODR**, and how personal cyber hygiene protects trading and Demat accounts. Now zoom out to the institutional level:

**What keeps a stock broker financially sound, operationally disciplined, and compliant with SEBI and exchange rules every single day?**

From **client fund segregation** (**Lesson 45**) and **margin pledging** (**Lesson 44**) to **net worth thresholds**, **periodic audits**, **technical glitch protocols**, and **prohibited conduct**, stock brokers operate under one of India's most tightly supervised intermediary frameworks—updated under the **SEBI (Stock Brokers) Regulations, 2026** (which replaced the legacy 1992 regulations).

---

## 1. Registration, Governance & Capital Adequacy (Net Worth)

A stock broker is not merely a software front-end; it is a regulated market intermediary subject to strict entry, governance, and financial strength standards:

* **Entry & Dual-Role Clarity:** Applicants seeking registration as a stock broker must satisfy experience criteria (minimum two years of experience in trading or dealing in securities) and capital requirements commensurate with their segments and activities. Under the **SEBI (Stock Brokers) Regulations, 2026**, a registered stock broker does not need a separate registration to act as a clearing member (or vice versa), subject to obtaining the required approval from the relevant clearing corporation or stock exchange.
* **Resident Designated Director:** Every broking entity must have at least one **Designated Director** (or designated partner/proprietor, as applicable) who resides in India for **182 days or more** during the financial year, ensuring local regulatory accountability.
* **Continuous Net Worth & Capital Adequacy:** Brokers must continuously maintain prescribed net worth and capital adequacy levels linked to the scale and risk of their operations (such as cash, F&O, or margin funding activities). Exchanges and clearing corporations require periodic **Net Worth Certificates** and audited financial submissions—with single-window filing streamlining for brokers registered across multiple exchanges (for instance, joint NSE–BSE members submitting annual audited net worth filings to NSE, while exclusive BSE members file on BSE's **BEFS** portal).

## 2. Core Operational Obligations: Records, Surveillance & Tech Glitches

To prevent operational blind spots and protect client assets, brokers are bound by strict day-to-day governance mandates:

| Compliance Pillar | Key Operational Requirement |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Books, Records & 8-Year Preservation** | Brokers must maintain accurate books of accounts, client ledgers, contract notes, and audit trails—including in electronic form—for a minimum preservation period of **8 years**. |
| **Client Asset Segregation & Upstreaming** | Client funds and securities must remain strictly segregated from the broker's proprietary funds, with idle client funds upstreamed daily to the Clearing Corporation and settled periodically. |
| **Internal Surveillance & Fraud Prevention** | Brokers must maintain internal surveillance controls to detect and report suspicious trading patterns, unauthorized terminal access, or potential market abuse and mule-account activity. |
| **Technical Glitch Governance** | Under SEBI's updated technical glitch framework (effective January 2026), brokers above the client threshold (>10,000 clients) must monitor critical trading system disruptions, notify exchanges/clients within **2 hours** via the Common Reporting Platform when triggered, and maintain resilient failover systems. |

## 3. Audits, Inspections & Enforcement Powers

Compliance on paper is verified through continuous **audits, inspections, and exchange supervision**:

1. **Internal & System Audits:** Stock exchanges mandate periodic **internal audits** (covering client fund/security reconciliation, margin collection, segregation, and KYC/AML compliance) as well as **information technology / system audits** by independent auditors.
2. **Exchange & SEBI Inspections:** NSE, BSE, clearing corporations, and SEBI conduct scheduled and surprise inspections of books, client records, branches/Authorized Persons, and trading systems.
3. **Supervisory & Enforcement Actions:** Non-compliance can trigger exchange penalties, trading/segment restrictions, suspension or cancellation of registration, or monetary remedies such as disgorgement or restitution—alongside public investor-protection disclosures on broker websites.

---

## 4. Strictly Prohibited Conduct for Stock Brokers

As a market participant, knowing what a registered broker **cannot** legally do helps you spot red flags immediately:

* **No Guaranteed or Assured Returns:** Brokers cannot promise, guarantee, or market assured profits on trading or investments.
* **No Direct Cash Acceptance from Clients:** Brokers are prohibited from accepting cash directly from clients (or via cash deposits) for trading/settlement.
* **No Misuse or Pooling of Client Funds/Securities:** Commingling or diverting client money or Demat shares for proprietary operations or unrelated businesses is strictly barred.
* **No Unapproved Side Activities:** Brokers cannot operate outside their permitted scope of registration without required approvals or regulatory frameworks.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **Stock brokers operate under SEBI's Stock Brokers Regulations (2026) and exchange supervision**, requiring ongoing net worth/capital adequacy, a resident designated director (182+ days), client asset segregation, and **8-year record preservation**.
* **Internal audits, system audits, and SEBI/exchange inspections** verify that client funds, margins, surveillance controls, and critical trading systems function reliably.
* **Promising assured returns, accepting cash directly from clients, and misusing client funds or securities** are strictly prohibited.
