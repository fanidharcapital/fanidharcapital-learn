## 1. Single-Trade Noise vs. Sample-Size Edge

*   **Any Single Trade Can Lose:** Even a high-probability breakout, pullback, or credit spread with clean chart structure and favorable risk-reward can fail on a single occurrence due to random market noise, unexpected news, or volatility shifts.
*   **Edge Emerges Across a Sample:** A genuine trading edge only reveals itself over a **sample of trades** (such as `20`, `50`, or `100` executions) where repeatable rules, positive expectancy, and controlled risk compound over time.

---

## 2. Escaping Outcome Bias Through Process Scoring

*   **What Is Outcome Bias?** Judging the *quality* of a decision purely by *how it turned out*—praising a rule-breaking, oversized impulse trade because it got lucky on green candles, or condemning a textbook setup with a proper stop-loss because price reversed.
*   **Score the Process, Not Just the P&L:** Grade every trade on two separate lenses:
    1.  **Setup & Risk Execution:** Did the trade meet your playbook criteria, respect `1R` sizing, and honor pre-planned targets/stops?
    2.  **Financial Result:** What was the net P&L in points/rupees or `R`-multiples?
*   A **disciplined loss** (`-1R` taken cleanly on a valid setup) is a **good trade**; a **sloppy win** (chasing without a stop or risking `5R`) is a **dangerous habit** waiting to punish you.

---

## 3. Key Takeaways

*   Treat every individual trade as one sample in a probability distribution—never expect certainty on a single order.
*   Defeat **outcome bias** by evaluating **rule adherence and risk discipline** independently from short-term P&L.
