Welcome to **Lesson 67** of **Module 03: Fundamental Analysis** (**Lessons 61–90**) in the **Shree Fanidhar Capital Educational Series**.

In **Lesson 66**, we mapped out the complete structure of the Income Statement (`P&L`) from top-line revenue down to bottom-line profit. Today, we zoom in on the **middle-to-bottom bridge**—the four critical deductions (**Depreciation, Amortization, Interest, and Taxes**) that determine how much of a company’s operating profit actually reaches equity shareholders.

---

## 1. The Profit Bridge: From `EBITDA` to `PAT`

Financial headlines frequently quote **`EBITDA`**, **`EBIT`**, **`PBT`**, and **`PAT`** interchangeably, even though each metric measures a very different layer of business reality:

$$\text{EBITDA} \xrightarrow{-\text{ D\&A}} \text{EBIT} \xrightarrow{+\text{ Other Income } - \text{ Interest}} \text{PBT} \xrightarrow{-\text{ Tax}} \text{PAT}$$

| Metric | Full Form | What It Measures | Best Used For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`EBITDA`** | Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation & Amortization | Core operational earning power before capital intensity, debt financing, and tax structures. | Comparing operating performance across peers with different debt levels or plant ages. |
| **`EBIT`** | Earnings Before Interest & Taxes *(Operating Profit)* | Operating profit *after* accounting for the annual wear-and-tear (`D&A`) of productive assets. | Evaluating true economic profitability in capital-intensive industries (manufacturing, telecom, power). |
| **`PBT`** | Profit Before Tax *(EBT)* | Earnings remaining after paying lenders (`Finance Costs`) and adding treasury/other income. | Judging pre-tax profitability independent of one-off tax credits or differing tax regimes. |
| **`PAT`** | Profit After Tax *(Net Income)* | Final net profit belonging exclusively to equity shareholders. | Calculating `EPS`, `P/E` ratios, dividend capacity, and retained earnings. |

## 2. Depreciation vs. Amortization (`D&A`): Non-Cash, Not "Non-Real"

When a company buys a ₹500 Crore factory or develops ₹80 Crore of proprietary software, accounting rules (`Ind AS 16` and `Ind AS 38`) do not expense the entire cost in Year 1. Instead, the cost is spread across the asset's useful economic life:

* **Depreciation (Tangible Assets):** Systematic annual write-off of physical assets—machinery, factory buildings, vehicles, and servers. *(Land is never depreciated because it does not wear out.)*
* **Amortization (Intangible Assets):** Systematic annual write-off of non-physical assets—patents, software licenses, trademarks, and customer contracts acquired in buyouts.

> **Why Charlie Munger Warned Against Blind `EBITDA` Worship:** While `D&A` does not involve cash leaving the bank *this year*, machines eventually wear out and must be replaced via **Maintenance Capital Expenditure (`Capex`)**. Ignoring `D&A` in heavy industries like steel, cement, or airlines makes an asset-heavy business look far more profitable than it truly is.

## 3. Finance Costs (Interest): How Debt Shapes Bottom-Line Growth

**Finance Costs** represent the interest paid on term loans, working-capital borrowings, corporate bonds, and lease liabilities (`Ind AS 116`).

* **Financial Leverage Effect:** Because interest is largely a **fixed expense**, a modest `15%` rise in `EBIT` for a company carrying debt can translate into a `25%–30%` surge in `PBT` and `PAT`—and conversely, a `15%` drop in `EBIT` can wipe out bottom-line profits during a slowdown.
* **Interest Coverage Ratio:** Always check how comfortably operating profit covers interest outgo:

$$\text{Interest Coverage Ratio} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Finance Costs}}$$

A ratio above **`4x–5x`** signals comfortable debt servicing; below **`2x`** warns of balance-sheet stress.

## 4. Corporate Taxes: Statutory Rate vs. Effective Tax Rate

In India, most domestic companies opt for the **Section `115BAA`** corporate tax regime, resulting in a headline statutory tax rate of **`25.17%`** (`22%` base tax + `10%` surcharge + `4%` cess).

Always compute the company’s **Effective Tax Rate (`ETR`)**:

$$\text{Effective Tax Rate} = \frac{\text{Total Tax Expense}}{\text{PBT}} \times 100$$

* **If `ETR` is consistently near `25%`:** Earnings are straightforward and tax-compliant.
* **If `ETR` is unusually low (`8%–15%`):** Check the Notes to Accounts—is it due to legitimate `SEZ`/renewable tax holidays or accumulated past losses, or a one-time deferred tax credit that will vanish next year?
* **If `ETR` is unusually high (`32%–40%`):** Investigate non-deductible expenses or unadjusted losses in overseas subsidiaries.

## 5. Worked Comparison: Asset-Light IT Firm vs. Asset-Heavy Manufacturer (`₹ Crore`)

Notice how two companies with the **exact same `₹200 Cr` `EBITDA`** deliver vastly different bottom-line profits:

| `P&L` Step | Asset-Light Software Firm (`₹ Cr`) | Asset-Heavy Auto Component Firm (`₹ Cr`) | Key Takeaway |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **`EBITDA`** | **`₹200`** | **`₹200`** | Identical headline `EBITDA` |
| **Less: Depreciation & Amortization** | `(₹12)` | `(₹65)` | Heavy machinery eats `32.5%` of `EBITDA` |
| **`EBIT` (Operating Profit)** | **`₹188`** | **`₹135`** | True operating gap emerges here |
| **Add: Other Income** | `₹12` | `₹5` | Net cash vs. indebted balance sheet |
| **Less: Finance Costs (Interest)** | `(₹0)` | `(₹40)` | Debt servicing takes another `₹40 Cr` |
| **`PBT` (Profit Before Tax)** | **`₹200`** | **`₹100`** | Half the pre-tax profit despite equal `EBITDA` |
| **Less: Corporate Tax (`25%`)** | `(₹50)` | `(₹25)` | Standard `25%` effective tax rate |
| **`PAT` (Net Profit)** | **`₹150`** | **`₹75`** | **2x higher bottom-line profit** for the asset-light business |

## Key Takeaways

* **`EBITDA` measures core cash-operating profit before capital intensity**, whereas **`EBIT` deducts `D&A`** to reflect the real economic cost of maintaining physical and intangible assets.
* **Interest (`Finance Costs`) amplifies both upside and downside**—always verify the **Interest Coverage Ratio (`EBIT / Interest`)** before trusting bottom-line growth.
* **Check the Effective Tax Rate (`Tax / PBT`)** to distinguish sustainable net earnings from one-off tax anomalies.
