## Introduction

Earlier lessons introduced individual building blocks—from market structure and shares to indices and order execution. **Lesson 04** ties these foundations together so you can see how **market participants** interact with **shares** and **indices** using specific **order types** on Indian exchanges like the **NSE** and **BSE**.

## 1. Market Participants

An active market needs distinct roles working together so liquidity exists on both sides of a trade:

* **Retail Investors & Traders:** Individuals participating directly through registered stockbrokers—ranging from long-term equity investors focused on business compounding to active traders managing short-term setups.
* **Institutional Participants:** Mutual funds, domestic financial institutions, insurers, and foreign portfolio investors (FPIs) that deploy larger capital pools and shape market depth and liquidity.
* **Market Infrastructure & Intermediaries:** Stock exchanges, clearing corporations, depositories, clearing banks, and SEBI-registered brokers/depository participants that route orders, match trades, and complete **T+1 settlement**.

## 2. Shares & Market Indices

Once you know *who* participates, the next step is knowing *what* is being owned or tracked:

* **Equity Shares:** Fractional ownership units in a listed company, carrying potential for capital appreciation and corporate actions (such as dividends) alongside market risk.
* **Market Indices:** Composite benchmarks (such as **NIFTY 50** and **SENSEX**) that track baskets of representative stocks to summarize segment or market performance, gauge sentiment, and act as benchmarks or underlyings for index products.
* **Price Discovery:** Continuous interaction between buyer bids and seller offers on the exchange determines the market price at which trades match.

## 3. Order Types in Practice

Choosing the right order type translates your market view into execution rules on the exchange:

* **Limit Order:** Sets the exact maximum buy price or minimum sell price you will accept—giving **price certainty** when liquidity reaches your level.
* **Market Order:** Seeks immediate execution at the best available price in the order book—prioritizing **fill speed** over exact price control.
* **Stop-Loss Order (SL-M / SL):** Uses a **trigger price** to activate an order only if price reaches a defined threshold, helping structure risk control or breakout entries alongside validity modifiers like **DAY**, **IOC**, and **GTT**.
