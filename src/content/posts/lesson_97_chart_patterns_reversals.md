## 1. Core Reversal Patterns at a Glance

Reversal patterns are **not** complete until price breaks and closes beyond the pattern's **neckline** (confirmation boundary). Before the neckline breaks, a potential reversal pattern is still just a sideways consolidation.

| Pattern | Prior Trend Required | Structural Anatomy | Confirmation Trigger | Measured Move Projection |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Double Top ("M" Pattern)** | Sustained Uptrend | Two roughly equal peaks separated by a trough (neckline), with lighter volume on the second peak | Decisive close **below** the trough neckline on expanding volume | Height from peaks to neckline projected **downward** from the breakdown point |
| **Double Bottom ("W" Pattern)** | Sustained Downtrend | Two roughly equal lows separated by a middle peak (neckline), often with a higher low or sweep on the second bottom | Decisive close **above** the peak neckline on strong volume | Height from bottoms to neckline projected **upward** from the breakout point |
| **Head & Shoulders (Bearish)** | Sustained Uptrend | Left Shoulder, a higher central **Head** (exhaustion peak), and a lower **Right Shoulder**, connected along a neckline | Decisive close **below** the neckline connecting the two intervening lows | Vertical distance from the **Head** to the neckline projected **downward** |
| **Inverse Head & Shoulders (Bullish)** | Sustained Downtrend | Left Shoulder, a deeper central **Head** (capitulation low), and a higher **Right Shoulder** | Decisive close **above** the neckline on surging volume | Vertical distance from the **Head** to the neckline projected **upward** |
| **Rounded Bottom / Saucer Base** | Extended Downtrend / Base | Gradual, U-shaped shift from selling pressure to quiet institutional accumulation | Breakout above the upper rim / resistance lip on heavy volume | Depth of the saucer bowl projected **upward** from the rim breakout |

## 2. Why Neckline Confirmation Prevents Premature Entries

One of the costliest mistakes in technical trading is shorting the "second peak" of a suspected Double Top or buying the "right shoulder" of an unconfirmed Inverse Head & Shoulders before the neckline breaks:

* **Unconfirmed Pattern Risk:** In strong bull markets on NSE, what looks like a "Double Top" on a Daily chart frequently turns into a bullish rectangle continuation once institutions absorb overhead supply and push to new highs.
* **Two Entry Tactics Once Confirmed:**
  1. **Breakout Close Entry:** Enter when a full-bodied candle closes cleanly across the neckline with above-average volume, placing the stop-loss beyond the Right Shoulder (or second peak/bottom).
  2. **Neckline Retest (Polarity Flip) Entry:** Wait for price to pull back and retest the broken neckline as new support (or new resistance in bearish setups), offering a tighter stop-loss and cleaner risk-reward.

---

## 3. Institutional Clues Inside Reversal Bases (NSE / BSE Context)

* **Volume Signature:** During a **Head & Shoulders** top, volume is often heavy on the Left Shoulder and Head, but noticeably weaker on the Right Shoulder rally—showing diminishing institutional appetite before the neckline breaks.
* **Rounded Bases & Stage-1 Accumulation:** On mid-cap and large-cap NSE stocks emerging from multi-quarter corrections, **Rounded Bottoms** reflect slow institutional accumulation without sharp V-shaped spikes. When volume expands sharply as price nears the right rim of the saucer, institutional sponsorship is usually confirmed.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

* **A reversal pattern needs a prior trend to reverse**—a Double Bottom inside a flat sideways range carries far less significance than one after a sustained decline.
* **Never trade the pattern before the neckline breaks:** Wait for a confirmed candle close beyond the neckline validated by volume.
* Use the **measured move** (pattern height) as an objective target zone, while anchoring your **stop-loss** beyond the right-side swing structure.
