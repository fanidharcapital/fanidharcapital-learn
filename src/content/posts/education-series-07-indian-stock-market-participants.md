Welcome to **Lesson 07** of **Module 01: Market foundation** (**Lessons 01–30**) in the **Shree Fanidhar Capital Education Series**.

![Who Participates in the Indian Stock Market](/images/lessons/lesson-07.png)

Every price tick on the **National Stock Exchange (NSE)** or **Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE)** is the result of millions of orders interacting in real time. Understanding **who** is placing those orders—and **why**—helps explain why markets rally, why sharp sell-offs occur even when company fundamentals look unchanged, and how market infrastructure keeps transactions safe.

In India, stock market participants fall into three broad layers: **Buyers & Sellers (Capital Providers)**, **Market Intermediaries**, and **Market Infrastructure & Regulators**.

---

## 1. Individual & Non-Institutional Participants

### Retail Individual Investors (RIIs)
Retail investors are everyday individuals, Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs), and eligible Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) investing their personal savings.
* **Capital Size:** In primary market offerings (IPOs), SEBI defines retail applications as bids **up to ₹2 lakh** per PAN.
* **Role in the Market:** Retail investors participate both directly (buying shares and ETFs) and indirectly (through mutual fund SIPs). Together, millions of retail accounts provide broad market depth and steady domestic inflows.

### Traders (Intraday, Swing & Positional)
While investors focus on multi-year business ownership and compounding, traders aim to profit from short- to medium-term price movements:
* **Intraday Traders:** Open and close positions within the same trading session (9:15 AM to 3:30 PM IST), providing crucial intraday liquidity and tighter bid-ask spreads.
* **Swing & Positional Traders:** Hold stocks or derivatives for days to weeks based on price action, earnings momentum, or technical setups.
* **Arbitrageurs:** Simultaneously buy and sell across different segments (such as cash vs. futures, or NSE vs. BSE) to capture tiny price differences, keeping prices aligned across venues.

### High Net-Worth Individuals (HNIs / NIIs)
Classified by SEBI under **Non-Institutional Investors (NIIs)** in IPOs, HNIs are wealthy individuals or family offices deploying larger ticket sizes—typically **above ₹2 lakh** (further categorized into Small NIIs from ₹2 lakh to ₹10 lakh, and Big NIIs above ₹10 lakh). Their block trades and concentrated positions often influence mid-cap and small-cap liquidity.

## 2. Institutional Investors (The Big Capital Movers)

Institutions manage pools of capital worth thousands of crores. Because of their order sizes, daily **FII/FPI and DII net buy/sell data** published by NSE and BSE is closely tracked across the market.

### Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs)
DIIs are Indian financial institutions investing domestic savings into Indian markets:
* **Mutual Funds (AMCs):** Deploy monthly SIP and lump-sum inflows from Indian households across large-, mid-, and small-cap stocks.
* **Insurance Companies (e.g., LIC & Private Insurers):** Invest long-term policyholder premiums with multi-year horizons.
* **Banks, Pension Funds (NPS / EPFO), & AIFs:** Provide steady, long-duration capital that frequently absorbs volatility during global sell-offs.

### Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs / FIIs)
Formerly referred to as Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) and now regulated under SEBI's **Foreign Portfolio Investor (FPI)** framework, these include global pension funds, sovereign wealth funds, foreign mutual funds, and hedge funds.
* **Market Impact:** FPIs hold substantial stakes in Nifty 50 and banking heavyweights. Their capital flows respond to global interest rates, US dollar strength, crude oil prices, and India's relative earnings growth.

### Proprietary Desks & Market Makers
* **Proprietary (Prop) Trading Desks:** Trading firms using their own capital and algorithmic strategies to trade equities, futures, and options.
* **Market Makers:** Registered intermediaries obligated to continuously quote both buy (bid) and sell (ask) prices in less liquid segments—such as SME stocks and ETFs—so investors can enter or exit smoothly.

---

## 3. Intermediaries & Infrastructure Institutions

An individual investor cannot walk directly onto the exchange floor to place an order. A regulated chain of institutions connects your screen to the matching engine:

| Participant | Core Function | Indian Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stockbrokers** | SEBI-registered members that route client orders to exchanges, manage trading terminals, and enforce margin rules. | Zerodha, Kotak Securities, Groww, ICICI Direct |
| **Stock Exchanges** | Electronic order-matching platforms where buyers and sellers discover prices transparently. | NSE, BSE |
| **Clearing Corporations** | Act as the central counterparty—guaranteeing that every trade settles on **T+1** even if one party defaults. | NSE Clearing (NCL), Indian Clearing Corporation (ICCL) |
| **Depositories & DPs** | Hold shares safely in electronic (dematerialized) form and transfer ownership upon settlement. | NSDL, CDSL (accessed via Depository Participants) |
| **Regulator** | Protects investor interests, sets disclosure and margin norms, and monitors market manipulation. | Securities and Exchange Board of India (**SEBI**) |

---

## 4. How All Participants Fit Together in a Single Trade

When you place a buy order for 10 shares on your broker app:
1. **Your Stockbroker** verifies your available funds/margin and routes the order to **NSE or BSE**.
2. **The Exchange Matching Engine** pairs your buy order with a matching sell order—which could come from a retail trader in Ahmedabad, a domestic mutual fund in Mumbai, or an FPI algorithm in Singapore.
3. **The Clearing Corporation** guarantees the transaction and nets all obligations at the end of the day.
4. **The Depository (CDSL/NSDL)** credits the shares to your Demat account on the **T+1 settlement cycle**, while funds are transferred to the seller.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **Diversity creates liquidity:** Retail investors, short-term traders, HNIs, DIIs, and FPIs operate on different time horizons—ensuring there is almost always a counterparty on the other side of a trade.
* **DIIs vs. FPIs shape macro trends:** FPIs respond quickly to global liquidity and currency shifts, while DIIs backed by domestic SIPs and insurance flows act as a structural stabilizer.
* **Ring-fenced safety:** Separating exchanges, brokers, clearing corporations, and depositories under SEBI oversight ensures your shares sit safely in your Demat account rather than on a broker's balance sheet.
