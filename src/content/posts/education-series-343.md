A trade that looks calm and profitable on a quiet Tuesday morning can experience sharp mark-to-market pressure in minutes if an unexpected global or domestic headline triggers a sudden volatility spike.

## Case Setup: Calm Grind Followed by a Sudden IV Jump

* **Initial Position:** A trader opens a short-theta credit structure with ~18 DTE while India VIX is low and option IV is compressed, collecting modest daily time decay.
* **The Shock:** Mid-week, an adverse macro headline hits; spot pulls back sharply and **India VIX jumps several points** intraday, pushing front-month option IV well above entry levels.
* **Why Mark-to-Market Swings:** Even though time decay still ticks forward, **short vega** (rising IV inflating option premiums) and **directional delta/gamma** move against the short side simultaneously—creating a temporary or sustained unrealized loss larger than a few days of theta.

## Key Execution Lessons

* **Respect Low-VIX Short-Vega Asymmetry:** When VIX is already near subdued levels, daily theta is small while the risk of a volatility rebound is elevated.
* **Keep Risk Defined & Pre-Planned:** Using defined-risk wings and pre-set exit/loss rules prevents a sudden volatility jump from turning a controlled trade into an outsized loss.
