Price rarely moves in straight lines; it steps from liquidity pool to liquidity pool across timeframes. Reading **multi-timeframe market structure** helps you see whether a move is a healthy trend continuation, a stop-hunt sweep before the real move, or an early sign that momentum is shifting.

## Core Structural Concepts

1. **Break of Structure (BOS):** In an uptrend, a clean candle close above a prior swing high confirms trend continuation. In a downtrend, a clean close below a prior swing low confirms bearish continuation.
2. **Change of Character (CHoCH):** The first structural break against the prevailing swing rhythm—such as a downtrend breaking above a lower high or an uptrend losing a higher low—warning that a reversal or deeper pullback may be forming.
3. **Liquidity Sweep (Stop Hunt):** Price briefly pokes past an obvious swing high or low where retail stop-losses and breakout orders cluster, triggers those orders, and quickly snaps back inside prior structure.

## Multi-Timeframe Hierarchy

| Timeframe Role | What to Identify | Practical Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Higher Timeframe (Daily / 4H)** | Primary trend bias, major swing highs/lows, and liquidity pools | Trade *with* higher-timeframe structure unless a clear CHoCH occurs |
| **Execution Timeframe (15m / 5m)** | Pullbacks into demand/supply, sweeps, and BOS/CHoCH triggers | Enter only after structural confirmation and a defined invalidation level |

## Key Takeaways

* Treat **BOS** as confirmation of existing trend direction and **CHoCH** as an early regime-shift alert—not an automatic reversal trade on its own.
* When a breakout above a prior swing high immediately fails and closes back inside the range on a lower timeframe, treat it as a **liquidity sweep** rather than a clean continuation.
