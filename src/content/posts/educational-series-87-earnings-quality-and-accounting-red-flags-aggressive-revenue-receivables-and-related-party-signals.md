## 1. Key Accounting & Earnings Quality Red Flags

Accounting standards leave room for judgment and estimates. Watch for clusters of these warning signals rather than jumping to conclusions from a single item:

| Red Flag Category | What to Look For in Filings & Ratios | Why It Matters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Aggressive Revenue & Channel Stuffing** | Sudden Q4 sales spikes, unusual credit terms pushed onto distributors, or revenue booked before delivery/acceptance is final. | Pulls future demand forward or books sales that turn into returns, discounts, or bad debts later. |
| **2. Swelling Receivables & Inventory** | Trade receivables (`Debtor Days`) or `Inventory Days` rising much faster than revenue over multiple quarters/years. | Signals customers paying slowly, difficulty collecting cash, or slow-moving/obsolete stock. |
| **3. Profit vs. Cash Flow Divergence** | Growing `PAT` or `EBITDA` paired with weak or negative `CFO` or persistently low `FCF / PAT` (`Lesson 78`). | Paper earnings may be trapped in working capital or non-cash accruals rather than real cash. |
| **4. Recurring "One-Off" Gains & Cost Capitalization** | Frequent "exceptional/other income" boosts, shifting routine expenses into `Capex` (capitalizing costs), or stretching useful lives/depreciation. | Artificially inflates operating profit, `EPS`, or `ROCE` while hiding ongoing economic costs. |
| **5. Related-Party & Governance Signals** | Large loans/advances to promoter entities, non-arm's-length pricing, frequent auditor changes, or qualified/emphasis-of-matter remarks (`Lesson 73`). | Can transfer value away from minority shareholders or mask underlying balance-sheet stress. |

## 2. Worked Diagnostic: Clean Compounder vs. Red-Flag Warning Profile

Compare two companies both reporting **`22%` YoY `PAT` growth** in the latest financial year:

| Check | Company X (`Clean Earnings`) | Company Y (`Needs Deeper Scrutiny`) | Analytical Takeaway |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Revenue Growth (`YoY`)** | `18%` | `20%` | Similar top-line expansion |
| **Receivable Days (`DSO`)** | `42 days` *(vs. `44 days` last year)* | `98 days` *(up from `61 days` last year)* | Company Y is funding sales through extended credit |
| **`CFO / PAT` Conversion** | `1.05x` | `0.38x` | Company X backs profits with operating cash; Company Y lags sharply |
| **Other Income Share of `PBT`** | `4%` *(routine treasury interest)* | `29%` *(asset/investment gains & write-backs)* | Core operating `PBT` growth in Company Y is much slower than headline `PAT` |
| **Related-Party Loans / Guarantees** | Minimal, disclosed & routine | Rising advances/guarantees to unlisted group entities | Requires careful footnote & governance review |

## 3. How to Investigate Red Flags Without Overreacting

1. **Look for Patterns, Not Isolated Anomalies:** A single quarter with higher receivables due to a festive season or a major project milestone can be normal—check multi-year trends and management's explanation.
2. **Read Footnotes, Auditor Notes & Cash Flows Together:** Always cross-check `Notes to Accounts` (`Lesson 73`), `Cash Flow Statement` (`Lesson 70`), and `CFO / EBITDA` (`Lesson 78`) before trusting headline `EPS` or `P/E` (`Lesson 82`).
3. **Remember Governance Is a Prerequisite to Valuation:** No valuation multiple or margin of safety fully protects minority shareholders if capital is consistently diverted or financial disclosures lack transparency.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **High reported `PAT` or `EPS` does not automatically equal high earnings quality**—always check cash conversion (`CFO` and `FCF`), working-capital trends, and the composition of profit.
* **Rising receivable/inventory days, frequent "one-time" gains, cost capitalization, and opaque related-party dealings** are classic signals to dig deeper.
* **Treat red flags as prompts for disciplined verification** in the footnotes and cash flows rather than ignoring them in pursuit of a low multiple.
