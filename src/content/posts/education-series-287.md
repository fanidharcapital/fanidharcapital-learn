Scheduled macro releases—such as the RBI Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) announcement at 10:00 AM IST, Union Budget speech, or domestic and US CPI prints—compress weeks of repricing into minutes. In the first few minutes after a release, bid-ask spreads widen, option implied volatility spikes, and false breakouts sweep liquidity on both sides.

## Three Phases of a Scheduled Macro Release

1. **Pre-Event Compression (24–48 Hours Prior):** Spot price often coils inside a narrow range while ATM option premiums inflate to price in the expected move.
2. **Initial Release Whip (First 15–30 Minutes):** Algorithmic headlines react to repo rate commentary, inflation projections, or fiscal deficit targets, frequently triggering sharp two-way spikes.
3. **Post-Event Structure Formation:** Once the market digests the full statement and bond yields settle, institutional order flow establishes a directional trend or clean range boundary.

## Rules for Event-Day Execution

* **Cut Position Size by 50% or More:** Wider intraday ranges require wider stop-losses; reducing lot size keeps rupee risk constant.
* **Avoid Market Orders During the Announcement Minute:** Slippage during fast ticks can distort your reward-to-risk ratio before the trade even starts.
* **Wait for a 15-Minute or 30-Minute Candle Close:** Let the initial liquidity sweep finish and enter only after price holds above or below the post-announcement range.
* **Respect Overnight Gap Risk:** For CPI or Fed releases announced after NSE/BSE market hours, avoid carrying unhedged naked futures or short options overnight.

## Key Takeaways

* Surviving macro events is about **controlling position size and waiting for post-headline structure**, not guessing the policy outcome ahead of time.
* A clean retest after the initial spike offers far better risk-adjusted entries than chasing the first vertical candle.
