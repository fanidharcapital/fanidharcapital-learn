In **Lesson #140**, we established that the location of a candle determines its meaning. In **Lesson #141**, we expand that context beyond single levels to the **overall Market Regime**. Markets do not behave the same way every day. Trying to trade a choppy, mean-reverting market with breakout strategies, or trying to fade every pullback in a strong trend, is the fastest way to drain your capital on NSE/BSE charts. You must learn to read the active regime and adapt your expectations.

## 1. Identifying the Two Primary Market Regimes

Before taking any trade, you must classify the current state of your NSE/BSE stock or index. Look at the last 50–100 candles on the 1-Hour or Daily chart.

| Regime | Price Action Footprint | Market Regime Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Trending Regime** | **Impulse & Pullback Cycles:** Higher Highs/Lows (Up) or Lower Highs/Lows (Down). | Imbalance exists. One side (Buyers or Sellers) is in control and moving price to new value areas. |
| **Choppy / Range Regime** | **Mean Reversion:** Overlapping candles, erratic swings, price rotates sideways between defined horizontal boundaries. | Equilibrium exists. Buyers and Sellers agree on value at current levels. No sustained directional conviction exists. |

---

## 2. Adapting Price-Action Strategy to Regime

Your trading playbook must align with the active regime.

### Playbook A: Trading the Trending Regime (Impulse/Pullback)
*   **Context:** The path of least resistance is established.
*   **Expectations:** Trend days (strong closes in one direction), shallow/orderly pullbacks, breakouts that find follow-through.
*   **High-Probability Setups (Part 3, Lesson #144):**
    1.  **Pullbacks to Structure:** Buying a rejection candle (Hammer) at a polarity flip level or rising moving average during an uptrend.
    2.  **Breakout-Retests:** Buying the successful retest of a broken resistance level.
*   **What to Avoid:** Fading strong momentum moves. Trying to pick the *exact* top or bottom.

### Playbook B: Trading the Choppy/Range Regime (Mean Reversion)
*   **Context:** The market is "in balance." Price reverts to the mean.
*   **Expectations:** Failed breakouts, rapid reversals at extremes, high intraday volatility without net directional change.
*   **High-Probability Setups (Part 3, Lesson #144):**
    1.  **Range Reversals / Fades:** Selling a rejection candle (Shooting Star) at the Range Ceiling (Resistance) targeting the Mean or Range Floor.
    2.  **Buying Support / Selling Resistance:** Entering **at the extremes** with tight stops.
*   **What to Avoid:** Chasing breakouts *outside* the range. Buying strength at Resistance or selling weakness at Support.

---

## 3. Regime Shifts: Compression Before Expansion

Trends do not last forever, and ranges do not last forever. The most powerful trades occur during **Regime Shifts**.

1.  **Range-to-Trend:** A long period of horizontal **Compression** (Lesson #139) builds energy. The breakout that follows ignition initiates a new Trend Regime.
2.  **Trend-to-Range:** An extended trend runs out of steam, momentum slows (creates a **Change of Character - CHoCH**), and price enters a Distribution / Accumulation phase, forming a new Range Regime.

When you identify that compression is tightening (Lesson #139), your bias shifts from mean-reversion (fading boundaries) to breakout preparation (ignition).

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

*   You cannot trade every setup every day. Your strategy must adapt to the market **Regime**.
*   **Trending markets** reward **buying pullbacks** and **trading breakouts**.
*   **Choppy/Range-bound markets** reward **mean reversion (fading extremes)** and punish breakout chasers.
*   Always identify the regime before analyzing patterns or looking for entries.
