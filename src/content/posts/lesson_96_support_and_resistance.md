## 1. Support & Resistance as Zones (Not Razor-Thin Lines)

Support and resistance represent price areas where an imbalance between buyers and sellers repeatedly halts or reverses price movement.

| Concept | Market Psychology | Institutional Behavior | Practical Charting Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Support (Demand Zone)** | Buying interest is strong enough to absorb incoming selling pressure | Institutions accumulate value on dips; short-sellers cover positions | Draw a shaded band spanning the candle bodies and wicks of prior swing lows |
| **Resistance (Supply Zone)** | Selling pressure overwhelms fresh buying demand | Institutions distribute holdings or book profits into rallies | Draw a shaded band spanning the candle bodies and wicks of prior swing highs |

### High-Confluence Level Anchors (NSE / BSE Context)
* **Major Swing Highs & Swing Lows:** Weekly and Daily turning points carry the heaviest memory among institutional desks.
* **Psychological Round Numbers:** Levels like **25,000** on Nifty 50 or **₹1,000 / ₹2,500** on large-cap NSE stocks attract heavy limit orders and options open-interest clustering.
* **Multi-Touch Bases:** A resistance band tested 3–4 times with progressively shallower pullbacks signals steady absorption of overhead supply.

## 2. The Polarity Flip (S/R Flip)

One of the highest-probability structural behaviors in technical analysis is **role reversal** once a major level gives way:

1. **Resistance Turns Into Support:** When price closes decisively *above* a well-established resistance band on strong volume, trapped short-sellers and sidelined buyers look to enter on the first pullback—turning the former ceiling into a new floor.
2. **Support Turns Into Resistance:** When price breaks down *below* a major support zone, trapped longs look to exit at breakeven on any bounce—turning the former floor into overhead supply.

---

## 3. Liquidity Dynamics: Breakouts vs. Stop-Runs ("Liquidity Sweeps")

Support and resistance levels are pools of resting orders—stop-losses from existing positions and breakout orders from momentum traders sit just beyond obvious highs and lows.

* **Genuine Breakout:** Price clears the zone with a strong full-bodied close, expanding volume, and holds above the level on subsequent retests.
* **Liquidity Sweep (False Breakout / Stop-Run):** Price pokes briefly above resistance (or below support), triggers retail breakout orders and resting stops, and then snaps back inside the prior range with a long rejection wick.
* **Execution Filter:** Waiting for the **candle close** beyond the zone (on your primary timeframe) rather than placing blind market stop-orders right on the boundary filters out most liquidity traps.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

* Treat support and resistance as **zones**, not single exact decimals, to account for intrabar wicks and order spread.
* The **polarity flip** (old resistance acting as new support) offers structured, lower-risk retest entries after confirmed breakouts.
* Always watch **how price closes** around a key level—sharp intrabar spikes that close back inside the range often signal a liquidity sweep rather than a true breakout.
