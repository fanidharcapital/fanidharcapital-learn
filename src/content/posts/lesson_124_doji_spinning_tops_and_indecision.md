In **Lesson #123**, we looked at candles where one side dominated the session—buying tails, selling wicks, and Marubozu bars. In **Lesson #124**, we examine the exact opposite state: **equilibrium and indecision**. When neither buyers nor sellers win the session, the chart prints **Doji** and **Spinning Top** candles—subtle pauses that often precede sharp volatility expansion on NSE/BSE charts.

---

## 1. What Indecision Actually Means

A Doji or Spinning Top occurs when price moves up and down during the session, yet closes almost exactly where it opened ($\text{Close} \approx \text{Open}$):

*   **Not "Nothing Happened":** Equilibrium does not mean traders were asleep. It means aggressive buying and aggressive selling offset each other into a temporary stalemate.
*   **Coiling Energy:** Think of an indecision candle as a compressed spring. Trends do not reverse or accelerate out of nowhere—they usually pause first while liquidity changes hands.

---

## 2. The Four Core Doji Variations & Spinning Tops

| Candle Type | Visual Structure | Auction Story |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Standard Doji** | $\text{Open} = \text{Close}$ with compact, balanced upper and lower wicks | Tight equilibrium; low-volatility pause or rest inside a move. |
| **Long-Legged Doji** | $\text{Open} \approx \text{Close}$ with tall upper and lower shadows | High-volatility tug-of-war; both bulls and bears tested extremes and failed to hold them. |
| **Dragonfly Doji** | $\text{Open} \approx \text{Close} \approx \text{High}$ with a long lower shadow | Sellers drove price down sharply, buyers absorbed all supply and closed at the highs. Bullish bias at support. |
| **Gravestone Doji** | $\text{Open} \approx \text{Close} \approx \text{Low}$ with a long upper shadow | Buyers pushed price up sharply, sellers rejected the highs and closed at the lows. Bearish bias at resistance. |
| **Spinning Top** | Very small green or red real body centered between noticeable upper and lower wicks | Mild directional tilt, but overall hesitation—momentum has clearly slowed down. |

---

## 3. Why Location Separates Signal From Noise

The single biggest mistake new price-action traders make is shorting every Doji in an uptrend or buying every Doji in a downtrend. **An indecision candle is a warning light, never a standalone entry trigger.**

### A. Mid-Range Chop (Ignore It)
When Nifty or a stock is drifting sideways in the middle of a range with no nearby support or resistance, Dojis and Spinning Tops print constantly. Trading them there guarantees death by a thousand stop-outs.

### B. After an Extended Trend at a Key Level (Pay Attention)
*   **At Major Resistance After a Rally:** Three wide-range green candles push an NSE stock into a multi-month supply zone, followed by a **Long-Legged Doji** or **Gravestone Doji**. Buyers have hit a wall of limit sell orders.
*   **At Major Support After a Sell-Off:** Steady red candles drive price into a higher-timeframe demand zone, followed by a **Dragonfly Doji** or **Spinning Top**. Selling pressure is drying up.

---

## 4. The Two-Step Confirmation Rule on NSE / BSE

Never enter on the Doji close itself—wait for the market to reveal which side broke the stalemate:

1.  **Spot the Pause at Structure:** Identify a Doji or Spinning Top forming cleanly at a pre-marked support, resistance, or moving-average value zone.
2.  **Wait for Directional Resolution:**
    *   **Bullish Confirmation:** The next candle breaks and closes **above the Doji's High**.
    *   **Bearish Confirmation:** The next candle breaks and closes **below the Doji's Low**.
3.  **Anchor Your Stop-Loss:** Place your invalidation stop just beyond the opposite extreme of the Doji (below the Doji low for longs; above the Doji high for shorts).

---

## 5. Key Takeaways

*   **Dojis** ($\text{Open} \approx \text{Close}$) and **Spinning Tops** (tiny real bodies) reflect buyer–seller equilibrium and momentum deceleration.
*   **Dragonfly** and **Gravestone** Dojis carry built-in directional rejection, whereas **Long-Legged Dojis** show intense two-way volatility.
*   Indecision candles only matter **at key structural levels after a directional move**—and always require the next candle to break their High or Low for confirmation.
