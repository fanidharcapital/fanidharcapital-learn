Welcome to **Lesson 79** of **Module 03: Fundamental Analysis** (**Lessons 61–90**) in the **Shree Fanidhar Capital Educational Series**.

In **Lesson 78**, we examined **Cash Flow Quality Ratios** (`CFO / EBITDA` and `FCF / PAT` Conversion). Once a business generates reliable cash flow, management must decide how much cash to retain for operations and how much to return to shareholders. Today, we focus on **Dividend Metrics**—**Dividend Yield**, **Dividend Payout Ratio**, and **Share Buybacks**.

---

## 1. Core Shareholder Cash-Return Metrics

| Metric | Formula | What It Measures | Key Analytical Nuance |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Dividend Yield** | $\frac{\text{Annual Dividend per Share}}{\text{Current Market Price per Share}} \times 100$ | Cash dividend return relative to today's share price. | A sharp jump in yield can come from a **falling share price** (price decline) rather than a dividend hike. |
| **2. Dividend Payout Ratio** | $\frac{\text{Total Dividends}}{\text{Profit After Tax (PAT)}} \times 100$ *(or $\frac{\text{DPS}}{\text{EPS}} \times 100$)* | Share of net earnings paid out as dividends (`100% − Payout Ratio` is retained). | Very high payouts in capital-intensive firms may leave too little cash for maintenance capex or growth. |
| **3. Total Shareholder Yield** | $\frac{\text{Dividends Paid} + \text{Share Buybacks}}{\text{Market Capitalization}} \times 100$ | Combined cash returned to owners via both dividends and buybacks. | Captures companies that prefer buybacks over regular dividends to return excess cash. |

---

## 2. Worked Comparison: High-Yield Mature Firm vs. Balanced Reinvestor

Consider two companies with `EPS = ₹40` and `Free Cash Flow` comfortably covering cash returns:

| Metric | Company A (`High-Yield Utility / PSU`) | Company B (`Steady Compounder`) | Analytical Takeaway |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Current Share Price** | `₹250` | `₹1,000` | — |
| **Annual Dividend per Share (`DPS`)** | `₹20` | `₹12` | — |
| **Dividend Yield (`DPS / Price`)** | **`8.0%`** (`20 / 250`) | **`1.2%`** (`12 / 1,000`) | Higher current cash yield on Company A |
| **Dividend Payout Ratio (`DPS / EPS`)** | **`50.0%`** (`20 / 40`) | **`30.0%`** (`12 / 40`) | Company B retains `70%` of earnings for reinvestment |
| **Share Buybacks** | Minimal / Occasional | Periodic buybacks when cash builds | Check total cash returned alongside `FCF` |

---

## 3. Reading Dividends & Buybacks Without Falling Into Traps

1. **The High-Dividend-Yield Trap:** If a stock drops `40%` because earnings are deteriorating or debt is rising, its trailing **Dividend Yield** spikes mechanically—even if the next dividend is cut. Always check **sustainability from `CFO` and `FCF`**, not just trailing yield.
2. **Payout Ratio Must Fit Business Economics:** Mature, cash-rich businesses with modest reinvestment needs can sustain higher payouts comfortably. High-`ROIC` growth businesses often keep payout ratios moderate so internal cash funds high-return expansion without heavy borrowing.
3. **Dividends vs. Buybacks:** Dividends distribute cash across all shareholders on the record date, whereas **share buybacks** repurchase and cancel shares—reducing share count (`EPS` rises if profits hold steady) and giving participating shareholders an exit while leaving non-participating holders with a larger ownership slice of the remaining business.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **Dividend Yield** shows cash dividend relative to current share price, while **Dividend Payout Ratio** shows the portion of earnings distributed rather than retained.
* **High dividend yield is not automatically a bargain**—always verify dividend sustainability against **Free Cash Flow (`FCF`)** and balance-sheet health.
* **Evaluating both dividends and share buybacks** gives a fuller picture of how management returns surplus cash to shareholders.
