## 1. What Are Swing Highs and Swing Lows?

Markets never move in straight vertical lines—they advance and decline in alternating **impulse legs** and **pullback legs**:

| Structural Pivot | Objective Definition | Market Auction Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Swing High (SH)** | A peak candle whose High is higher than the highs of at least **2 to 3 candles immediately to its left** and **2 to 3 candles immediately to its right**. | **Supply Inflection Point:** Buyers pushed price up until supply overwhelmed demand, forcing a measurable downward pullback. |
| **Swing Low (SL)** | A trough candle whose Low is lower than the lows of at least **2 to 3 candles immediately to its left** and **2 to 3 candles immediately to its right**. | **Demand Inflection Point:** Sellers drove price down until demand absorbed supply, triggering a measurable upward bounce. |

*   **Why the "Right-Side Candles" Rule Matters:** You cannot confirm a Swing High or Swing Low while the peak candle is still forming. Price must move away from the pivot for at least 2–3 completed bars to prove that a real turn occurred.

## 2. Major Structural Swings vs. Minor Intra-Leg Noise

New price-action traders often mark every tiny 1-bar pause as a swing point—creating cluttered charts and premature stop-outs. Separate **Structural Pivots** from **Intra-Leg Noise** using three filters:

### A. Visible Pullback Depth (Counter-Trend Displacement)
*   A valid **Swing High** should be followed by a visible downward pullback (typically retracing at least **25%–38%** of the prior upward leg, or spanning at least **3–5 candles**).
*   A single small red candle inside a strong 10-bar rally is a brief breath—not a major structural Swing High.

### B. Opposite-Color Candle Confirmation
*   Clean Swing Highs transition from green buying candles into a sequence of red selling candles.
*   Clean Swing Lows transition from red selling candles into a sequence of green buying candles.

### C. The "Squint Test" (2-Meter Chart Rule)
*   Zoom out to 100–150 candles on your NSE Daily or 15-minute chart and squint slightly. The obvious peaks and valleys that stand out immediately are your **Primary Structural Swings**. If you have to zoom in 400% to spot a pivot, it is noise.

## 3. Why Swing Points Are the Backbone of Price Action

Once you can mark Swing Highs and Swing Lows consistently, four essential trading decisions become mechanical:

1.  **Trend Identification (Lesson #130):** Comparing consecutive Swing Highs and Swing Lows tells you objectively whether an NSE stock is in an uptrend, downtrend, or sideways range.
2.  **Structure Breaks (Lesson #131):** A candle closing beyond a prior confirmed Swing High or Swing Low signals either trend continuation or an early shift in character.
3.  **Support & Resistance Zones (Lesson #132):** Horizontal supply and demand zones originate directly from major Swing Highs and Swing Lows.
4.  **Structural Stop-Loss Placement (Lesson #146):** Placing stops just beyond a confirmed Swing Low (for longs) or Swing High (for shorts) anchors your risk to genuine market invalidation rather than arbitrary Rupee amounts.

## 4. Practical Application on NSE / BSE

1.  **Keep Timeframe Consistency:** Mark your structural swings on the **same timeframe** you are analyzing. A Swing Low on a 5-minute Nifty chart is simply an intra-bar wick on a Daily chart.
2.  **Include Wicks in Swing Extremes:** Always measure the Swing High from the **highest wick tip** and the Swing Low from the **lowest wick tip**—that exact price is where opposing institutional orders actually defended the level.
3.  **Practice Tracing Zig-Zags:** On any NSE large-cap Daily chart, connect confirmed Swing Lows to Swing Highs alternatingly (`SL → SH → SL → SH`). You will immediately see clean impulse waves versus corrective pullbacks.

---

## 5. Key Takeaways

*   A **Swing High** requires lower highs on both sides; a **Swing Low** requires higher lows on both sides (typically 2–3 bars left and right).
*   Filter out **intra-leg noise** by requiring visible counter-trend pullback depth and applying the "Squint Test."
*   Wick extremes define the exact price boundaries of every swing pivot—forming the raw coordinates for trends, zones, and stop-losses.
