## Introduction

Knowing how to read order books and place orders does not protect capital on its own. **Lesson 05** covers the practical mechanics of **risk management and position sizing**—helping market participants size trades sensibly, define exit thresholds in advance, and survive inevitable losing streaks in Indian equities.

## 1. Risk Fundamentals & Capital Preservation

Sustainable participation starts with protecting principal before chasing returns:

* **Capital Preservation First:** A 50% drawdown requires a 100% gain just to get back to breakeven. Keeping drawdowns contained preserves both capital and decision-making clarity.
* **Defined Risk Per Trade:** Instead of picking an arbitrary share count first, decide the maximum rupee loss you are willing to accept on a single trade if your thesis is invalidated.
* **Pre-Defined Stop-Loss:** Every trade benefits from an objective invalidation level—placed where market structure proves the setup wrong rather than at a random round number.

## 2. Position Sizing & Risk-Reward Math

Position sizing connects your rupee risk budget to the distance to your stop-loss:

* **Position Size Formula:**
  $$\text{Quantity} = \frac{\text{Rupee Risk Per Trade}}{\text{Entry Price} - \text{Stop-Loss Price}}$$
  *(For a short setup, use $\text{Stop-Loss Price} - \text{Entry Price}$ in the denominator.)*
* **Example in Indian Equities:** Suppose your trading capital is ₹5,00,000 and you cap risk at 1% (₹5,000) per trade. If you buy at ₹1,020 with a stop-loss at ₹995 (₹25 risk per share), your position size is $\frac{₹5,000}{₹25} = 200\text{ shares}$ (₹2,04,000 total position value, subject to margin and liquidity checks).
* **Risk-Reward & Portfolio Concentration:** Compare potential reward against risk before entering, and avoid stacking correlated positions across the same sector so one market shock hits multiple trades at once.

## 3. Execution Discipline in Practice

Good math only works when paired with consistent execution:

* **Avoid Moving Stops Wider:** Widening a stop-loss after entry turns a planned small loss into an unplanned larger one; if a trade is not working, honor the stop or reduce size.
* **Account for Costs & Volatility:** Factor in brokerage, statutory charges/taxes, slippage during fast moves, and stock volatility so a normal intraday wiggle does not knock out a wider swing plan.
* **Review & Iterate:** Log entry rationale, stop distance, position size, and outcome so rules stay objective rather than emotional.
