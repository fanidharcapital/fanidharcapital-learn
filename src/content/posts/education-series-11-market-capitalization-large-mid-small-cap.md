When investors talk about "blue-chip giants," "emerging mid-caps," or "high-growth small-caps," they are grouping companies by **Market Capitalization** (or **Market Cap**). 

Many beginners confuse a company's **share price** with its **size**—assuming a ₹50 stock is "small" and a ₹4,000 stock is "large." In reality, share price alone tells you nothing about a company's scale. Market capitalization measures the total market value of a company's equity.

---

## 1. What Is Market Capitalization?

**Market Capitalization** is the total value placed by the stock market on all outstanding equity shares of a listed company.

$$\text{Market Capitalization} = \text{Current Share Price} \times \text{Total Outstanding Equity Shares}$$

### Why Share Price Alone Can Mislead You
Consider two hypothetical companies:
* **Company A:** Share price is **₹4,000**, with **1 crore** shares outstanding → Market Cap = **₹4,000 crore**.
* **Company B:** Share price is **₹250**, with **80 crore** shares outstanding → Market Cap = **₹20,000 crore**.

Even though Company B's share price looks much lower, Company B is **five times larger** in total market value than Company A.

### Full Market Cap vs. Free-Float Market Cap
* **Full Market Capitalization:** Includes *all* issued equity shares—both promoter holdings and public shares. **SEBI uses Full Market Cap** to classify companies into Large, Mid, and Small Cap.
* **Free-Float Market Capitalization:** Counts *only* shares readily available for public trading (excluding locked-in promoter or government stakes). Major indices like **NIFTY 50** and **SENSEX** use Free-Float Market Cap to weight their constituent stocks.

---

## 2. How SEBI & AMFI Classify Large-Cap, Mid-Cap & Small-Cap

In India, **SEBI** defines market-cap categories by **company rank** rather than fixed rupee amounts. Why? Because as the overall stock market grows over time, fixed rupee cut-offs quickly become outdated.

Every six months (in **January** and **July**), the **Association of Mutual Funds in India (AMFI)** publishes the official classification list based on each listed company's **6-month average full market capitalization** across NSE, BSE, and MSEI:

* **Large-Cap Companies (Ranks 1st to 100th):** The top 100 listed companies in India by full market capitalization.
* **Mid-Cap Companies (Ranks 101st to 250th):** The next 150 listed companies ranked from 101 to 250.
* **Small-Cap Companies (Rank 251st onwards):** All remaining listed companies ranked 251 and below.

*(Note: Under AMFI's July 2026 review covering Jan–Jun 2026 data, the Large-Cap entry threshold stood around ₹1,06,300 crore and the Mid-Cap entry threshold around ₹33,500 crore—and these rupee cut-offs adjust every January and July as market values move.)*

---

## 3. Characteristics & Trade-Offs Across Categories

| Parameter | Large-Cap (Rank 1–100) | Mid-Cap (Rank 101–250) | Small-Cap (Rank 251+) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Business Profile** | Established market leaders with proven cash flows and strong balance sheets. | Growing challenger companies scaling market share or expanding nationally. | Emerging, niche, or regional businesses in earlier expansion stages. |
| **Volatility** | **Lower** relative to broader equities; falls less sharply in market corrections. | **Moderate to High**; sharper swings during earnings misses or rate cycles. | **Highest**; steep rallies in bull runs and deep drawdowns in bear phases. |
| **Trading Liquidity** | **Very High**; tight Bid-Ask spreads and high daily turnover. | **Moderate**; generally liquid, though impact cost rises on large orders. | **Lower**; thin order books and wider spreads during market stress. |
| **Analyst & Institutional Coverage** | Heavily tracked by domestic & foreign institutions (DIIs & FPIs). | Moderately tracked; room for valuation re-rating as coverage grows. | Under-researched; higher information asymmetry and governance risk. |
| **Key Benchmark Indices** | NIFTY 50, NIFTY Next 50, BSE Sensex | NIFTY Midcap 150, BSE Midcap | NIFTY Smallcap 250, BSE Smallcap |

## 4. Why Classification Matters for Investors & Mutual Funds

1. **Mutual Fund Portfolio Rules:** SEBI requires equity mutual funds to follow strict category mandates—for example, a **Large-Cap Fund** must invest at least **80%** of its assets in top-100 stocks, while **Mid-Cap** and **Small-Cap Funds** must hold at least **65%** in their respective buckets.
2. **Half-Yearly Rebalancing Flows:** When AMFI updates its list every January and July, stocks upgraded from Mid-Cap to Large-Cap (or downgraded from Large-Cap to Mid-Cap) often see institutional buying or selling as funds rebalance within SEBI's prescribed window.
3. **Portfolio Role & Time Horizon:**
   * **Large-Caps** typically act as the **core stability anchor** of an equity portfolio.
   * **Mid-Caps** serve as the **growth engine**, blending scalability with established operations.
   * **Small-Caps** offer **long-term wealth creation potential** if selected carefully, but demand a longer horizon (7+ years) and higher tolerance for sharp drawdowns.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **Market cap measures company size, not share price:** Always multiply `Share Price × Total Shares` rather than judging a stock as "cheap" or "expensive" by its per-share price.
* **SEBI classifies by rank, updated twice a year:** Top 100 = Large-Cap, 101–250 = Mid-Cap, and 251+ = Small-Cap based on 6-month average full market capitalization.
* **Size is not a guarantee of safety:** Large-caps offer superior liquidity and resilience, while mid- and small-caps carry higher growth potential alongside sharper volatility and liquidity risk.
