Before picking any entry pattern, disciplined traders ask one question first: *Is the market making higher swings, lower swings, or moving sideways?*

## 1. The Three Market Regimes

Every liquid index or NSE equity operates in one of three structural states at any given timeframe:

| Market Regime | Swing Sequence | Institutional Behavior | Core Strategy Bias |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Uptrend (Bullish Structure)** | **Higher Highs (HH)** + **Higher Lows (HL)** | Institutions accumulate on dips; buyers defend prior swing lows before pushing to fresh highs | Buy pullbacks into HL zones or confirmed breakouts above prior HH |
| **Downtrend (Bearish Structure)** | **Lower Highs (LH)** + **Lower Lows (LL)** | Institutions distribute into rallies; sellers cap bounces below prior swing highs | Avoid catching falling knives; look for rallies into LH supply zones |
| **Sideways / Consolidation Range** | Equal highs and equal lows (`HH ≈ LH`, `HL ≈ LL`) | Equilibrium between buyers and sellers; accumulation or distribution base building | Trade range extremes or wait for a high-volume structural breakout |

## 2. Break of Structure (BOS) vs. Change of Character (CHoCH)

Understanding the difference between trend **continuation** and trend **transition** prevents premature entries:

1. **Break of Structure (BOS — Continuation):**
   * In an **uptrend**, price pulls back to form a **Higher Low (HL)** and then closes decisively above the previous **Higher High (HH)**. That upward breach confirms trend health.
   * In a **downtrend**, price bounces to form a **Lower High (LH)** and then breaks below the prior **Lower Low (LL)**.

2. **Change of Character (CHoCH — Early Warning Shift):**
   * In an established **uptrend**, price fails to make a new HH and instead breaks *below* the most recent **Higher Low (HL)**.
   * This structural break warns that buyers failed to defend their last anchor—signaling a potential transition into a sideways range or a new downtrend once a **Lower High (LH)** forms.

### Indian Equities Practical Lens (Nifty 50 / Sector Leaders)
* During strong bull runs on NSE, leading stocks frequently pull back 5%–12% on low volume, form a **Higher Low** near a prior breakout zone or rising moving average, and then print a **BOS** above the prior swing high on expanding volume.
* Conversely, when a stock loses its last major Higher Low on heavy delivery volume, treat subsequent green candles as suspect until a new bullish structure rebuilds.

## 3. Trendlines & Trend Channels

* **Ascending Trendline:** Drawn by connecting at least **two (ideally three) Higher Lows** beneath an uptrend. A valid trendline acts as dynamic demand without cutting through candle bodies aggressively.
* **Descending Trendline:** Drawn by connecting at least **two (ideally three) Lower Highs** above a downtrend, marking dynamic overhead supply.
* **Parallel Trend Channel:** Constructed by projecting a parallel line across the opposite swings (`HH` line parallel to `HL` trendline).
  * **Channel Support (Lower Rail):** High-probability zone to look for long setups in an uptrend.
  * **Channel Resistance (Upper Rail):** Logical zone to book partial profits or tighten trailing stops—not where you chase fresh longs.
  * **Channel Overthrow / Acceleration:** When price punches *above* the upper rail of an ascending channel on surging volume, momentum is accelerating; when it fails to reach the upper rail and rolls over, trend momentum is weakening.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

* **Market structure comes first:** Never look for bullish candle setups in a clear `LH + LL` downtrend without higher-timeframe support.
* **An uptrend stays intact until its last Higher Low breaks**; a downtrend stays intact until its last Lower High is reclaimed.
* **Trend channels** frame both dynamic support and realistic upside targets so you avoid buying extended moves at channel resistance.
