## 1. What Is a Valuation Multiple?

A **valuation multiple** expresses a company’s market price relative to a fundamental yardstick—such as earnings, book value, operating cash profit, or earnings growth rate. Multiples let you compare how much the market is willing to pay for `₹1` of a company’s fundamentals across time and peers.

| Multiple | Formula | What It Measures | Best Fit & Key Limitation |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Price-to-Earnings (`P/E`)** | $\frac{\text{Share Price}}{\text{Earnings Per Share (EPS)}}$ *(or $\frac{\text{Market Cap}}{\text{PAT}}$)* | How many rupees investors pay today for `₹1` of annual net profit. | Works well for steady earners; **distorted** in cyclical peaks/troughs, loss-making firms, or one-time `PAT` spikes. |
| **Price-to-Book (`P/B`)** | $\frac{\text{Share Price}}{\text{Book Value Per Share}}$ *(or $\frac{\text{Market Cap}}{\text{Net Worth}}$)* | How much market value is assigned above or below accounting net worth. | Useful for **banks, NBFCs, and asset-heavy/cyclical firms**; less intuitive for asset-light tech/services with unrecorded intangibles. |
| **`EV / EBITDA`** | $\frac{\text{Enterprise Value (EV)}}{\text{EBITDA}}$ | Total value of the entire business (equity + net debt) relative to core operating cash profit. | **Capital-structure neutral**—ideal for comparing companies with different debt/cash levels or heavy `D&A`. |
| **`PEG` Ratio** | $\frac{\text{P/E Ratio}}{\text{Expected Annual EPS Growth Rate (\%)}}$ | Evaluates `P/E` relative to expected earnings growth (`g`). | Helps spot growth at a reasonable price; **sensitive to optimistic growth forecasts** and ignores `ROIC` or cash flow quality. |

---

## 2. Enterprise Value (`EV`): Why `EV / EBITDA` Goes Beyond Market Cap

**Market Capitalization** only measures what equity shareholders own (`Share Price × Total Shares`). If a company carries heavy debt or sits on a huge cash reserve, looking at `P/E` or `Market Cap` alone misses part of the picture.

**Enterprise Value (`EV`)** represents the theoretical cost to acquire the entire operating business (paying off debt and claiming cash):

$$\text{Enterprise Value (EV)} = \text{Market Cap} + \text{Total Debt} + \text{Minority Interest \& Preferred Stock} - \text{Cash \& Cash Equivalents}$$

* Because **`EBITDA`** sits *before* interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization, pairing **`EV`** (which includes debt and excludes cash) with **`EBITDA`** creates an **apples-to-apples operating multiple** across companies with different borrowing levels.

---

## 3. Worked Comparison: `P/E` vs. `EV / EBITDA` (`₹ Crore`)

Consider two companies with identical **Market Cap (`₹2,400 Cr`)** and **EBITDA (`₹300 Cr`)**, but different debt and cash profiles:

| Metric | Debt-Free / Cash-Rich Firm (`₹ Cr`) | Leveraged Firm (`₹ Cr`) | Analytical Insight |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Market Cap** | `₹2,400` | `₹2,400` | Same headline market cap |
| **Total Debt** | `₹50` | `₹1,200` | Leveraged firm carries `₹1,200 Cr` debt |
| **Cash & Liquid Investments** | `₹450` | `₹100` | Debt-free firm holds `₹450 Cr` cash |
| **Enterprise Value (`EV`)** | **`₹2,000`** | **`₹3,500`** | True business value differs by `₹1,500 Cr`! |
| **`EBITDA`** | `₹300` | `₹300` | Same operating cash profit |
| **`EV / EBITDA` Multiple** | **`6.7x`** | **`11.7x`** | Leveraged firm is far costlier on an `EV` basis |

## 4. Reading `P/E`, `P/B`, and `PEG` Without Falling Into Traps

1. **The Cyclical `P/E` Trap:** In cyclical sectors (metals, cement, commodities, shipping), `P/E` often looks **artificially low at the peak** of the cycle (because `EPS` is temporarily huge) and **artificially high at the trough** (because `EPS` is depressed). Always evaluate cyclical earnings across a full cycle.
2. **`P/B` Is Anchored by `ROE`:** A high `P/B` (say `6x–8x`) is justified only when a business earns a consistently high `ROE` / `ROIC` well above its cost of capital. A low `P/B` (`0.7x`) with a `5%` `ROE` can be a permanent **value trap** if capital destroys value rather than compounding it.
3. **`PEG` Ratio Sanity Check:** A `PEG` around **`1.0x` or lower** is often cited as a rough benchmark for reasonable growth valuation—provided the projected `EPS` growth is realistic, backed by cash flows (`CFO`), and earned at high `ROIC`.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **Valuation multiples (`P/E`, `P/B`, `EV/EBITDA`, `PEG`) measure what you pay relative to a specific financial anchor**—no single multiple works universally across every sector.
* **`EV / EBITDA` accounts for debt and cash**, making it stronger than `P/E` alone when comparing companies with different capital structures.
* **Always pair multiples with business quality (`ROIC`, `ROE`, `CFO`) and cycle context** so a "cheap" multiple isn't a value trap and a "high" multiple isn't misread without growth and moat context.
