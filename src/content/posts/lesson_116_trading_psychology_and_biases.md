## 1. Four High-Cost Traps in Technical Execution

Technical analysis gives you objective zones, triggers, and invalidation levels—but stress, boredom, or urgency can easily override your plan:

| Trap / Bias | What It Looks Like in Action | Typical Chart Mistake | Practical Guardrail |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **FOMO (Fear of Missing Out)** | Seeing a green candle spike or social chatter and jumping in late without a setup | Chasing extended breakouts far above support or moving averages with wide stops | Require a **pre-defined trigger and stop distance**; if price runs away without you, let the next setup come |
| **Revenge Trading** | Taking an impulsive, oversized trade right after a stop-out to "win back" the loss immediately | Ignoring invalidation structure, skipping position-size math, or trading lower-quality setups | Enforce a **mandatory 15–30 minute cool-off** (or stop trading for the session) after hitting a loss limit |
| **Overtrading** | Firing trade after trade out of restlessness, boredom, or trying to force action in a flat market | Taking marginal setups inside choppy ranges or between key structural levels | Set a **daily/weekly trade cap** and require a full checklist match before every order |
| **Confirmation Bias** | Highlighting only bullish clues while ignoring bearish structure, resistance walls, or warning signals | Drawing biased trendlines, dismissing a clear breakdown, or refusing to cut a broken thesis | Actively ask **"What proves this trade wrong?"** before entry and honor your stop-loss |

## 2. How Emotions Corrupt Good Technical Rules

Notice how each bias attacks a specific link in the execution chain you learned in Lessons #111–#115:

* **FOMO distorts Entry & R:R:** Entering late stretches your distance to stop-loss (`1R`) while shrinking remaining room to resistance—turning a `1:2.5` setup into a `1:0.7` chase.
* **Revenge trading destroys Position Sizing:** Doubling quantity after a loss turns routine variance into a sharp account drawdown.
* **Overtrading erodes Expectancy:** Taking low-edge, noisy trades adds transaction costs, taxes, STT/exchange charges, and slippage while dragging down your average trade expectancy.
* **Confirmation bias blinds you to Invalidation:** Treating a stop-loss as optional when a trade goes red turns small planned losses into outsized damage.

## 3. Pre-Trade Reset Routine for NSE / BSE Sessions

Build a brief pause between **seeing a move** and **placing an order**:

1. **Check Your Emotional State First:** Are you calm and objective, or feeling rushed, frustrated from a prior loss, or eager to make something happen? If you are agitated, step away from the screen for a few minutes before touching the order window.
2. **Verify the Setup on Paper Before Clicking:** Confirm higher-timeframe bias, structural level, trigger close, stop-loss invalidation, and exact rupee position size.
3. **Respect Hard Stop Limits:** If you hit your daily loss cap or a streak of consecutive stop-outs, close the terminal for the rest of the session—preserving mental clarity is part of preserving capital.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

* **FOMO, revenge trading, overtrading, and confirmation bias** are normal human tendencies, not character flaws—but unguarded, they undo strong technical analysis.
* Most severe drawdowns happen not from a single bad indicator, but from **breaking risk and sizing rules under emotional stress**.
* Simple friction—a checklist, fixed position-size math, and cooling-off rules—keeps execution aligned with your edge.
