![Case Study — ATR & Volatility-Adjusted Position Sizing Across Changing Ranges](/images/lessons/lesson-348.png)

When a stock or index moves from a quiet **20-point** daily swing to a **50-point** daily swing, trading the exact same quantity with a tight stop often gets you chopped out—while trading the same quantity with a wider stop doubles your rupee risk. **ATR-based sizing** solves both problems.

## Case Walkthrough: ₹6,000 Rupee Risk Budget Across Two Volatility Weeks

* **Quiet Week (14-Day ATR = ₹20; Stop = $1.5\times\text{ATR}$ = ₹30):**
  * Quantity = $₹6,000 \div ₹30 = \mathbf{200\text{ shares}}$.
* **Volatile Week (14-Day ATR = ₹50; Stop = $1.5\times\text{ATR}$ = ₹75):**
  * Quantity = $₹6,000 \div ₹75 = \mathbf{80\text{ shares}}$.

## Why This Adjustment Works

* In the volatile week, your stop has room to breathe outside normal daily noise, yet if the trade fails at the stop, **your loss is still capped at ₹6,000**.

## Key Execution Lessons

* Let **structural invalidation and ATR** set your stop distance, and let **your fixed rupee risk budget** determine your quantity.
* Never force a full-sized position into a wide-ATR move if it pushes your risk above plan.
