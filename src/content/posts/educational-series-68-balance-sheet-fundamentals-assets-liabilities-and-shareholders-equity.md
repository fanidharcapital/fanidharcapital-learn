Welcome to **Lesson 68** of **Module 03: Fundamental Analysis** (**Lessons 61–90**) in the **Shree Fanidhar Capital Educational Series**.

In **Lessons 66 and 67**, we examined the **Income Statement (`P&L`)**, which tracks revenues, costs, and profits over a period of time. Now we move to the second pillar of financial statements: the **Balance Sheet**, which acts as a **financial snapshot on a single date** (such as `31 March 2026`) showing what a company owns, what it owes, and how its assets are financed.

---

## 1. The Core Accounting Equation

Every Balance Sheet rests on a single identity that always balances:

$$\text{Assets} = \text{Shareholders’ Equity} + \text{Liabilities}$$

* **Assets** represent economic resources controlled by the business that are expected to provide future economic benefit.
* **Shareholders’ Equity + Liabilities** show the **sources of funding**—how much came from owners/retained profits versus external lenders and trade creditors.

> **Analytical Insight:** A company can survive a bad quarter on the `P&L`, but a weak or over-leveraged Balance Sheet can threaten survival during a credit crunch or demand downturn. **Balance sheet strength is a company's shock absorber.**

---

## 2. Assets: Non-Current vs. Current

Under `Ind AS` (Schedule III of the Companies Act, 2013), assets are split by how quickly they convert into cash or are consumed in operations (generally using a **12-month operating cycle** threshold):

| Asset Category | Key Sub-Items | What It Tells an Analyst |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Non-Current Assets** *(Long-Term, `>12 months`)* | **Property, Plant & Equipment (`PPE`)**, Capital Work-in-Progress (`CWIP`), Intangible Assets & Goodwill, Long-Term Investments, Deferred Tax Assets. | Shows physical/operational capacity (`PPE`), ongoing expansion (`CWIP`), and long-term strategic investments. Watch `CWIP` for stalled projects or cost overruns. |
| **Current Assets** *(Short-Term, `≤12 months`)* | **Inventories** (raw materials, WIP, finished goods), **Trade Receivables** (money owed by customers), **Cash & Cash Equivalents**, Short-Term Loans/Advances. | Reflects short-term liquidity and working capital health. Rapidly rising receivables or inventory outpacing sales growth warrants close scrutiny. |

---

## 3. Shareholders’ Equity & Liabilities: Sources of Capital

The right-hand side of the Balance Sheet details who has a claim on the business’s assets:

### A. Shareholders’ Equity *(Net Worth)*
* **Equity Share Capital:** Paid-up face value of shares issued (`Number of Shares × Face Value`, e.g., `₹1`, `₹2`, or `₹10`).
* **Other Equity (`Reserves & Surplus`):** Cumulative retained profits (`PAT` not paid out as dividends), securities premium, and other reserves.
* **Book Value (`Net Worth`):** `Equity Share Capital + Other Equity`—representing the accounting net value belonging to equity shareholders.

### B. Liabilities *(Obligations to Outsiders)*
* **Non-Current Liabilities:** Long-term borrowings (term loans, NCDs), long-term lease liabilities, and non-current provisions/deferred tax liabilities.
* **Current Liabilities:** Short-term borrowings (working capital lines, commercial paper), **Trade Payables** (amounts owed to suppliers), current lease liabilities, and short-term provisions.

## 4. Worked Example: Simplified Balance Sheet Snapshot (`₹ Crore`)

Consider **Narmada Precision Engineering Ltd.** as of `31 March 2026`:

| Assets (`₹ Cr`) | `FY26` | Equity & Liabilities (`₹ Cr`) | `FY26` |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :---: |
| **Non-Current Assets** | | **Shareholders’ Equity** | |
| Property, Plant & Equipment (`PPE`) | `₹320` | Equity Share Capital (`FV ₹2`) | `₹40` |
| Capital Work-in-Progress (`CWIP`) | `₹30` | Other Equity (`Reserves & Surplus`) | `₹460` |
| Other Non-Current Assets | `₹20` | **Total Equity (`Net Worth`)** | **`₹500`** |
| **Current Assets** | | **Non-Current Liabilities** | |
| Inventories | `₹140` | Long-Term Borrowings | `₹110` |
| Trade Receivables | `₹130` | Lease & Other Non-Current Liabilities | `₹20` |
| Cash & Bank Balances | `₹80` | **Current Liabilities** | |
| Other Current Assets | `₹20` | Short-Term Borrowings | `₹40` |
| | | Trade Payables & Other Current Liab. | `₹70` |
| **Total Assets** | **`₹740`** | **Total Equity & Liabilities** | **`₹740`** |

**Quick Snapshot Reading:**
* Total Debt (`₹110 Cr` long-term + `₹40 Cr` short-term = `₹150 Cr`) against `₹80 Cr` cash gives **Net Debt of `₹70 Cr`** and a modest **Debt-to-Equity ratio of `0.30x`** (`150 / 500`).
* Working capital items (`Inventories` + `Receivables` − `Payables`) are balanced and easy to track across quarters.

## 5. Five Practical Red Flags on a Balance Sheet

1. **Debt Growing Faster Than Equity & Operating Cash:** Repeated borrowing just to fund routine operations or pay dividends weakens solvency.
2. **Ballooning Trade Receivables:** Receivables growing much faster than revenue may signal aggressive sales booking, weak collection discipline, or customer distress.
3. **Swelling Inventory Without Demand:** Unsold finished goods or obsolete raw materials can turn into future write-downs.
4. **Large `CWIP` or Goodwill With Weak `ROCE`:** Heavy capital locked in unfinished projects or pricey acquisitions that fail to generate adequate returns.
5. **Hidden Off-Balance-Sheet Commitments:** Always cross-check the **Notes to Accounts** for corporate guarantees, letter-of-credit exposures, and contingent liabilities not shown in main totals.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **The Balance Sheet is a point-in-time snapshot** governed by $\text{Assets} = \text{Equity} + \text{Liabilities}$.
* **Assets split into Non-Current and Current**, revealing long-term productive capacity and short-term working-capital liquidity.
* **Always inspect debt alongside cash and `Net Worth`**, and check the **Notes to Accounts** for off-balance-sheet items and working-capital quality.
