While Lesson #93 focused on single-candle anatomy and rejection wicks, **Lesson #94** moves to **multi-candle price action patterns**. Reading two or more consecutive candles reveals how control transfers from one side to the other, how volatility compresses before an expansion move, and how trends digest gains before continuing.

## 1. Core Multi-Candle Price Action Setups

| Setup | Candle Structure | Market Psychology | Primary Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bullish / Bearish Engulfing** | A strong second candle whose real body completely overtakes the prior candle's real body in the opposite direction | Aggressive absorption of prior momentum; clear shift in short-term order flow | Reversal at support/resistance or momentum shift after a pullback |
| **Inside Bar** | A smaller candle whose High and Low stay completely inside the prior ("mother") candle's High and Low | Temporary volatility contraction, coiling, or pause after an impulse move | Breakout / breakdown continuation or coiled expansion setup |
| **Pin Bar (Multi-Candle Context)** | A candle with a pronounced rejection tail/wick at an extreme, paired with prior trend context and follow-through | Trap of late breakouts or breakdown sellers followed by swift mean reversion | Rejection reversal at key structural levels or trendline/zone retests |
| **Continuation Sequence** | A strong impulse candle followed by 2–4 small, orderly pullback/consolidation candles and a resumption candle | Profit-taking is orderly and absorbed without breaking market structure | Trend continuation with defined risk below the pullback swing |

## 2. Inside Bars & Volatility Contraction (NSE Practical Lens)

An **Inside Bar** shows a market catching its breath—range narrows, volume often cools off, and pressure builds toward a directional resolution:

* **With-Trend Inside Bar:** Forms after a strong bullish or bearish leg and breaks in the direction of the dominant trend. Often offers a tighter stop-loss (e.g., beyond the mother candle or inside bar extreme) than chasing an extended breakout.
* **Breakout vs. False Break ("Inside Bar Fakeout"):** Markets frequently sweep just past the inside bar high or low to trigger resting stops before reversing hard. Waiting for a clean close beyond the mother candle or confirming with the next session reduces whipsaws.

## 3. Engulfing Candles & Continuation Structures

### Engulfing Context
* A **Bullish Engulfing** at the end of a pullback into a rising trend or demand zone shows buyers stepping in with size and closing above the prior red candle's body.
* A **Bearish Engulfing** at a prior swing high or supply zone shows sellers overwhelming late buyers.
* Avoid treating a mid-range engulfing candle—where overhead resistance is immediately above—as a high-reward entry.

### Orderly Pullback & Continuation
* Healthy trends rarely move in a straight vertical line. Look for **strong expansion candles** alternating with **smaller, overlapping corrective candles** rather than wild, high-volatility whipsaws.
* Once price breaks out of a brief multi-candle pause in the trend direction, the prior pullback low (in an uptrend) gives a logical structural reference for risk management.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

* **Multi-candle patterns** clarify momentum transfer (Engulfing), volatility compression (Inside Bars), and orderly trend pauses (Continuation structures).
* **Inside bars** signal coiling and potential expansion—always pair the break direction with higher-timeframe trend alignment.
* **Location and risk-reward** determine trade quality far more than spotting a pattern on an isolated chart.
