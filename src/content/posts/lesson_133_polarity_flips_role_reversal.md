In **Lesson #132**, we mapped horizontal support and resistance as supply and demand zones. In **Lesson #133**, we examine one of the highest-probability structural phenomena in technical analysis: **Polarity Flips** (also known as **Role Reversal** or **Breakout–Retest**). When a heavily defended zone finally breaks with conviction, its market role inverts—former resistance becomes future support, and former support becomes future resistance.

## 1. The Order-Flow Psychology Behind Role Reversal

Polarity flips are not magic; they are the predictable result of **trapped traders and missed participants** reacting to the same price zone:

### A. Broken Resistance $\rightarrow$ New Support (Bullish Polarity Flip)
1.  **Trapped Short Sellers:** Traders who shorted at resistance (`₹850`) are now underwater as price closes decisively above `₹850`. When price pulls back to `₹850`, they buy back their shorts to exit at breakeven—creating fresh demand.
2.  **Missed Breakout Buyers:** Institutional and swing buyers who refused to chase the initial breakout spike wait patiently for price to return to `₹850` to initiate long positions at fair value.
3.  **Flipped Defending Buyers:** Traders who sold early at `₹850` realize the breakout is real and re-enter on the dip.

### B. Broken Support $\rightarrow$ New Resistance (Bearish Polarity Flip)
1.  **Trapped Longs:** Buyers who defended support (`₹600`) are now holding losing positions after a decisive breakdown. When price bounces back up to `₹600`, they sell to escape at breakeven—creating heavy overhead supply.
2.  **Breakdown Short Sellers:** Momentum sellers add to shorts on the relief bounce into the broken floor.

## 2. Anatomy of a High-Probability Polarity Flip

Not every broken line produces a clean flip. Look for four quality markers on NSE/BSE charts:

| Quality Filter | What to Verify on the Chart |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Multiple Prior Touches** | The original level rejected price at least **2–3 times** before breaking—proving major liquidity sat there. |
| **2. Decisive Breakout Displacement** | Price broke through the zone on a **wide-range body close** with strong volume/delivery, moving meaningfully away (at least `1.5×–2× ATR`) before pulling back. |
| **3. Controlled, Low-Volume Retest** | Price drifts back toward the broken zone on **smaller, overlapping candles** rather than crashing back through it on wide red bars. |
| **4. Rejection Confirmation at the Flip Zone** | Price prints a Hammer, Buying Tail, Bullish Engulfing, or Inside Bar breakout right inside the flipped zone. |

---

## 3. Executing the Breakout–Retest on NSE / BSE

Trading the polarity flip solves the classic trader dilemma: *"If I buy the initial breakout, my stop is too wide; if I wait, the stock runs without me."*

1.  **Mark the Breakout Zone:** Highlight the `wick-to-body` band of the resistance ceiling that just broke.
2.  **Let the First Wave Go:** Do not chase the green breakout bar when it is already `5%–7%` above the base.
3.  **Enter on the Retest Trigger:** When price pulls back into the flipped zone and prints a bullish close, enter with your stop-loss placed **cleanly below the flipped zone** (giving you a compact `1R` stop and clear upside toward the next resistance).

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

*   **Polarity flips** occur when broken resistance transforms into support, or broken support transforms into resistance.
*   The flip is driven by **trapped traders exiting at breakeven** and **patient institutional orders** stepping in at the proven level.
*   Require **strong displacement away from the level** first, followed by a **calm retest and rejection candle** inside the flipped zone.
