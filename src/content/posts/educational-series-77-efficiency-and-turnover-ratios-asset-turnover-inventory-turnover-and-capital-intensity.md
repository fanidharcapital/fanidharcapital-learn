## 1. Core Efficiency & Turnover Ratios

Turnover ratios measure **how many times per year** a balance-sheet item turns over relative to sales or cost of goods sold—and can be converted into **days** to show how many days of activity sit tied up:

| Ratio | Formula | Days Equivalent | What It Measures |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **1. Total Asset Turnover** | $\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Average Total Assets}}$ | — | Overall revenue generated per rupee of total assets employed. |
| **2. Fixed Asset Turnover (`FAT`)** | $\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Average Net Fixed Assets}}$ | — | How productively plants, machinery, and property generate sales (**capital intensity** check). |
| **3. Inventory Turnover** | $\frac{\text{COGS}}{\text{Average Inventory}}$ | $\frac{365}{\text{Inventory Turnover}}$ *(Inventory Days)* | How fast stock moves off shelves/warehouses; slow turnover risks obsolescence or write-downs. |
| **4. Receivables Turnover** | $\frac{\text{Credit Sales (or Revenue)}}{\text{Average Trade Receivables}}$ | $\frac{365}{\text{Receivables Turnover}}$ *(DSO / Debtor Days)* | How quickly customers pay bills; rising debtor days can signal weak bargaining power or aggressive booking. |

*(Note: Pairing **Inventory Days + Debtor Days − Creditor Days** gives the **Cash Conversion Cycle (`CCC`)**, showing how many days of working capital must be financed.)*

## 3. Capital Intensity & Business Economics

**Capital intensity** describes how much upfront and ongoing capital (`Capex` + working capital) a business needs to grow sales:

* **Low Capital Intensity (`Asset-Light`):** Software/IT services, asset-light consumer brands, and fee-based platforms can scale revenue with modest `Capex`, often converting high margins directly into strong `Free Cash Flow`.
* **High Capital Intensity (`Asset-Heavy`):** Steel, cement, power generation, refining, and heavy engineering require large continuous `Capex` just to maintain or grow capacity. Even when `OPM` looks healthy, heavy depreciation and ongoing `Capex` mean **turnover and `ROIC` determine whether cash actually compounds**.

> **Analytical Trap to Avoid:** A sudden jump in **Inventory Turnover** or **Receivables Turnover** can look great on paper—but verify whether it came from true operational discipline or from **aggressive discounting / factoring receivables**, which can squeeze margins or shift credit risk.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **Turnover ratios measure speed and capital efficiency:** Higher asset, fixed-asset, and inventory turnovers mean less capital is trapped supporting each rupee of sales.
* **Convert turnover into Days (`365 / Turnover`)** alongside payables days to understand the working-capital cash cycle intuitively.
* **Always judge turnover and capital intensity against sector norms**—a `1.0x` asset turnover can be normal in heavy manufacturing, whereas a low turnover in retail or distribution is a major efficiency red flag.
