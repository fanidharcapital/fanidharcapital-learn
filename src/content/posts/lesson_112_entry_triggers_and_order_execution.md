## 1. Two Primary Entry Models: Breakouts vs. Pullbacks

Once your higher-timeframe bias and key levels are mapped, most technical setups fall into one of two execution styles:

| Entry Style | Best Market Condition | Trigger Signal | Key Risk / Pitfall | How to Manage It |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Breakout Entry** | Tight consolidation coil, triangle, or base at major resistance with expanding volume | Decisive candle close beyond boundary on strong volume (and/or retest hold) | Chasing extended gap-ups or low-volume false breakouts ("bull/bear traps") | Require volume confirmation; avoid chasing vertical candles far above structure |
| **Pullback Entry** | Established trend (`HH/HL` or `LH/LL`) with rising/falling moving averages or polarity flip | Retracement into support/EMA zone followed by a bullish reversal/continuation candle | Catching a falling knife if a supposed pullback is actually a trend reversal | Require a **trigger candle** showing buyers stepping back in—never buy blind mid-drop |

## 2. Order Types & Slippage Control on NSE / BSE

**Slippage** is the difference between the price you expect when placing an order and the actual average price at which your order executes. Slippage widens during fast openings (9:15–9:30 AM IST), illiquid mid/small-cap stocks, or thin order books:

* **Limit Order:** Specifies the **maximum price you are willing to pay** (for a buy) or the **minimum price you will accept** (for a sell). Protects against adverse price spikes, though your order may go partially or completely unfilled if price races away without pulling back to your limit.
* **Market Order:** Executes immediately at the best available opposing quotes in the order book. Guarantees execution speed, but exposes you to **slippage** during fast momentum bursts or wide bid-ask spreads.
* **Stop-Loss (SL) vs. Stop-Loss Market (SL-M) / Stop-Loss Limit:**
  * A **Stop-Limit** order triggers a limit order once the trigger price is hit—preventing terrible fill prices in fast gaps/drops, at the risk of not filling if price gaps straight past your limit band.
  * Understand your broker's order rules and circuit/volatility freezes on NSE/BSE so your risk management behaves predictably when volatility spikes.

## 3. Practical Execution Habits That Protect Your Fill Price

1. **Let the Trigger Candle Close:** Entering intrabar on a half-formed wick often traps traders in fakeouts that reverse before the candle closes. Waiting for a confirmed close (or a clean retest of a broken level) filters out noisy spikes.
2. **Watch Liquidity & Bid-Ask Spreads:** Liquid large-cap stocks and index derivatives generally have tight spreads and deep order books; lower-liquidity stocks can jump several ticks on a single mid-sized order.
3. **Avoid Emotional Market Chasing:** If a stock gaps up or spikes past your planned entry zone right at the open, forcing a market order inflates your stop-loss distance and ruins your risk-reward ratio. Let the setup come to you or pass cleanly.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

* **Breakout entries** capture fresh expansion out of bases; **pullback entries** buy orderly dips into support/moving averages once a trigger candle confirms demand.
* **Limit orders** control execution price; **market orders** prioritize immediate fills at the cost of potential **slippage** in fast or thin markets.
* Waiting for candle confirmation and respecting liquidity keeps entry prices disciplined and stop-loss distances realistic.
