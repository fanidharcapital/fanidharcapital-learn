## 1. What Is Basis?

**Basis** captures the price relationship between a **futures contract** and the **underlying spot/cash price** at a given moment:

*   **Futures Premium (`Futures > Spot`):** The futures contract trades above the underlying spot price (commonly observed when interest/financing carry exceeds expected dividend yield or when bullish/rollover demand pushes futures higher).
*   **Futures Discount (`Futures < Spot`):** The futures contract trades below the underlying spot price (commonly seen around ex-dividend expectations or when hedging/shorting pressure weighs on futures).

---

## 2. Convergence Toward Expiry

One of the most reliable rules in derivatives pricing is **convergence**:

*   As a futures contract approaches its **expiry date**, its remaining time to maturity shrinks toward zero.
*   At settlement/expiry, arbitrage forces and settlement rules anchor the futures price to the underlying spot/settlement price—so **basis converges toward zero** at expiry.
*   Even if a futures contract trades at a large premium or discount weeks earlier, that spread cannot persist indefinitely past expiry.

---

## 3. Practical Takeaways for Chart & Trade Reading

*   **Compare Futures vs. Spot Charts:** Always know whether you are reading a **futures chart** (which includes basis and rollover shifts) or a **spot/index chart** (which reflects pure underlying cash/index levels).
*   **Watch Expiry Convergence:** Do not expect a wide premium or discount to stay frozen into expiry week—basis adjusts as time runs out.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

*   **Basis** reflects the difference between **futures price** and **spot price** (premium vs. discount).
*   As expiry nears, **basis converges toward zero** as time remaining runs out and settlement/arbitrage anchors pricing.
*   Always check whether your levels and alerts are set on **futures** or **spot** so basis doesn't trick you.
