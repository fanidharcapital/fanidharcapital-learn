When spot moves steadily toward one side of a credit spread or iron condor, traders face a choice: **adjust within strict rules** or **take the planned exit**.

## Case Walkthrough: Spot Approaches the Short Call Spread

* **Setup:** A trader holds an iron condor on an index with defined wings on both sides. A morning rally pushes spot close to the short call strike while the put side is well out of the money and inexpensive to close.
* **Disciplined Option 1 (Roll Untested Put Side):** If rules permit and strikes stay cleanly separated without increasing max loss, closing the cheap put spread and re-selling closer to spot collects extra credit and offsets some call pressure.
* **Disciplined Option 2 (Exit at Hard Stop):** If spot breaks past the short call with strong momentum or the trade hits its pre-set rupee stop-loss, closing the position cleanly preserves capital without over-engineering.

## What to Avoid

* **Never Double Lots to "Fix" a Threatened Spread:** Adding extra short contracts to rescue a challenged position multiplies gamma and gap risk if the trend continues.

## Key Execution Lessons

* Adjustments should **stay inside your original risk budget**—never violate your hard stop-loss just to stay in a trade.
* Knowing when to **close and move on** is just as important as knowing how to roll.
