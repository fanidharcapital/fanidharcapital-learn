Welcome to **Lesson 76** of **Module 03: Fundamental Analysis** (**Lessons 61–90**) in the **Shree Fanidhar Capital Educational Series**.

In **Lesson 74**, we studied relative valuation multiples (`P/E`, `P/B`, `EV/EBITDA`, `PEG`), which tell us how a stock is priced *compared to peers*. Today, we explore **absolute valuation** through **Discounted Cash Flow (`DCF`)**—the foundational framework that values any business based on the present value of all future cash it can generate for its owners.

---

## 1. The Core Principle of `DCF` Valuation

A rupee received five years from today is worth less than a rupee in your hand today because of opportunity cost, inflation, and business uncertainty. Therefore, the **intrinsic value** of a business equals the **sum of all expected future Free Cash Flows (`FCF`), discounted back to today** at an appropriate risk-adjusted rate:

$$\text{Enterprise Value (EV)} = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{\text{FCFF}_t}{(1 + \text{WACC})^t} + \frac{\text{Terminal Value}_n}{(1 + \text{WACC})^n}$$

* **`FCFF` (Free Cash Flow to Firm):** Cash generated by operations after taxes, working-capital needs, and capital expenditure—available to *all* capital providers (both debt holders and equity shareholders).
* **`WACC` (Weighted Average Cost of Capital):** The blended minimum annual return demanded by equity and debt investors given the company’s risk profile.

---

## 2. The 4-Step `DCF` Valuation Roadmap

| Step | What You Estimate | Formula / Practical Method | Key Analytical Guardrail |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Project Future Free Cash Flows (`5–10 Years`)** | Explicit annual `FCFF` during the company's visible growth runway. | $\text{FCFF} = \text{EBIT}(1 - t) + \text{D\&A} - \text{Capex} - \Delta\text{NWC}$ | Anchor revenue growth and margins to historical `ROIC` and industry size—avoid hockey-stick assumptions. |
| **2. Determine Discount Rate (`WACC`)** | Opportunity cost of capital reflecting business and financial risk. | $\text{WACC} = (W_e \times K_e) + \bigl(W_d \times K_d (1 - t)\bigr)$ | In Indian equities, `WACC` typically falls around **`10.5%–13.5%`** depending on sector stability and leverage. |
| **3. Calculate Terminal Value (`TV`)** | Value of steady-state cash flows beyond the explicit forecast period (`Year n` onward). | $\text{TV}_n = \frac{\text{FCFF}_{n+1}}{\text{WACC} - g}$ *(Gordon Growth)* | Keep perpetual growth rate **`g` conservative (`3%–5%`)**—never higher than long-term nominal GDP growth. |
| **4. Discount to Present Value & Bridge to Equity** | Sum discounted `FCFF` + discounted `TV` to get `EV`, then adjust for cash and debt. | $\text{Intrinsic Equity Value} = \text{EV} + \text{Cash} - \text{Total Debt}$ | Divide by diluted share count for **Intrinsic Value per Share**, then apply a **`20%–30%` Margin of Safety**. |

## 3. Worked Example: 5-Year `DCF` Walkthrough (`₹ Crore`)

Suppose **Kaveri Consumer Appliances Ltd.** generates **`₹100 Cr` in `FCFF` today**, is expected to grow `FCFF` at **`12%` annually for 5 years**, and then settle into a conservative **`4%` perpetual growth rate (`g`)** with a **`11%` `WACC`**:

| Year | Projected `FCFF` (`₹ Cr`) | Discount Factor $\frac{1}{(1.11)^t}$ | Present Value (`₹ Cr`) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Year 1** | `₹112.0` | `0.9009` | `₹100.9` |
| **Year 2** | `₹125.4` | `0.8116` | `₹101.8` |
| **Year 3** | `₹140.5` | `0.7312` | `₹102.7` |
| **Year 4** | `₹157.4` | `0.6587` | `₹103.7` |
| **Year 5** | `₹176.2` | `0.5935` | `₹104.6` |
| **Sum of Present Value of 5-Year `FCFF`** | | | **`₹513.7`** |

**Step 3 — Terminal Value at End of Year 5:**
$$\text{FCFF}_6 = 176.2 \times 1.04 = ₹183.2\text{ Cr}$$
$$\text{Terminal Value}_5 = \frac{183.2}{0.11 - 0.04} = \frac{183.2}{0.07} = ₹2,617.1\text{ Cr}$$
$$\text{Present Value of Terminal Value} = 2,617.1 \times 0.5935 = ₹1,553.2\text{ Cr}$$

**Step 4 — Enterprise Value to Intrinsic Value per Share:**
* **Estimated Enterprise Value (`EV`):** `₹513.7 + ₹1,553.2 = ₹2,066.9 Cr`
* **Add Cash (`₹180 Cr`) & Less Debt (`₹46.9 Cr`):** **Intrinsic Equity Value = `₹2,200 Cr`**
* If the company has **`10 Crore` shares**, **Intrinsic Value = `₹220` per share** (offering a `25%` Margin of Safety at a market price of `₹165`).

---

## 4. Strengths & Pitfalls of `DCF` Modeling

* **Why `DCF` Is Indispensable:** It forces you to think explicitly about **cash generation (`FCFF`), reinvestment (`Capex` + `NWC`), capital efficiency (`ROIC`), and risk (`WACC`)** rather than relying on market mood.
* **The "Garbage In, Garbage Out" Trap:** Because **60%–75% of a `DCF` valuation often sits inside Terminal Value**, tiny tweaks to `WACC` (`10.5%` vs. `12.0%`) or terminal growth `g` (`3.5%` vs. `5.0%`) can swing intrinsic value by `30%–40%`.
* **Best Practice — Use a Sensitivity Range, Not a Single Number:** Always test **Bear, Base, and Bull cases** across `WACC` and growth rates so you arrive at a **valuation range** (e.g., `₹195–₹245`) rather than false precision.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **`DCF` values a business by discounting projected Free Cash Flows (`FCFF`) and Terminal Value back to today** using the risk-adjusted **`WACC`**.
* **Bridge from `Enterprise Value` to `Equity Value`** by adding cash and subtracting total debt before dividing by share count.
* **Keep terminal growth conservative (`3%–5%`) and build Bear/Base/Bull sensitivity ranges**—`DCF` is best used as a disciplined thinking framework paired with a **Margin of Safety**.
