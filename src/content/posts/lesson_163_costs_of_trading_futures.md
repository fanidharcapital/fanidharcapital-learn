In **Lesson #163**, we wrap up **Part 2** by looking at **the friction costs of trading futures**: **brokerage, Securities Transaction Tax (STT), exchange transaction charges, stamp duty, GST, and slippage**. In leveraged, frequent, or tight-target trading, ignoring frictional costs can turn a strategy that looks positive on paper into a net loser in your ledger.

## 1. Explicit Statutory & Execution Charges

When you buy and sell futures contracts on NSE or BSE, round-trip costs typically include:

1.  **Brokerage:** Flat per-order or percentage/per-lot fee charged by your broker for executing buy and sell orders.
2.  **Securities Transaction Tax (STT):** Statutory tax levied on futures transactions as prescribed under Indian tax/finance rules.
3.  **Exchange Transaction Charges:** Fees charged by the exchange (NSE/BSE) based on turnover/value traded (and any applicable clearing/SEBI turnover fees).
4.  **Stamp Duty:** Statutory duty applicable on buy-side transactions per prevailing state/central rules.
5.  **GST:** Goods and Services Tax applicable on brokerage and eligible exchange/transaction charges.

*(Always check current statutory rates and your broker's fee schedule—rates can be revised in Union Budgets or exchange/regulatory circulars.)*

---

## 2. Implicit Cost: Slippage

Unlike fixed or turnover-based charges, **slippage** is variable and often larger than people expect:

*   **What Is Slippage?** The difference between the **expected execution price** (such as your stop-loss trigger or breakout level) and the **actual average fill price**.
*   **When It Spikes:** Fast momentum bursts, opening gaps, illiquid strikes/months, or using market orders in thin order books can widen slippage noticeably.

---

## 3. Impact on Net Expectancy & `R`-Multiples

Suppose a trade has a planned `1R` stop of `₹2,000` and a `1:2` target (`+₹4,000` gross), and round-trip costs + slippage equal `₹450`:

*   **On a Winning Trade:** Gross `+₹4,000` becomes `~₹3,550` net (`~1.775R` instead of `2.0R`).
*   **On a Losing Trade:** Gross `-₹2,000` becomes `~-₹2,450` net (`-1.225R` instead of `-1.0R`).
*   Over dozens or hundreds of trades, overtrading low-reward setups stacks fixed/slippage drag against your edge.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

*   Futures trading costs include **brokerage, STT, exchange/regulatory charges, stamp duty, GST**, plus **slippage**.
*   **Slippage** varies with volatility, liquidity, and order type, and can easily exceed statutory charges on fast moves.
*   Evaluate your performance on a **net basis** and avoid overtrading setups with thin reward-to-risk.
