## 1. Price Relative Strength vs. Oscillator RSI

In technical analysis, **Relative Strength (RS)** compares how one stock or sector performs **relative to a benchmark** (such as Nifty 50, Nifty Bank, Nifty IT, Nifty Auto, Nifty Pharma, or Nifty PSU Bank)—which is completely different from **RSI** (Lesson #103), which measures internal momentum speed of a single instrument:

* **Outperforming (Strong RS):** When a stock or sector rises faster than Nifty 50 during up-legs—or **holds support and drops less** when Nifty pulls back—buyers are absorbing supply aggressively.
* **Underperforming (Weak RS):** When a stock bounces weakly on green index days and sells off sharply on red index days, institutional demand is absent or rotating elsewhere.
* **Ratio Chart (`Stock ÷ Benchmark`):** Plotting a ratio line (for example, a stock divided by Nifty 50, or a sector index divided by Nifty 50) lets you apply basic trend and breakout structure directly to relative performance:
  * A **rising ratio line** making Higher Highs and Higher Lows shows sustained outperformance.
  * A **ratio line breaking out of a multi-week base** often precedes or confirms strong absolute price leadership.

## 2. Sector Rotation Across Market Regimes

Institutional money rarely lifts every sector at the same pace simultaneously. Different NSE/BSE sectors tend to catch leadership at different stages of growth, liquidity, interest-rate, and earnings cycles:

| Market / Cycle Phase | Typical Leadership Dynamics | How to Apply on NSE / BSE |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Early Recovery / Liquidity Expansion** | Rate-sensitive, cyclical, and broad domestic growth themes often gain traction as confidence returns | Track which sectoral indices clear key moving averages and bases first with strong breadth |
| **Mid-Cycle Expansion / Earnings Broadening** | Capex, industrials, financials, consumer, or manufacturing pockets may take turns leading | Rotate watchlists toward sectors showing **rising RS ratio** and clean daily/weekly structures |
| **Late Cycle / Defensive or Volatile Shift** | Capital often rotates toward defensive or cash-flow-resilient pockets when volatility rises | Reduce exposure to stretched high-beta laggards; favor strong RS setups with disciplined risk (`1R`) |

*Note: Sector rotation is never a rigid mechanical clock—always verify current price structure, RS ratio trend, and index breadth rather than assuming past cycle order repeats identically.*

---

## 3. Practical Workflow: Pairing Sector RS With Stock Selection

1. **Rank Sectoral Indices First:** Check which NSE sectoral indices are trading above rising moving averages and outperforming **Nifty 50** on a ratio chart.
2. **Pick Leaders Inside Leading Sectors:** When a sector is in strong demand, its top-performing stocks often show **tight bases, clean polarity flips, and strong volume/delivery**—whereas bottom-ranked laggards in the same sector often carry broken charts or overhead supply.
3. **Avoid Buying "Cheap" Laggards Solely Because They Haven't Moved:** A stock lagging its sector index is often cheap for a structural reason; let price and RS confirm demand before committing capital.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

* **Relative Strength (RS)** compares a stock or sector against a benchmark like **Nifty 50**—distinguishing true market leaders from lagging stocks.
* A rising **Stock ÷ Benchmark** ratio signals outperformance during both rallies and market pullbacks.
* Combining **sectoral relative strength** with **clean chart structure** (Lessons #95–#113) keeps your focus where institutional flow is actively building.
