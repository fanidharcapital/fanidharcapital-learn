Watch any live ticker on **NSE** or **BSE** for five minutes and you will see prices ticking up and down continuously—even when nothing obvious is happening inside the company at that exact second.

To invest and trade with clarity, you need to separate **what moves a stock price minute-by-minute** from **what drives its value over quarters and years**.

---

## 1. The Immediate Engine: Demand and Supply in the Order Book

At any given second, a stock's price moves for one direct reason: **an imbalance between active buyers (Demand) and active sellers (Supply)** in the exchange order book.

* **When Demand Exceeds Supply:** Buyers are willing to lift higher **Ask** prices to get filled, pushing the Last Traded Price (LTP) upward.
* **When Supply Exceeds Demand:** Sellers are willing to hit lower **Bid** prices to exit, pushing the LTP downward.
* **Why Prices Move Even Without "New" Information:** Every buyer and seller enters the market with a different time horizon, liquidity need, valuation view, or risk limit. A mutual fund rebalancing its portfolio or a trader squaring off an intraday position creates real order flow even on a quiet news day.

---

## 2. Short-Term vs. Long-Term Price Drivers

Different forces dominate depending on the timeframe you are watching:

| Time Horizon | Primary Price Drivers | Examples in Indian Equities |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Intraday & Days** | Order flow, technical levels, news headlines, FII/DII daily activity, global cues | US market overnight moves, crude oil spikes, block deals, index rebalancing |
| **Weeks & Quarters** | Quarterly earnings, management guidance, sector rotation, interest rate & inflation expectations | Q1–Q4 profit growth, RBI repo rate policy, order-book wins, margin expansion |
| **Years & Decades** | Compounding earnings per share (EPS), Return on Equity (ROE), competitive moat, cash flows | Sustained revenue growth, market-share gains, disciplined capital allocation |

---

## 3. News vs. Expectations ("Priced In" Reality)

One of the most confusing moments for beginners is watching a company report **20% profit growth**—only for its stock price to **fall 5%** the next morning.

Markets are **forward-looking discounting machines**, not backward-looking scoreboards:

1. **Expectations Are Built Before the Announcement:** Analysts, institutional desks, and active investors continuously estimate future earnings, margins, and rate cuts—and buy or sell *ahead* of the actual event.
2. **Surprises Move Prices, Not Raw Headlines:**
   * **Positive Surprise:** Results or guidance beat consensus expectations → buyers re-rate the stock higher.
   * **Negative Surprise:** Profit grew 20%, *but* the market had already priced in 30% growth (or management lowered next-quarter guidance) → disappointed holders sell, pushing the price down.
3. **"Buy the Rumor, Sell the News":** When optimism has already pushed a stock up sharply before a known event (such as a budget, product launch, or earnings date), early buyers often book profits once the good news becomes official.

---

## 4. Major Catalysts That Shift Demand and Supply

* **Company-Specific Catalysts:** Quarterly financial results, order wins, capacity expansion, management changes, regulatory approvals, promoter pledge changes, or corporate governance issues.
* **Sector & Industry Triggers:** Raw material cost shifts (e.g., crude oil, metals, agro commodities), government policy incentives, export demand, or pricing wars.
* **Macroeconomic & Institutional Flows:** RBI monetary policy and interest rates, inflation data, GDP growth, currency (INR/USD) moves, and large buying or selling by **Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs)** and **Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs)**.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **Demand and supply set the tick; expectations set the direction:** Order-book imbalances move prices second-by-second, while changing expectations about future cash flows drive bigger trends.
* **Compare news against market expectations:** Good news only lifts a stock if it is *better than what the market had already priced in*.
* **Match your focus to your horizon:** Short-term price noise is driven by flows and sentiment, whereas long-term wealth creation tracks sustained earnings growth and business quality.
