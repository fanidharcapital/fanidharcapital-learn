In **Lesson #234**, we warned against impulsive over-tinkering. In **Lesson #235**, we look at structured **adjustments and rolling**—closing an existing leg or spread and simultaneously opening a new one at a **different strike** or **later expiry**—and how to tell when a roll makes sense versus when to **simply take the loss**.

![Educational Series #235: Adjusting & Rolling Option Positions](/images/lessons/lesson-235.png)

---

## 1. Two Core Rolling Techniques

*   **Rolling Out in Time (Same Strike, Next Expiry):** Closing a short option or credit spread in the expiring cycle and reopening it in the **next weekly/monthly expiry**—used when your directional or range thesis still holds, you can collect a **net credit** for the roll, and extra time (`DTE`) gives price room to stabilize.
*   **Rolling Strikes Up or Down (Same Expiry):**
    *   *Rolling the Untested Side (e.g., Iron Condor):* If spot rallies toward your short Call, you may roll your **untested short Put spread higher** to collect extra credit, cushioning total risk without expanding wing width.
    *   *Rolling the Tested Strike Away:* Shifting a challenged short strike further `OTM` for a net credit (often paired with rolling out in time) to widen your breakeven buffer.

---

## 2. Three Rules for Knowing When to Take the Loss Instead

1.  **Never Roll for a Net Debit That Breaches `1R`:** If rolling requires paying extra cash out of pocket or widening wings in a way that pushes worst-case loss past your **`1R` risk budget**, do not roll—close the trade.
2.  **Thesis Invalidation Means Exit, Not Adjustment:** If a support shelf breaks cleanly on high volume or a major macro/earnings catalyst changes the trend, rolling merely postpones and compounds the loss.
3.  **Watch Liquidity & Transaction Costs:** Rolling a 4-leg Iron Condor generates **8 leg executions**—if bid-ask spreads are wide or remaining credit is tiny, taking the clean stop-loss is far smarter.

---

## 3. Key Takeaways

*   **Rolling** shifts strikes or expiries to buy time or collect additional credit when your core market thesis remains intact.
*   If a roll fails to bring in a **net credit**, violates your **`1R` max-risk limit**, or fights an invalidated chart thesis, **take the planned loss and move on**.
