In **Lesson #245**, we examined confirmation bias and anchoring. In **Lesson #246**, we address **Recency Bias** and **Overconfidence**—how recent winning or losing streaks tempt traders to over-leverage on hot streaks or abandon valid rules on cold streaks.

![Educational Series #246: Recency Bias & Overconfidence](/images/lessons/lesson-246.png)

---

## 1. Win-Streak Overconfidence vs. Loss-Streak Recency Drift

*   **Overconfidence After Winning Streaks:** A few consecutive wins—especially in a favorable market phase—can create a false sense of invulnerability. Traders begin skipping filters, taking lower-grade setups, or **inflating lot size** right before a normal reversal or drawdown hits.
*   **Recency Drift After Losing Streaks:** Conversely, a cluster of `3–5` losses makes recent pain feel permanent. Traders may assume every breakout will fail or every range will snap, abandoning a sound strategy right when its favorable regime returns.

---

## 2. Keeping Rules Steady Across Normal Variance

*   **Streaks Are Normal Variance:** Even profitable strategies experience winning clusters and losing clusters—that is the nature of probabilistic markets.
*   **Lock In Guardrails:** Keep your **`1R` risk per trade**, **playbook filters**, and **stop-loss rules** steady across streaks. Any scaling up or down should follow **pre-planned review rules** over a meaningful sample—never impulse or mood.

---

## 3. Key Takeaways

*   **Overconfidence** after wins often leads to over-sizing and rule-skipping; **recency bias** after losses tempts you to abandon a valid edge.
*   Maintain consistent risk and execution discipline across both winning and losing streaks.
