Drawdown recovery is **non-linear**. Losing 10% of your capital does not require a 10% gain to get back to even—it requires an **11.1%** gain, and deep drawdowns demand massive climbs just to return to square one.

## The Drawdown Recovery Curve

| Capital Drawdown | Remaining Capital | Gain Required to Recover to Breakeven |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **-10%** | $90\%$ | **+11.1%** |
| **-20%** | $80\%$ | **+25.0%** |
| **-30%** | $70\%$ | **+42.9%** |
| **-50%** | $50\%$ | **+100.0%** |
| **-75%** | $25\%$ | **+300.0%** |

## Risk of Ruin & Behavioral Contagion

1. **Risk of Ruin:** The probability that a sequence of losing trades—combined with your risk per trade and leverage—erodes your account below a minimum viable operating level. Oversized bets during a losing streak dramatically elevate ruin risk.
2. **Decision Quality Degrades in Deep Drawdowns:** When an account falls 25%–40%, traders often become fearful on valid setups or reckless in trying to "win it back" quickly—compounding mathematical damage with emotional errors.

## Practical Rules to Keep Drawdowns Shallow

* **Cap Per-Trade and Daily Risk:** Keep standard risk per trade modest and enforce a hard daily/weekly stop so a bad week never becomes a career-ending drawdown.
* **Cut Size Automatically During Drawdowns:** If your account enters a defined drawdown tier (for example, down 8%–10% from peak), cut position size in half until consistency returns.

## Key Takeaways

* **Preventing a 40% loss is far easier than earning a 66.7% recovery gain.**
* Capital preservation and drawdown control are the foundation of staying in the game long enough for your edge to compound.
