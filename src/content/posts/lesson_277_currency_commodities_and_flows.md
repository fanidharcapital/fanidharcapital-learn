## 1. Uneven Sector Impact: Why One Macro Shift Helps Some and Hurts Others

Indian equities are not a single monolithic block—different sectors face opposite cost and flow dynamics when macro variables move:

*   **Crude Oil & Input Costs:** Rising crude oil pressures **oil-marketing/aviation/paint/chemical or logistics** input costs and inflation expectations, whereas upstream energy producers or energy-linked plays behave differently depending on pricing and realizations.
*   **`USD/INR` Movements:** Rupee depreciation can lift **export-oriented sectors** (such as `IT` services or select `pharma` exporters earning in foreign currency) on translation/competitiveness, while raising costs for **import-heavy businesses** and affecting foreign investor return math in dollar terms.
*   **Global Risk-On vs. Risk-Off:** In a **risk-on** global backdrop, investors often favor cyclicals, growth, and emerging-market equities; in a **risk-off** scare, capital frequently turns cautious or rotates toward defensive/domestic earnings leaders.

---

## 2. Check Relative Strength & Price Structure, Never Assume Uniformity

Even a textbook macro relationship (such as crude rising or `INR` slipping) can be overridden by domestic earnings, valuation, or global equity trends. Always check **sector relative strength and chart structure** on `NSE/BSE` rather than trading a macro story blindly.

---

## 3. Key Takeaways

*   **`USD/INR`, crude oil, and global risk appetite** affect Indian sectors differently—never assume every stock or index reacts identically.
*   Pair macro awareness with **sector relative strength and price structure** before entering trades.
