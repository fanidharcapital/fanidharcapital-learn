![Case Study — Institutional Absorption vs. Buying Exhaustion at Key Resistance](/images/lessons/lesson-334.png)

When price reaches a major daily resistance zone on high volume, traders often assume a breakout is imminent—or assume a top is guaranteed. Looking closely at **candle progress vs. volume** and **prior trend leg length** helps distinguish **absorption** from **exhaustion**.

## Case A: Supply Absorbing Buyers at Resistance

* **Observation:** Price pushes into multi-week resistance with heavy volume across several candles, yet each push stalls at almost the exact same ceiling with overlapping bodies and upper wicks.
* **Meaning:** Large resting supply is absorbing incoming buy orders; if buyers cannot clear the ceiling after repeated attempts, price often rotates down once demand cools.

## Case B: Buying Exhaustion After an Extended Run

* **Observation:** After a sharp 4-day rally, a stock gaps or surges into resistance with a wide-range bullish candle and huge volume spike, followed immediately by narrow, stalling candles and a lower-timeframe CHoCH.
* **Meaning:** Late momentum buyers rushed in at the top while early buyers took profits—leaving a vacuum that frequently triggers a multi-session pullback or consolidation.

## Key Execution Lessons

* **Measure Progress Relative to Effort:** High volume that fails to advance price past resistance is a yellow flag for breakout chasers.
* **Wait for Structural Confirmation Before Fading:** Let price show rejection or a lower-timeframe break rather than shorting a strong green candle mid-push.
