## 1. Anatomy of Bollinger Bands (Standard: 20, 2)

Bollinger Bands consist of three lines plotted directly over price:

| Band Component | Standard Formula | What It Represents | Analytical Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Upper Band** | $\text{20-SMA} + (2 \times \text{20-period Std. Dev.})$ | Two standard deviations above the rolling mean | Marks upper volatility boundary; acts as dynamic resistance in ranges or upside momentum gauge in trends |
| **Middle Band** | $\text{20-period Simple Moving Average (SMA)}$ | Baseline short-to-medium-term trend and mean | Acts as dynamic support/resistance and mean-reversion magnet |
| **Lower Band** | $\text{20-SMA} - (2 \times \text{20-period Std. Dev.})$ | Two standard deviations below the rolling mean | Marks lower volatility boundary; acts as dynamic support in ranges or downside pressure gauge |

Because band width scales with **standard deviation**, periods of quiet consolidation pull the bands inward, while sharp breakouts or earnings reactions push them wide apart.

---

## 2. Three Core Bollinger Band Behaviors

### A. The Bollinger Squeeze (Volatility Contraction Before Expansion)
* When a stock or index trades quietly inside a tight range, the upper and lower bands pinch inward toward the 20-SMA—known as a **Squeeze** (low-volatility regime).
* **The Trap:** A narrow squeeze tells you **volatility is compressed and an expansion move is brewing**, *not* which direction price will break.
* **Execution:** Wait for a decisive candle close outside the upper or lower band—ideally backed by volume expansion (Lesson #99) and horizontal structure alignment (Lesson #96)—before committing in the breakout direction.

### B. Band Walks (Strong Trending Momentum)
* A common retail error is assuming "touching the Upper Band means sell" or "touching the Lower Band means buy."
* During a powerful uptrend on NSE/BSE, price can repeatedly ride or "walk" along the **Upper Band** while the middle 20-SMA slopes steadily upward. Shorting simply because price tags the upper band during a band walk often fights strong institutional momentum.
* In strong trends, look for pullbacks toward the **Middle Band (20-SMA)** rather than fading every upper-band touch.

### C. Mean Reversion (Range-Bound Markets)
* When the 20-SMA is **flat** and price swings horizontally between support and resistance, price tends to test the **Lower Band** near support and revert toward the **Middle Band** or **Upper Band** near resistance.
* Mean-reversion entries work best when paired with rejection candles at clear horizontal range boundaries—and fail when a true breakout begins a band walk.

---

## 3. Indian Equities Practical Lens (NSE / BSE)

* **Multi-Day Coils Ahead of Catalysts:** Stocks consolidating into a tight band squeeze near a multi-week resistance level often deliver sharp directional expansion once volume returns.
* **Index vs. Single-Stock Nuance:** Broad indices like Nifty 50 or Bank Nifty frequently transition between narrow squeezes and multi-day trend legs; checking whether the 20-SMA is flat or sloping helps decide whether to apply **mean reversion** or **trend/band-walk** rules.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

* **Bollinger Bands** measure relative volatility through standard deviation around a **20-SMA**.
* A **Squeeze** signals low volatility and impending expansion—always wait for a confirmed directional breakout rather than guessing direction early.
* **Band walks** show trend strength (avoid fading strong trends at the outer band); **flat middle bands** favor mean reversion between range extremes.
