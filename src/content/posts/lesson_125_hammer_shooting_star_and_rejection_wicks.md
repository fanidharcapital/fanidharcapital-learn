## 1. Anatomy of Rejection Wicks

A rejection wick forms when price pushes aggressively into one side of the range during a session or bar, meets opposing liquidity, and snaps back before the close:

| Pattern | Body & Shadow Structure | What It Reveals |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hammer** (after a decline) | Compact real body near the top of the range; lower shadow at least $2\times$ the body size; minimal/no upper wick | Sellers drove price lower, but buyers stepped in with conviction to reject the lows and close near the top. |
| **Hanging Man** (after a rally) | Same shape as a Hammer, appearing after an upward move | Buyers still hold the top of the candle, yet the long lower drop warns that selling pressure beneath the surface is testing support. |
| **Shooting Star** (after a rally) | Compact real body near the bottom of the range; upper shadow at least $2\times$ the body size; minimal/no lower wick | Buyers pushed price higher into supply, but sellers rejected the highs and forced price back down to close near the bottom. |
| **Inverted Hammer** (after a decline) | Same shape as a Shooting Star, appearing at the bottom of a drop | Buyers probed higher during the session, showing early demand interest, though supply capped the close near the lows. |

*   **Body Color Note:** A green Hammer or red Shooting Star often carries slightly cleaner directional alignment, but **close position** and **structural context** carry far more weight than candle color alone.

---

## 2. Why Location Matters More Than Shape Alone

Two candles with identical OHLC geometry can have completely opposite probabilities depending on market context:

1.  **High-Probability Location (Key Structure + Prior Leg):**
    *   A **Hammer** printing right at a higher-timeframe support zone, rising moving average, or prior swing low after a multi-candle pullback shows trapped short-sellers and absorbed supply.
    *   A **Shooting Star** printing right at a clear resistance zone, supply shelf, or prior swing high after an extended push shows trapped breakout buyers and active distribution.
2.  **Low-Probability / Trap Location (Mid-Range or Into Strong Momentum):**
    *   A **Shooting Star** in the middle of a tight range with no overhead resistance often gets swallowed by the next green expansion candle.
    *   A **Hammer** during a vertical, news-driven breakdown into thin air is frequently just a brief breather before another leg lower.

---

## 3. Executing Rejection Wicks on NSE / BSE

1.  **Wait for the Close:** Never buy a Hammer at 10:30 AM or short a Shooting Star at 2:00 PM while the wick is still stretching. Judge only completed bars.
2.  **Require Confirmation or Clean Structure:** Many price-action traders wait for the next candle to break the Hammer's high (for longs) or the Shooting Star's low (for shorts), especially when trading counter-trend or near major inflection points.
3.  **Place Invalidation Beyond the Wick:** For a long off a Hammer, structural invalidation lies just below the Hammer's low (accounting for volatility/noise). If price breaks cleanly back below that low, the demand thesis is wrong.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

*   **Hammers** and **Shooting Stars** show sharp one-sided rejection—long lower wicks reflect demand stepping in; long upper wicks reflect supply pushing back.
*   **Location outranks pattern geometry:** A rejection wick at an established support or resistance zone carries far greater significance than one in mid-range chop.
*   Always wait for the candle to close and pair the wick with broader trend and structural context on NSE/BSE charts.
