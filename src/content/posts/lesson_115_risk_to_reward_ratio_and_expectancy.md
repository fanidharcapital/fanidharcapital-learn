In Lesson #114, you learned how fixed fractional position sizing protects your capital against losing streaks. In **Lesson #115**, you examine the other half of the trading equation: **Risk-to-Reward Ratio (`R:R`) & Mathematical Expectancy**—why a trader can lose more often than they win and still compound steadily, or win frequently yet bleed capital if winners are small and losers are wide.

---

## 1. Risk-to-Reward Ratio (`R:R`) Explained

Before entering a trade, **Risk-to-Reward Ratio** compares how much you stand to lose at your technical invalidation stop (`1R`) against how much realistic upside or downside exists to your logical target:

$$\text{Risk per Unit (1R)} = |\text{Entry Price} - \text{Stop-Loss Price}|$$

$$\text{Reward Potential} = |\text{Target Price} - \text{Entry Price}|$$

$$\text{Risk-to-Reward Ratio} = 1 : \left(\frac{\text{Reward Potential}}{\text{Risk per Unit}}\right)$$

* **Example (`1:2.5 R:R`):** You enter an NSE stock at `₹500`, place your invalidation stop at `₹480` (`₹20` risk = `1R`), and target a logical resistance/measured-move zone at `₹550` (`₹50` reward = `2.5R`). For every `₹1` risked, the setup offers `₹2.50` of potential reward.
* **Reality Check on Targets:** Never invent an unrealistic `1:5` target in the middle of a tight trading range just to force a pretty ratio. Anchor targets to **real structural resistance, measured moves, or higher-timeframe zones** (Lessons #96–#100, #111).

## 2. Mathematical Expectancy: Why Win Rate Alone Is Misleading

**Expectancy** measures the average amount you expect to make (or lose) **per rupee risked** across a large sample of trades:

$$\text{Expectancy (in R)} = (\text{Win Rate} \times \text{Average Win in R}) - (\text{Loss Rate} \times \text{Average Loss in R})$$

Look at how **different combinations of Win Rate and Payoff** produce wildly different results:

| System Profile | Win Rate ($W$) | Average Win | Average Loss | Expectancy per Trade | Long-Term Outcome |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Trend / Breakout System (`1:2.5` avg win)** | `40%` | `+2.5R` | `-1.0R` | $(0.40 \times 2.5) - (0.60 \times 1.0) = \mathbf{+0.40R}$ | **Positive edge:** Averages `+0.40R` per trade despite losing 60% of the time |
| **Mean-Reversion / Scalping System (`1:0.8` avg win)** | `70%` | `+0.8R` | `-1.0R` | $(0.70 \times 0.8) - (0.30 \times 1.0) = \mathbf{+0.26R}$ | **Positive edge:** High win rate works because losses are strictly capped at `1R` |
| **The "Small Wins, Huge Losses" Trap** | `65%` | `+0.6R` | `-2.5R` | $(0.65 \times 0.6) - (0.35 \times 2.5) = \mathbf{-0.485R}$ | **Negative edge:** Feels good on frequent small wins, but occasional big stop-outs wipe out gains |

## 3. Practical Rules for Evaluating Setups Before Entry

1. **Skip Low-R:R Chases:** If a stock has already rallied right up to a heavy resistance wall and only `₹12` of room remains before supply while your technical stop is `₹24` away (`1:0.5 R:R`), pass on the trade—even if the chart looks bullish.
2. **Distinguish Setup R:R From Realized R:R:** Your *planned* `R:R` at entry is a hypothesis; your *realized* `R:R` over 50–100 trades reflects trailing exits, partial profit-taking, slippage, and occasional rule-breaking.
3. **Evaluate Across Samples, Not Single Trades:** A single `1:3` trade can lose, and a single `1:1` trade can win. Judge your process over **blocks of 20–50 setups**, not today's single P&L print.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

* **Risk-to-Reward (`R:R`)** measures potential gain against technical stop distance (`1R`) before you commit capital.
* **Win rate alone never defines a system**—**Expectancy** combines win rate with average win and average loss size.
* Avoid setups where a wide stop faces an immediate resistance ceiling, and never let occasional uncontained losses erase many small wins.
