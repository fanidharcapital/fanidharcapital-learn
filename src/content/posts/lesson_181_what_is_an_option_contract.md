Welcome to **Module 07: Options Market (Lessons #181–#210)**! In **Module 06**, we mastered **futures**—where both buyer and seller carry an equal obligation to settle at the agreed futures price. Now, in **Lesson #181**, we introduce **Option Contracts**, where the payoff becomes **asymmetric**: the **option buyer** pays an upfront fee for a **right** (without obligation), while the **option seller (writer)** collects that fee and takes on a conditional **obligation**.

## 1. Futures vs. Options: Symmetry vs. Asymmetry

*   **Futures Contract:** Symmetric commitment. Both buyer and seller participate dollar-for-dollar (or rupee-for-rupee) in upside and downside moves.
*   **Option Contract:** Asymmetric contract.
    *   **Option Buyer (Holder):** Pays a non-refundable upfront **premium** for the **right, but not the obligation**, to buy or sell the underlying at a fixed price on/by expiry. Maximum loss is capped at the premium paid (plus charges).
    *   **Option Seller (Writer):** Receives the **premium** upfront and carries the **obligation** to fulfill the contract if exercised by the buyer. Maximum profit is capped at the premium collected, while potential loss can be substantial.

---

## 2. Five Core Terms Every Option Contract Has

1.  **Underlying Asset:** The index (such as Nifty or Sensex) or individual NSE/BSE F&O stock from which the option derives its value.
2.  **Option Type:** Either a **Call (`CE`)**—the right to buy—or a **Put (`PE`)**—the right to sell.
3.  **Strike Price:** The pre-agreed reference price at which the option right is anchored (e.g., Nifty `24,500` Strike).
4.  **Expiry Date:** The finite date and session close (`3:30 PM IST`) on which the contract expires and ceases to exist.
5.  **Premium & Lot Size:**
    *   **Premium:** The market price per unit/share paid by the buyer to the seller.
    *   **Total Contract Premium Paid/Received:** $\text{Option Premium} \times \text{Lot Size}$.

---

## 3. Why Traders Use Options

*   **Directional Leverage With Capped Buyer Risk:** Expressing a bullish or bearish view with known maximum premium outlay.
*   **Portfolio Hedging:** Protecting an existing stock or index portfolio against downside drops (like buying insurance).
*   **Income / Range-Bound Strategies:** Collecting time-decay premium when a trader expects price to stay below resistance or above support.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

*   An option gives the **buyer a right (not an obligation)** in exchange for paying an upfront **premium**, while the **seller receives the premium** and assumes the **obligation**.
*   Every option is defined by five parameters: **Underlying, Type (`CE`/`PE`), Strike Price, Expiry Date, and Premium (multiplied by Lot Size)**.
