## 1. Two-Candle Stories: Reversal vs. Absorption

A single candle shows one session's auction; a **two-candle sequence** reveals how the market *responded* to that auction on the very next period:

| Two-Candle Pattern | Visual Structure | Behavioral Story |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bullish Engulfing** (at support / after decline) | Candle 1: Red body.<br>Candle 2: Larger green real body that opens at or below Candle 1's close and closes above Candle 1's open. | **Immediate Momentum Flip:** Sellers controlled Candle 1, but buyers stepped in aggressively on Candle 2 and wiped out all prior selling progress. |
| **Bearish Engulfing** (at resistance / after rally) | Candle 1: Green body.<br>Candle 2: Larger red real body that opens at or above Candle 1's close and closes below Candle 1's open. | **Immediate Supply Overwhelm:** Buyers pushed into resistance on Candle 1, only for sellers to engulf the entire gain on Candle 2. |
| **Bullish Harami** (after decline) | Candle 1: Wide red body.<br>Candle 2: Small green (or Doji) body completely contained within Candle 1's real body. | **Selling Exhaustion & Absorption:** Downward momentum slammed into limit buy orders; sellers could no longer push price to new lows. |
| **Bearish Harami** (after rally) | Candle 1: Wide green body.<br>Candle 2: Small red (or Doji) body completely contained within Candle 1's real body. | **Buying Exhaustion & Compression:** Upward drive stalled inside the prior candle's range as supply quietly absorbed demand. |

*   **Terminology Note:** *Harami* is the classical Japanese candlestick term for an **inside real body** ("pregnant" shape)—conceptually related to the Inside Bar we will explore in Lesson #128, though Harami focuses specifically on **real body containment** rather than full high-to-low range containment.

## 2. Grading Quality: High-Conviction vs. Weak Two-Candle Signals

Not every overlapping pair of candles deserves capital. Filter for three quality markers:

1.  **Size Disparity:**
    *   In an **Engulfing** pattern, the stronger Candle 2's body is relative to Candle 1 (especially when it swallows 2–3 prior candles with elevated NSE delivery/volume), the stronger the shift in control.
    *   In a **Harami**, Candle 1 should be a noticeably wide expansion bar, and Candle 2 should be compact (ideally under 35% of Candle 1's body).
2.  **Structural Location:**
    *   Bullish Engulfing / Harami carries edge when forming at a **higher-timeframe support zone**, rising 20/50-EMA, or pullback swing low.
    *   Bearish Engulfing / Harami carries edge at **major horizontal resistance**, multi-week supply zones, or after an overextended vertical run.
3.  **Close Location on Candle 2:**
    *   A Bullish Engulfing candle that closes near its own high (minimal upper wick) is far stronger than one that leaves a tall selling wick at the top.

---

## 3. Execution Rules on NSE / BSE

*   **Trading Engulfing Patterns:** Because Candle 2 has already proved directional commitment by closing beyond Candle 1's body, traders either enter near the close/open of the next bar or wait for a shallow 30–50% pullback into Candle 2's body, placing the stop-loss just beyond the two-candle extreme.
*   **Trading Harami Patterns (Wait for the 3rd Candle):** Because a Harami represents *equilibrium/absorption* rather than an active reversal, **never enter on the Harami close alone**. Wait for Candle 3 to break and close above the Harami high (bullish confirmation) or below the Harami low (bearish confirmation).

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

*   **Engulfing patterns** represent an abrupt shift in control where the second candle's real body completely swallows the first.
*   **Harami patterns** represent deceleration and absorption, where a wide momentum bar is followed by a small contained body.
*   **Engulfing** signals immediate directional action; **Harami** signals compression and requires a third-candle breakout for confirmation.
