In Lesson #97, you learned how reversal patterns signal that an existing trend is exhausting itself. In **Lesson #98**, you will examine the opposite dynamic: **continuation chart patterns**. Strong trends rarely move in a straight vertical line—institutions pause to digest gains, absorb short-term profit-taking, and build energy before launching the next leg in the dominant trend direction.

## 1. Core Continuation Patterns at a Glance

Continuation patterns represent **temporary pauses and volatility contraction** inside an established trend. A textbook continuation setup shows **shrinking volume during the consolidation** followed by **expanding volume on the breakout**.

| Pattern | Visual Geometry | Structural Meaning | Breakout Confirmation & Target |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ascending Triangle (Bullish Bias)** | Flat horizontal resistance top + rising trendline of **Higher Lows** | Buyers step in at progressively higher prices while overhead supply sits at a fixed price ceiling | Decisive close **above** flat resistance; target = widest triangle height projected upward |
| **Descending Triangle (Bearish Bias)** | Flat horizontal support floor + falling trendline of **Lower Highs** | Sellers become increasingly aggressive on every bounce while testing a static demand floor | Decisive close **below** flat support; target = widest triangle height projected downward |
| **Symmetrical Triangle (Coiling)** | Converging lower highs and higher lows forming a tightening apex | Balanced coil where volatility compresses toward a directional expansion | Trade **only** in the direction of the confirmed close beyond trendline (ideally matching prior trend) |
| **Bull / Bear Flag** | Sharp, high-volume **Flagpole** followed by a tight, parallel, slightly counter-trend channel | Brief, orderly profit-taking after an explosive impulse move without giving back key gains | Breakout from the flag channel on volume; target = flagpole length projected from breakout |
| **Pennant** | Sharp **Flagpole** followed by a brief, compact converging mini-triangle | Rapid digestion of an impulse move over a shorter duration (typically 1–3 weeks on Daily charts) | Clean close beyond pennant boundary; target = flagpole height projected from breakout |
| **Rectangle (Trading Box)** | Parallel horizontal support and resistance boundaries bounding sideways swings | Institutional re-accumulation (in an uptrend) or re-distribution (in a downtrend) | Confirmed close outside box boundary; target = vertical box height projected from breakout |

## 2. The Three Rules of High-Probability Continuation Setups

1. **Prior Impulse Must Exist:**
   * A **Flag** or **Pennant** without a steep, high-volume flagpole preceding it is just random sideways drift. Look for a clear directional leg before the pause begins.
2. **Volume Should Dry Up Inside the Pause:**
   * Healthy consolidation shows declining volume and narrowing candle ranges—indicating that holders are not dumping shares aggressively.
   * When price finally clears the upper boundary (in an uptrend), volume should surge back above its 20-day average to confirm fresh institutional demand.
3. **Avoid Late-Apex Squeezes in Triangles:**
   * Breakouts occurring **two-thirds to three-quarters** of the way toward a triangle's apex tend to carry the strongest momentum. When price drifts all the way into the final tip of the apex, breakouts often fizzle into sideways chop.

## 3. Indian Equities Practical Lens (NSE / BSE)

* **Post-Earnings & Sector-Leader Flags:** On NSE mid-cap and large-cap leaders, an earnings gap-up or sector catalyst often creates a multi-day **Flagpole**, followed by a 5- to 12-session low-volume **Bull Flag** or **Rectangle** that respects the rising 10-EMA or 20-EMA before breaking out again.
* **Stop-Loss Placement:** Rather than placing a wide stop below the start of the entire flagpole, continuation traders anchor risk just below the **most recent Higher Low inside the triangle/flag** or below the midpoint of a tight rectangle box—keeping risk compact relative to the measured move target.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

* **Continuation patterns** are pauses that refresh an active trend through volatility contraction and supply absorption.
* **Ascending Triangles** and **Bull Flags** reflect buyer urgency (higher lows or shallow pullbacks) ahead of a breakout.
* **Volume contraction during the coil + volume expansion on the breakout** is the primary filter separating high-probability continuations from false breakouts.
