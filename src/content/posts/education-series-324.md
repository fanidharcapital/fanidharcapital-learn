Even a sound strategy breaks down when rules stay vague in your head. During live market hours, ambiguous criteria invite **discretionary drift**—widening stops "just a little," chasing late entries out of FOMO, or cutting winners early out of anxiety.

## Codifying the Four Parts of Every Setup

1. **Context & Eligibility Filter:** Specify the exact trend, volatility regime, or session window required before a setup is valid (e.g., *only trade pullbacks above rising VWAP and 20-EMA when India VIX < 16*).
2. **Objective Entry Trigger:** Define what price action or spread condition must occur to enter (e.g., *candle close above prior swing high with defined invalidation*).
3. **Hard Stop-Loss & Invalidation:** State the exact price or spread loss level where the trade thesis is proven wrong—and place the protective order immediately upon entry.
4. **Exit & Profit-Management Rules:** Pre-define target zones, trailing rules, or time-based exits (such as closing before expiry settlement or ahead of major macro announcements).

## Avoiding Mid-Trade Rule Drift

* **Separate "Improving Execution" From "Changing Rules Mid-Trade":** You can refine a playbook on weekends after reviewing data, but once a trade is live, follow your pre-planned exit rules.
* **Write Down One-Sentence "Do Not Trade" Conditions:** Explicitly listing when *not* to trade (such as illiquid spreads or right at an announcement print) prevents low-edge impulse trades.

## Key Takeaways

* Clear written rules reduce emotional friction and let you **measure whether losses come from the setup or from breaking your own rules**.
* A stop-loss is only effective if it is **pre-planned, objective, and honored**.
