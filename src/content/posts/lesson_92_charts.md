## 1. Core Chart Types

| Chart Type | Data Plotted | Strengths | Limitations | Best Use Case |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Line Chart** | Closing price connected over time | Strips intraday noise; highlights clean swing highs, lows, and long-term trendlines | Hides intraday volatility, wicks, gaps, and open/high/low range | Multi-year macro sweeps, clean line-breakouts, and multi-timeframe trend mapping |
| **OHLC Bar Chart** | Open, High, Low, Close (`Open` left tick, `Close` right tick) | Shows full price range and momentum within each bar | Visually denser for quick retail pattern scanning than candles | Institutional range analysis and volatility expansion/contraction reads |
| **Candlestick Chart** | Open, High, Low, Close with filled/hollow or colored body + wicks | Immediately shows session direction, rejection wicks, and buyer/seller control | Can create over-analysis on very low timeframes if taken out of context | Primary day-to-day execution, price action structure, and entry/exit timing |

### Indian Market Practical Note (NSE / BSE)
* On Indian equities and indices (such as Nifty 50, Bank Nifty, or liquid NSE cash stocks), **closing prices** carry particular weight because institutional funds, index rebalances, and daily settlement reference marks anchor to the close.
* If a stock spikes intraday during a volatile morning session on NSE but closes back inside its prior range on a line chart, switching to a line view helps confirm whether structural resistance actually broke.

## 2. Multi-Timeframe Alignment (Top-Down Approach)

Trading a single timeframe in isolation often leads to buying a breakout on a 15-minute chart directly into major overhead supply on a daily chart. Multi-timeframe analysis aligns **direction**, **setup**, and **execution**:

1. **Higher Timeframe (Trend & Key Zones):** Defines the primary directional bias and major support/resistance levels (e.g., **Weekly** or **Daily** for swing/positional trades; **Daily** or **1-Hour** for intraday structure).
2. **Intermediate Timeframe (Setup & Structure):** Identifies pullbacks, consolidations, or breakout structures within the higher-timeframe bias (e.g., **Daily** or **4-Hour / 1-Hour** for swing trades; **15-Minute** for intraday).
3. **Lower Timeframe (Precision Entry & Risk):** Refines entry triggers, stop-loss placement, and position sizing without widening risk unnecessarily (e.g., **1-Hour / 15-Minute** for swing trades; **5-Minute / 3-Minute** for intraday).

### Rule of Alignment
* **Aligned Timeframes:** Higher timeframe is trending up, intermediate timeframe pulls back cleanly to support, and lower timeframe shows buyers stepping back in. Probabilities favor continuation.
* **Conflicting Timeframes:** Higher timeframe is in a clear downtrend while a 5-minute chart shows a sharp bounce. Treat counter-trend moves as tactical bounces rather than trend reversals unless structure shifts on higher frames.

## 3. Matching Timeframes to Trading Style

* **Long-Term / Positional Investors:** Focus primarily on **Monthly** and **Weekly** charts, using the **Daily** chart only to time entries near support or post-earnings bases.
* **Swing Traders (Days to Weeks):** Anchor bias on **Weekly** and **Daily** charts, and look for setups/entries on **Daily** and **1-Hour** charts.
* **Intraday Traders:** Anchor bias on **Daily** and **1-Hour** charts, refine structure on **15-Minute**, and time execution on **5-Minute** (or **3-Minute**) charts—mindful of NSE opening volatility (9:15 AM–9:45 AM IST) and closing flows (3:00 PM–3:30 PM IST).

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

* **Line charts** clarify macro direction and structural closes; **OHLC bars** and **candlesticks** reveal intrabar range and rejection.
* Never pick a timeframe based on boredom or speed—pick the timeframe that matches how long you can realistically hold and manage risk.
* Always let the **higher timeframe** set the directional permission before taking signals on a **lower timeframe**.
