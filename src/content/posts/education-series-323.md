Position sizing answers one vital question before entry: **how many shares or lots should I trade given where my stop-loss sits and how wide the market's swings are right now?** Consistent position sizing turns stop-losses into predictable rupee risks rather than random dollar losses.

## Core Position-Sizing Methods

### 1. Fixed Fractional Risk Sizing
Risk a fixed percentage (or fixed rupee unit) of your trading capital if the trade hits its structural stop-loss:

$$\text{Position Size (Units)} = \frac{\text{Rupee Risk Budget}}{\text{Entry Price} - \text{Stop-Loss Price}}$$

* If your risk budget is ₹5,000 and your stop is ₹25 away from entry, position size is **$5,000 \div 25 = 200$ units**. If your stop is ₹50 away, position size drops to **$100$ units**—keeping rupee risk equal.

### 2. ATR-Based Sizing
* Measure stop distance in multiples of **Average True Range (ATR)** (for example, $1.5\times$ or $2.0\times$ daily/intraday ATR) rather than arbitrary fixed points.
* When ATR expands during volatile weeks, automatic unit reduction prevents a normal volatility swing from stopping you out prematurely with oversized lots.

### 3. Volatility-Adjusted Sizing
* Scale down notional exposure or option lot count when **India VIX** or stock **IV/HV** rises above normal levels, and keep sizing disciplined when volatility is low.

## Key Takeaways

* **Stop distance and position size are inversely linked:** wider structural stops require fewer lots/shares to hold rupee risk steady.
* Never pick lot sizes based on how confident you feel—let **risk math and volatility** govern size.
