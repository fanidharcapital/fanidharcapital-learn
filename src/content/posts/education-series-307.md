Obvious chart patterns—such as textbook double tops, clean horizontal ranges, or trendlines—attract clustered buy-stop and sell-stop orders just beyond the boundary. Because those clusters represent concentrated liquidity, price frequently pokes past the level (**a false breakout or trap**) before reversing sharply.

## Anatomy of a Classic Breakout Trap

1. **Obvious Level & Stop Cluster:** A clear swing high or range ceiling sits just above visible resistance, drawing breakout buyers and short-seller stop-losses.
2. **The Poke / Sweep:** Price pushes slightly above resistance—often with an emotional burst or news tick—triggering breakout entries and short covering.
3. **Failure & Re-Entry Into Range:** Momentum stalls immediately, price closes back below the breakout level, and trapped late buyers become forced sellers as price rotates back toward the opposite side of the range.

## How to Avoid Getting Trapped

* **Require Acceptance, Not Just a Wick:** A brief spike beyond resistance that closes back inside within one or two candles is a warning sign, not confirmed breakout acceptance.
* **Place Stops Beyond Structural Invalidation:** Putting stops right at obvious round numbers or just behind a minor 5-minute swing makes your trade an easy target for routine noise.
* **Confirm Reversal Structure:** Before fading a failed breakout, wait for a clear rejection candle or lower-timeframe CHoCH back inside the prior range.

## Key Takeaways

* False breakouts thrive where **obvious levels and crowded stops** sit together.
* Waiting for **re-entry and acceptance** protects you from buying the exact top of a trap.
