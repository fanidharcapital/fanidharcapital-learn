## 1. Technical Invalidation vs. Arbitrary Percentage Stops

A **stop-loss** is not a random pain threshold—it is an objective **invalidation level** on the chart:

| Stop-Loss Approach | How It Is Set | Strength | Key Weakness |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Technical Invalidation + Volatility Buffer** | Placed beyond a structural swing low/high, support/resistance zone, or broken trend base, padded with ATR (Lesson #106) | Tied directly to market structure—if price crosses it, your setup thesis has genuinely failed | Requires **adjusting share quantity** (position size) rather than shrinking the stop into noise |
| **Arbitrary Fixed % Stop (e.g., "Always 3%")** | Fixed percentage away from entry regardless of chart structure or stock volatility | Simple mental rule for beginners | Ignores structure—can sit inside normal daily noise on high-ATR stocks, or far past real invalidation on low-ATR stocks |

### The Golden Rule of Stop Placement
**Never move a stop-loss closer just to fit a larger position size.** If a 3% stop sits right inside a stock's normal 2-day pullback swing, a routine dip will stop you out before the trend resumes. Instead, **keep the stop at the true technical invalidation level** and **reduce position size** so your rupee loss stays within your risk limit.

---

## 2. Where Logical Invalidation Levels Sit

1. **Bullish Breakout or Polarity Flip:** Just below the **broken resistance-turned-support zone** or below the **most recent Higher Low (HL)** that launched the move. If price falls back inside the prior range and holds below the swing low, the breakout has failed.
2. **Trend Pullback to Moving Average / Ribbon:** Just below the **pullback swing low** and below the relevant moving average (such as the 20-EMA or 50-EMA), padded by a small volatility buffer ($1.0\times$–$1.5\times$ ATR) to absorb brief wick sweeps.
3. **Bearish Breakdown / Short Setup:** Just above the **most recent Lower High (LH)** or above the broken support shelf that flipped into resistance.

---

## 3. Practical Realities in Indian Equities (NSE / BSE)

* **Stop-Loss Hunting & Liquidity Sweeps:** On NSE/BSE, obvious round numbers and exact prior swing lows often attract clusters of retail stop-loss orders. Brief wick dips slightly below a swing low—followed by an immediate reclaim—are common; giving a modest **ATR buffer** rather than placing a stop on the exact tick of a round number helps avoid premature whipsaws.
* **Overnight Gap Risk:** An intraday or positional stop-loss order cannot execute *inside* a price gap if a stock opens sharply below your stop after earnings, global news, or macro announcements. Factor overnight gap potential into your holding horizon and position size (Lesson #106).

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

* A valid **stop-loss** marks the price where your **chart thesis is invalidated**, not an arbitrary percentage chosen out of habit.
* If a true technical stop is wide, **shrink your position size**—do not cram the stop into normal market noise.
* Add a **volatility buffer** beyond obvious swing lows/round numbers to reduce whipsaws while respecting gap risk on NSE/BSE.
