## 1. Union Budget: Fiscal Path, Borrowing & Capex Themes

*   **Fiscal Deficit & Market Borrowing:** When government spending exceeds revenue receipts, the gap is the **fiscal deficit**, financed partly through **market borrowing** via dated `G-Sec` bonds and Treasury bills. Markets watch both the deficit consolidation path and gross/net borrowing numbers closely because heavy bond supply can pressure bond prices and push yields higher.
*   **Capex Push vs. Consumption Support:** Allocation toward **capital expenditure (`capex`)**—such as roads, railways, urban infrastructure, defense, power, and logistics—supports multi-year physical asset creation and order pipelines, whereas consumption or subsidy measures affect household demand and staples/rural themes differently.

---

## 2. G-Sec Yields & Event Volatility Around the Budget

*   **Benchmark Cost of Capital:** **Government Security (`G-Sec`) yields** (such as the benchmark 10-year `G-Sec` yield) act as a key sovereign rate anchor in India—influencing lending rates, corporate bond pricing, and equity valuation multiples.
*   **Budget-Day Volatility & Post-Budget Follow-Through:** Budget sessions often bring sharp **intraday volatility and `India VIX` spikes** as headlines hit fast; sustainable sector trends emerge over the days and weeks that follow as actual execution and order flow confirm the re-rating.

---

## 3. Key Takeaways

*   Fiscal policy shapes both **sector themes** (such as `capex` vs. consumption) and **bond yields** through deficit and borrowing math.
*   Watch **`G-Sec` yield reactions** and wait for **post-event price follow-through** rather than chasing knee-jerk headline ticks.
