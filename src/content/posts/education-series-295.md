Earnings season separates traders who gamble on overnight announcements from traders who understand **implied volatility pricing** and **Post-Earnings Announcement Drift (PEAD)**.

## Case Setup: IT Heavyweight Reports Results After Market Close

Imagine a Tier-1 IT stock trading at ₹1,600 ahead of quarterly earnings. Front-month ATM option implied volatility jumps from **22% to 44%**, pricing an expected move of **±5.5% (±₹88)**.

### Comparing Three Approaches on the Same Event

1. **Trader A — Buys Naked ₹1,600 CE at ₹48 Before Results:**
   * Next morning, the company beats margin guidance and the stock gaps up **+3.2% to ₹1,651**.
   * Despite guessing direction correctly, ATM IV collapses from 44% back to 21% at 9:15 AM. The ₹1,600 CE opens near ₹54—barely covering transaction costs and risking full loss if the stock stalls.
2. **Trader B — Sells Defined-Risk Bull Put Spread (₹1,580 PE / ₹1,540 PE) Before Results:**
   * Collects inflated pre-event credit with defined maximum risk.
   * When the stock gaps up to ₹1,651 and IV crushes, both puts lose extrinsic value immediately, locking in **75%+ of max profit** within the first 15 minutes.
3. **Trader C — Waits for Post-Earnings Drift (PEAD) After 9:45 AM:**
   * Institutional analysts raise full-year EPS estimates, triggering multi-day mutual fund accumulation.
   * Trader C buys a **Bull Call Spread** after the stock holds above its opening 30-minute high with lower, normalized IV—riding a steady 8-day trend from ₹1,655 to ₹1,740.

## Execution Takeaways From This Case

* **Being Right on Direction Is Not Enough Before Earnings:** If the stock moves less than the option chain's implied move, naked option buyers still lose to **vega collapse**.
* **Post-Earnings Drift Is Often Cleaner Than the Gap:** Large institutional funds require several sessions to build positions after strong earnings surprises, creating high-probability trend continuation setups once IV resets.
