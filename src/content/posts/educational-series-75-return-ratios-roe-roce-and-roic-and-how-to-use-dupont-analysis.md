Welcome to **Lesson 75** of **Module 03: Fundamental Analysis** (**Lessons 61–90**) in the **Shree Fanidhar Capital Educational Series**.

In **Lesson 74**, we examined **Profitability Ratios** (`Gross Margin`, `OPM`, and `NPM`), which measure profit per rupee of *sales*. However, a business can earn high margins on sales and still be a mediocre compounder if it must lock up massive amounts of machinery, inventory, or receivables to generate those sales. Today, we evaluate **Return Ratios—`ROE`, `ROCE`, and `ROIC`**—and unlock **DuPont Analysis** to see what truly drives returns on capital.

---

## 1. Comparing `ROE`, `ROCE`, and `ROIC`

| Return Ratio | Formula | Capital Base Measured | Best Analytical Use & Limitation |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Return on Equity (`ROE`)** | $\frac{\text{Profit After Tax (PAT)}}{\text{Average Shareholders’ Equity}}$ | Equity shareholders' capital (`Share Capital + Reserves`). | Measures compounding power for equity owners; **can be artificially inflated by heavy debt** or deflated by huge idle cash. |
| **2. Return on Capital Employed (`ROCE`)** | $\frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Capital Employed}}$ *(where $\text{Capital Employed} = \text{Total Equity} + \text{Total Debt}$ or $\text{Total Assets} - \text{Current Liabilities}$)* | Both equity and debt capital (`pre-tax operating basis`). | **Capital-structure neutral**—ideal for comparing leveraged vs. debt-free companies in capital-intensive industries. |
| **3. Return on Invested Capital (`ROIC`)** | $\frac{\text{NOPAT}}{\text{Invested Capital}}$ *(where $\text{NOPAT} = \text{EBIT}(1 - t)$ and $\text{Invested Capital} = \text{Equity} + \text{Debt} - \text{Non-Operating Cash}$)* | Only the **actual cash deployed in core operations** (excluding idle treasury cash). | The **gold-standard measure of core business quality & moat**; shows true return earned on operating assets. |

> **Institutional Benchmark:** Across Indian equities, a consistent **`ROCE` and `ROIC` above `15%–18%`** (well above the `~11%–12%` cost of capital) signals genuine economic value creation.

## 2. Worked Comparison: Why Debt Can Make `ROE` Misleading (`₹ Crore`)

Consider two competitors earning identical operating profit (**`EBIT = ₹200 Cr`**) on **`₹1,000 Cr`** of operating capital (tax rate = `25%`, interest rate on debt = `10%`):

| Metric | Company A (`Zero Debt`) | Company B (`70% Debt Funded`) | Analytical Takeaway |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Shareholders’ Equity** | `₹1,000 Cr` | `₹300 Cr` | Company B uses thin equity |
| **Total Debt (`@ 10% interest`)** | `₹0` | `₹700 Cr` | Company B carries heavy leverage |
| **Total Capital Employed** | `₹1,000 Cr` | `₹1,000 Cr` | Identical physical capital base |
| **`EBIT` (Operating Profit)** | `₹200 Cr` | `₹200 Cr` | Identical operating performance |
| **Less: Interest (`10% × Debt`)** | `₹0` | `(₹70 Cr)` | Debt service consumes `35%` of `EBIT` |
| **Profit Before Tax (`PBT`)** | `₹200 Cr` | `₹130 Cr` | — |
| **Profit After Tax (`PAT @ 25% tax`)** | **`₹150 Cr`** | **`₹97.5 Cr`** | Company A earns `₹52.5 Cr` more net profit |
| **`ROCE` (`EBIT / Capital Employed`)** | **`20.0%`** | **`20.0%`** | Reveals both businesses earn the exact same `20%` operating return |
| **`ROE` (`PAT / Equity`)** | **`15.0%`** | **`32.5%`** | Company B's `32.5%` `ROE` is **leverage-driven**, not superior operations! |

## 3. DuPont Analysis: Decomposing `ROE` Into Its Three Engines

Created by the DuPont Corporation in the 1920s, **3-Step DuPont Analysis** breaks `ROE` into three distinct drivers so you immediately see *how* a company achieves its return on equity:

$$\text{ROE} = \underbrace{\frac{\text{PAT}}{\text{Revenue}}}_{\text{Net Profit Margin}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Average Total Assets}}}_{\text{Asset Turnover}} \times \underbrace{\frac{\text{Average Total Assets}}{\text{Average Equity}}}_{\text{Equity Multiplier (Leverage)}}$$

| DuPont Driver | What High Performance Signals | Classic Industry Archetype |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. High Net Profit Margin (`PAT / Revenue`)** | Strong pricing power, brand moat, IP, or proprietary differentiation. | Branded Pharma, Luxury Goods, Enterprise Software. |
| **2. High Asset Turnover (`Revenue / Assets`)** | Lean working capital, rapid inventory turns, and capital-light scale. | Value Retail (`DMart`-style), FMCG Distributors, QSR Chains. |
| **3. High Equity Multiplier (`Assets / Equity`)** | Higher financial leverage—boosts `ROE` in good times, magnifies downside in recessions. | Banks & NBFCs *(natural business model)* vs. Over-leveraged Industrials *(risk flag)*. |

---

## Key Takeaways

* **Never look at `ROE` in isolation:** Always pair `ROE` with **`ROCE`** and **`ROIC`** so you distinguish true operating excellence from debt-fueled leverage or idle cash drag.
* **`ROIC` strips out excess cash and non-operating investments**, revealing the pure economic return generated by core operating assets.
* **Use 3-Step DuPont Analysis (`Margin × Asset Turnover × Financial Leverage`)** to pinpoint whether a company's `ROE` comes from pricing power, asset efficiency, or borrowing.
