In **Lesson #154**, we covered daily Mark-to-Market settlement. In **Lesson #155**, we examine **final settlement at contract expiry** on NSE—specifically **Cash Settlement** (used for index futures) versus **Physical Settlement** (used for single-stock futures). Knowing this distinction is essential so you never get caught off guard by delivery obligations or expiry-week margin increases.

## 1. Cash Settlement vs. Physical Settlement

| Feature | Cash Settlement (Index Futures) | Physical Settlement (Stock Futures) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **How Final Settlement Occurs** | Settled in **cash** based on the final settlement price at expiry. | Settled via **actual delivery/receipt of underlying shares** through demat/clearing. |
| **Share Delivery Needed?** | No—there is no single underlying stock to deliver. | **Yes**—long futures must take delivery (pay full contract value); short futures must deliver shares. |
| **Expiry-Week Margin Impact** | Standard exchange/expiry margin rules apply. | Brokers typically increase delivery/expiry margins ahead of expiry to cover full physical settlement exposure. |

---

## 2. What Physical Settlement Means for Stock Futures Traders

If you hold a **stock futures** contract through expiry without squaring off or rolling over:

*   **Long Stock Future:** You are obligated to buy the underlying shares at the final settlement price—requiring **full contract value** (not just margin) plus applicable transaction/delivery costs.
*   **Short Stock Future:** You are obligated to deliver the underlying shares into settlement—requiring those shares in your demat/holdings (or borrowing/settlement arrangements per exchange/broker rules).
*   **Broker Risk Controls:** Because many retail traders do not intend to take or give physical delivery of large stock lots, brokers progressively raise margins in the days leading into expiry or square off positions if delivery margin requirements are not met.

---

## 3. Key Takeaways

*   **Index futures** are **cash-settled**; **stock futures** on NSE are **physically settled** at expiry.
*   Physical settlement on stock futures involves full underlying share delivery/payment obligations and higher expiry-week margins.
*   If you only want price exposure without physical delivery, **square off or roll over** before expiry window restrictions kick in.
