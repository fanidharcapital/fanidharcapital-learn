In **Lesson #155**, we examined cash settlement versus physical settlement at expiry. In **Lesson #156**, we look at the **calendar rhythm of futures contracts**: **Expiry Cycles and Rollovers**. Every futures contract has a finite life, which means positions held longer than one contract cycle must either be closed out or rolled forward into a subsequent expiry series—creating distinct liquidity, spread, and volatility patterns as expiry approaches.

## 1. Near, Mid & Far Month Contracts

Exchange-traded futures typically list multiple active contract months simultaneously (such as **Near Month**, **Next Month**, and **Far Month** series):

*   **Near Month (Current Series):** Holds the highest trading volume, deepest order book, and tightest bid-ask spreads for most of the cycle—until rollover activity shifts liquidity toward the next contract in expiry week.
*   **Next Month & Far Month:** Useful for multi-month directional views or calendar spreads, though far-month contracts generally have thinner liquidity and wider spreads than the active near-month contract.

---

## 2. What Is a Rollover & Why Does It Matter?

A **rollover** happens when a trader closes an expiring near-month futures position and simultaneously opens the same directional position in the next expiry month:

1.  **Rollover Percentage:** Market participants track **rollover %** (the share of near-month open interest shifting into the next month) in the final days before expiry to gauge whether institutional conviction is carrying into the next series or unwinding.
2.  **Calendar Spread (Roll Cost):** Rolling is economically similar to executing a **calendar spread**—buying/selling one expiry month against the opposite leg in another expiry month. Your net cost or credit depends on the price difference between the two contracts (covered in detail in **Lesson #162**).

---

## 3. Expiry-Day & Expiry-Week Dynamics on NSE / BSE

As expiry approaches, order flow changes noticeably:

*   **Liquidity Migration:** In the final sessions before expiry, volume and open interest gradually shift from the expiring contract to the next contract. Avoid trading illiquid expiring contracts late on expiry day unless you have a clear execution reason.
*   **Pinning, Unwinding & Settlement Flows:** Index and stock futures see position squaring, hedging adjustments, and rollovers that can produce sharp intraday swings or late-session volatility.
*   **Stock Futures Cutoffs:** Remember from **Lesson #155** that stock futures are **physically settled**—so traders who do not intend to take or deliver shares must exit or roll over ahead of broker expiry-week risk cutoffs.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

*   Futures run on **finite expiry cycles** (near, next, and far months), with near-month liquidity shifting into the next month near expiry.
*   **Rolling over** carries a position forward into the next contract month and reflects whether participants are extending or closing exposure.
*   Watch **liquidity migration** and **delivery/risk cutoffs** during expiry week so you aren't caught in thin books or forced square-offs.
