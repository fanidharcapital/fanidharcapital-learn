In **Lesson #274**, we examined domestic fiscal policy, borrowing, and `G-Sec` yields. In **Lesson #275**, we look outside India at **Global Cues & Cross-Asset Signals**—how **US Federal Reserve policy**, **US Treasury yields**, **crude oil**, and **`USD/INR`** influence global risk appetite and Indian market openings.

![Educational Series #275: Global Cues & Cross-Asset Signals](/images/lessons/lesson-275.png)

---

## 1. US Fed Policy, Treasury Yields & Global Liquidity

*   **Global Rate & Dollar Anchor:** Because the US dollar is the dominant global funding and reserve currency, **US Federal Reserve (`Fed`) rate expectations** and **US Treasury yields** (such as the 10-year yield) strongly influence global liquidity, dollar strength, and foreign portfolio flows into emerging markets like India.
*   **Shifting Rate Expectations:** Markets react not only to actual `Fed` rate decisions, but also to inflation prints, labor data, and policy commentary that shift expectations for future rate cuts, holds, or hikes.

---

## 2. Crude Oil, USD/INR & Overnight-to-Open Context

*   **Crude Oil & `USD/INR`:** As a major net importer of crude oil, India monitors oil price swings and **`USD/INR`** closely because higher energy costs or currency pressure can feed into inflation, import bills, and risk sentiment.
*   **Context, Not Blind Prediction:** Strong US/Asian cues or a quiet overnight session help frame **likely opening gap context at `9:15 AM`**, but domestic trend continuation or reversal still depends on how Indian indices and institutional flows behave once trading opens.

---

## 3. Key Takeaways

*   **US Fed policy, US Treasury yields, crude oil, and `USD/INR`** are key cross-asset gauges shaping global risk mood and opening sentiment.
*   Use global cues for **context and risk calibration**, never as a substitute for domestic price structure.
