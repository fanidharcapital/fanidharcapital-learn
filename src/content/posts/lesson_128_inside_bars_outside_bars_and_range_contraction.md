## 1. Inside Bars vs. Outside Bars: Range Geometry

Unlike Harami or Engulfing patterns (which measure **real body** containment), Inside and Outside Bars measure the **entire High-to-Low range**:

| Bar Formation | Strict Structural Definition | Volatility & Order-Flow Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Inside Bar (IB)** | $\text{High}_2 < \text{High}_1$ AND $\text{Low}_2 > \text{Low}_1$ (entire range sits inside the prior **Mother Bar**) | **Range Contraction / Coiling:** Volatility drops sharply as buyers and sellers reach temporary equilibrium inside yesterday's boundaries. |
| **Double / Triple Inside Bar** | Two or more consecutive candles nested inside the same Mother Bar | **Extreme Volatility Squeeze:** Coiled spring energy—often precedes explosive multi-day expansion once the Mother Bar range breaks. |
| **Outside Bar (OB)** | $\text{High}_2 > \text{High}_1$ AND $\text{Low}_2 < \text{Low}_1$ (sweeps both extremes of the prior candle) | **Range Expansion / Liquidity Sweep:** Price probes one extreme, traps breakout traders, reverses through the opposite extreme, and closes with authority. |

---

## 2. Why Range Contraction Produces Asymmetric Trades

Think of volatility like an accordion: tight compression periods inevitably give way to wide expansion periods.

*   **Tighter Risk Denominator (`1R`):** Because an Inside Bar has a smaller High-to-Low range than the Mother Bar, your structural stop-loss distance (`1R`) is naturally compact—allowing larger position sizing within your fixed Rupee risk cap (Lesson #114) and higher Reward-to-Risk multiples (Lesson #115).
*   **Institutional Accumulation / Distribution:** On NSE Daily charts, when a stock breaks out on strong volume and prints a tight **Inside Bar** the next session, institutions are absorbing profit-booking without letting price surrender ground.

## 3. High-Probability Execution Rules on NSE / BSE

### A. Trading Inside Bar Breakouts (Trend Continuation)
1.  **Trade With the Dominant Trend:** Inside Bars work best as **continuation pauses** right after an impulsive breakout leg—not in the middle of messy, sideways chop.
2.  **Trigger on Range Resolution:**
    *   In an uptrend, enter when price breaks above the **Inside Bar's High** (or the Mother Bar's High for a more conservative filter).
    *   In a downtrend, enter when price breaks below the **Inside Bar's Low**.
3.  **Stop-Loss Placement:** Place your stop just below the Inside Bar's Low (aggressive) or below the Mother Bar's Low (conservative).

### B. Reading Outside Bars (Failed Breakouts & Directional Shifts)
1.  **Check Where the Outside Bar Closes:** An Outside Bar that sweeps below yesterday's low and closes in the **top 25% of its range** (bullish outside bar) signals a classic bear trap and demand takeover.
2.  **Avoid Mid-Range Outside Bars:** Wide-swinging Outside Bars in the middle of a range simply reflect erratic two-way volatility—wait for Outside Bars at **key swing highs, swing lows, or range boundaries**.

## 5. Key Takeaways

*   **Inside Bars** measure **full High-to-Low contraction** inside a Mother Bar, signaling coiled energy and tight risk parameters.
*   **Outside Bars** sweep both prior extremes, signaling sudden volatility expansion and potential liquidity traps.
*   Pair Inside Bar breakouts with an existing directional trend on NSE/BSE charts rather than trading every contraction inside sideways chop.
