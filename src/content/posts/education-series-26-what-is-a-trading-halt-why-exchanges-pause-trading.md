In an efficient market, trading should be continuous from 9:15 AM to 3:30 PM. However, the **NSE** and **BSE** sometimes find it necessary to intervene and press "Pause" on the market. This is called a **Trading Halt**.

A trading halt is an involuntary, temporary suspension of trading for a specific security, or sometimes the entire market, mandated by the exchange or the market regulator (SEBI). It is not a failure of the market; it is a safety feature designed to maintain fairness and order.

---

## 1. Why Do Exchanges Halt Trading?

Halts are never arbitrary. They are implemented to address specific issues that could otherwise lead to chaos or unfair information advantages. The primary goals are:

*   **Information Dissemination:** To ensure all investors have time to receive and digest material news.
*   **Price Discovery:** To allow a volatile market to reset rationally rather than spiraling due to panic or greed.
*   **Fairness:** To prevent front-running or trading on inside information before it is public.
*   **System Integrity:** To address technical failures within the exchange infrastructure.

---

## 2. Common Types of Trading Halts

Trading halts generally fall into four main categories:

### Type 1: Regulatory or Investigatory Halt
This is initiated by SEBI or the exchange when they suspect manipulation, irregular trading activity, or violation of market regulations in a particular stock.
*   **Duration:** Can last from a few hours to several days or weeks until the investigation concludes.

### Type 2: News Pending Halt
This is the most common type of halt for individual stocks. A company is about to release "material price-sensitive information" (like earnings, a merger, acquisition, or major regulatory approval).
*   **Process:** The company requests the halt *before* the news hits the wires. Trading stops immediately.
*   **Duration:** Usually short. The halt typically lasts 30 to 60 minutes, or until the news is officially published on the exchange website. Trading then resumes, often with high volatility.

### Type 3: Circuit Breaker Halt (Limit Up/Down)
We covered this in detail in **Lesson 25**, but it is worth mentioning as a form of mandatory halt.
*   **Individual Stocks:** Halts automatically when a stock hits its fixed daily band (e.g., 5%, 10%, 20%).
*   **Market-Wide:** The entire NSE and BSE halt when the Nifty 50 or Sensex drops 10%, 15%, or 20%.

### Type 4: Technical Glitch Halt
Sometimes the exchange's trading engine, connectivity lines, or data dissemination systems fail.
*   **Why Halt:** If price feeds are incorrect or orders cannot be processed fairly for all brokers, the exchange must halt all affected segments (or the entire market) to fix the technical issue.
*   **Duration:** Unknown until the fix is verified. These halts are highly frustrating for traders but necessary to maintain market integrity.

---

## 3. What Happens During a Trading Halt?

When a halt is active:

*   **No New Orders Match:** You cannot buy or sell the halted security.
*   **Order Modification:** Depending on the exchange rules and the type of halt, you may be able to place, modify, or cancel **Limit Orders** in the background, but they will not execute until the halt is lifted.
*   **Market Orders:** Placing a market order during a halt is extremely risky; it will execute at the very first available price when trading resumes, which could be dramatically different from the pre-halt price (heavy **slippage**).
*   **Options Exercise:** You generally cannot exercise options during a halt, though this is rare to encounter.

When the halt is lifted (often referred to as "Resumption of Trading"), the exchange usually initiates a brief **Pre-Open Call Auction session** (similar to the 9:00 AM session) to determine a single, fair **Re-Opening Price** before continuous trading resumes.

---

## Key Takeaways

*   **Halts are a safety mechanism:** They protect you from executing trades based on incomplete information or broken systems.
*   **Respect the News Pending halt:** If a stock you own suddenly stops trading around earnings season, do not panic; check the exchange website for an impending announcement.
*   **Avoid Market Orders during halts:** If you hold a position that is halted, your best strategy is usually to wait patiently until the re-opening price is discovered through the call auction.
*   **Understand the risk of overnight holds:** If a company is set to announce results *after* market hours, the first indication you might get the next morning is a "News Pending" halt followed by a gap up or down at the open.
