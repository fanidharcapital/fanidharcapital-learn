![Case Study — Asymmetric Payoff Engineering With Ratio Spreads & Backspreads](/images/lessons/lesson-344.png)

Choosing between a **1x2 ratio spread** and a **1x2 backspread** comes down to what kind of move you expect—and which tail risk you are willing to manage.

## Case A: Measured Move Into a Resistance Target (1x2 Ratio)

* **Thesis:** Price is likely to rally toward a clear overhead resistance level and stall rather than surge far beyond it.
* **Payoff Behaviour:** As price rises toward the short strike, the structure gains value; if price rockets well past the short strike, the extra short leg creates adverse directional risk unless capped with a further-OTM protective leg.

## Case B: Low-IV Coil With Potential Explosive Breakout (1x2 Backspread)

* **Thesis:** IV is low and price is coiling tightly with potential for a sharp directional breakout once compression resolves.
* **Payoff Behaviour:** If price barely moves or stalls right on the long strike at expiry, the structure faces its maximum planned loss; if a fast breakout with rising IV occurs, the two long legs drive strong asymmetric upside or downside profit.

## Key Execution Lessons

* **Match Payoff Shape to Expected Path:** Ratio structures favor controlled moves to a target zone; backspreads favor sharp breakouts out of low volatility.
* **Never Leave Unintended Tail Risk Unchecked:** Always size and cap tail exposure in line with your margin and drawdown limits.
