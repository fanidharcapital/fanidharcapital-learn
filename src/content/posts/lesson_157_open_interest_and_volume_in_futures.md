## 1. Volume vs. Open Interest (OI)

*   **Volume:** Measures **total contracts traded** during a session or period. Every time a contract changes hands, volume increases by 1 (or 1 lot).
*   **Open Interest (OI):** Measures **total active, unsettled futures contracts** open in the market at a given point. When a new buyer and new seller open a fresh contract, OI rises; when an existing long and existing short close out opposite sides, OI falls.

---

## 2. The Four Price + Open Interest Combinations

| Price Action | Open Interest (OI) | What It Typically Signals | Market Behavior |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Price Rising ($\uparrow$)** | **OI Rising ($\uparrow$)** | **Long Buildup** | Fresh long positions are being initiated with conviction; buyers are stepping in and holding. |
| **Price Falling ($\downarrow$)** | **OI Rising ($\uparrow$)** | **Short Buildup** | Fresh short positions are being initiated aggressively; sellers are pressing down. |
| **Price Falling ($\downarrow$)** | **OI Falling ($\downarrow$)** | **Long Unwinding** | Existing long holders are booking profits or cutting losses and exiting positions. |
| **Price Rising ($\uparrow$)** | **OI Falling ($\downarrow$)** | **Short Covering** | Existing short sellers are buying back contracts to exit—often fueling a sharp bounce that may fade if no fresh longs enter. |

---

## 3. Practical Rules for Reading OI With Price Action

1.  **Price Structure Comes First:** Never treat OI as a standalone buy or sell indicator. Always read OI *inside* your price-action context—at support, resistance, breakouts, or pullbacks (Lessons #140–#144).
2.  **Distinguish Fresh Trend From Short Covering:** A rally accompanied by **falling OI** often reflects **short covering** rather than fresh institutional long buildup. If price reaches resistance while OI drops, be cautious about chasing as if a brand-new multi-week trend just started.
3.  **Watch Rollover Distortion Near Expiry:** Near month-end expiry, falling OI in the expiring contract can simply reflect **rollovers** into the next month rather than genuine position liquidation—always check combined or next-month OI too.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

*   **Volume** counts total traded activity; **Open Interest** tracks total **active, open contracts**.
*   Rising price + rising OI = **Long Buildup**; falling price + rising OI = **Short Buildup**.
*   Falling price + falling OI = **Long Unwinding**; rising price + falling OI = **Short Covering**.
*   Always interpret OI alongside **price structure** and **expiry/rollover context**.
