Welcome to **Lesson 72** of **Module 03: Fundamental Analysis** (**Lessons 61–90**) in the **Shree Fanidhar Capital Educational Series**.

In **Lesson 71**, we introduced **Return Ratios (`ROE`, `ROCE`, `ROIC`)**, profit margins, and asset turnover ratios—and saw how two companies can post the exact same headline `ROE` for completely different reasons. Today, we unlock the **DuPont framework**, a classic diagnostic tool that decomposes **`ROE`** into its underlying operational and financing drivers.

---

## 1. The 3-Step DuPont Identity

Algebraically, `Return on Equity` (`PAT / Equity`) can be expanded by multiplying and dividing by `Revenue` and `Total Assets`:

$$\text{ROE} = \underbrace{\left(\frac{\text{PAT}}{\text{Revenue}}\right)}_{\text{Net Profit Margin}} \times \underbrace{\left(\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Total Assets}}\right)}_{\text{Asset Turnover}} \times \underbrace{\left(\frac{\text{Total Assets}}{\text{Shareholders’ Equity}}\right)}_{\text{Equity Multiplier (Leverage)}}$$

Notice how the intermediate terms (`Revenue` and `Total Assets`) cancel out to leave `PAT / Equity`—while revealing **three distinct levers** behind the headline number:

| DuPont Component | Formula | What It Captures | Strategic Archetype |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Net Profit Margin** | $\frac{\text{PAT}}{\text{Revenue}}$ | Profitability & pricing power per rupee of sales (after operating costs, interest, and tax). | High-margin brands, specialty chemicals, software, or patented pharma. |
| **2. Asset Turnover** | $\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Total Assets}}$ | Capital & asset efficiency—how much sales each rupee of assets generates. | High-volume, low-margin retail, FMCG distribution, or fast-turning traders. |
| **3. Equity Multiplier** | $\frac{\text{Total Assets}}{\text{Shareholders’ Equity}}$ | Financial leverage—how much debt/liabilities amplify equity returns (`1.0x` = zero debt/non-equity funding). | Banks/NBFCs (by design) or leveraged cyclicals (higher risk if earnings dip). |

> **Core Takeaway:** A **`20%` `ROE`** driven by **strong margins and high asset turnover** with a low equity multiplier (`~1.2x–1.5x`) is far more resilient than a **`20%` `ROE`** propped up by a **`4.0x` equity multiplier** and razor-thin margins.

---

## 2. Worked Comparison: Two Paths to a `20%` `ROE`

Suppose **Alpha Consumer Goods** and **Beta Heavy Materials** both report a **`20.0%` `ROE`**, yet look inside the 3-step DuPont breakdown:

| Metric | Alpha Consumer Goods | Beta Heavy Materials | What DuPont Reveals |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Net Profit Margin (`PAT / Revenue`)** | `10.0%` | `8.0%` | Alpha retains more bottom-line profit per rupee of sales |
| **Asset Turnover (`Revenue / Assets`)** | `1.60x` | `0.83x` | Alpha sweats its asset base nearly **2x faster** |
| **Equity Multiplier (`Assets / Equity`)** | `1.25x` | `3.00x` | Beta relies heavily on leverage (`3.0x` vs. `1.25x`) |
| **DuPont `ROE` (`Margin × Turnover × Leverage`)** | **`20.0%`** | **`20.0%`** | Identical `ROE`, **very different risk & quality** |

In a downturn when demand softens or interest rates rise, **Beta’s high leverage (`3.0x`)** creates sharper earnings volatility, whereas **Alpha’s high asset turnover and low leverage** provide a much sturdier cushion.

---

## 3. Extending to the 5-Step DuPont Breakdown

For deeper fundamental scrutiny, analysts split the **Net Profit Margin** further so interest burden and tax effects don't hide inside a single number:

1. **Tax Burden:** $\frac{\text{PAT}}{\text{PBT}}$ (shows how much pre-tax profit survives corporate taxes; closer to `1.0` means lower effective tax).
2. **Interest Burden:** $\frac{\text{PBT}}{\text{EBIT}}$ (shows how much operating profit `EBIT` is eroded by net interest expense; `1.0` means zero net interest drag).
3. **Operating Margin (`EBIT Margin`):** $\frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Revenue}}$ (pure core operating profitability before interest and taxes).
4. **Asset Turnover:** $\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Total Assets}}$ (asset productivity).
5. **Equity Multiplier:** $\frac{\text{Total Assets}}{\text{Shareholders’ Equity}}$ (financial leverage).

$$\text{ROE} = \left(\frac{\text{PAT}}{\text{PBT}}\right) \times \left(\frac{\text{PBT}}{\text{EBIT}}\right) \times \left(\frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Revenue}}\right) \times \left(\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Total Assets}}\right) \times \left(\frac{\text{Total Assets}}{\text{Shareholders’ Equity}}\right)$$

---

## 4. How to Use DuPont Analysis in Real Research

* **Track Multi-Year Shifts (`5–7 Years`):** If a company's `ROE` rises from `14%` to `22%`, check whether **Operating Margin** or **Asset Turnover** expanded—or whether **Equity Multiplier** jumped because debt rose or buybacks shrank equity.
* **Benchmark Within the Same Sector:** A `0.6x` asset turnover is normal for capital-intensive utilities or metals, whereas a `0.6x` turnover in retail would signal poor inventory/store productivity.
* **Pair `ROE` DuPont With `ROIC` & Cash Flow:** Even a clean DuPont `ROE` should be cross-checked against **Operating Cash Flow (`CFO`)** and **`ROIC`** so accounting profits aren't outrunning cash generation.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **DuPont analysis breaks `ROE` into three levers:** **Net Profit Margin**, **Asset Turnover**, and **Equity Multiplier (Leverage)**.
* **Quality of `ROE` matters as much as the level of `ROE`:** Margin and turnover strength indicate business durability, while high leverage increases risk during downturns.
* **The 5-step DuPont model** isolates **interest burden** and **tax burden** from core **operating margin (`EBIT / Revenue`)**.
