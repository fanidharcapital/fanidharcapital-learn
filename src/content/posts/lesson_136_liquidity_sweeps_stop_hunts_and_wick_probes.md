## 1. Where Liquidity Pools Sit on Every Chart

Large institutions cannot buy 500,000 shares of an NSE stock or execute massive Nifty futures blocks in thin air without moving price against themselves. They need **counterparty liquidity**—clusters of resting orders sitting at predictable spots:

1.  **Just Below Obvious Swing Lows & Equal Lows ("Double Bottoms"):**
    *   Long traders place their **sell stop-loss orders** just beneath prior swing lows.
    *   Breakdown traders place **sell-stop entry orders** at the exact same spot.
    *   Together, these form a dense pool of **sell liquidity** that large buyers can fill against.
2.  **Just Above Obvious Swing Highs & Equal Highs ("Double Tops"):**
    *   Short sellers place **buy-to-cover stop-losses** just above prior swing highs.
    *   Breakout chasers place **buy-stop entry orders** right above the ceiling.
    *   Together, these form a dense pool of **buy liquidity** that large sellers can distribute into.

## 2. Anatomy of a Liquidity Sweep (Wick Probe)

A **Liquidity Sweep** (also called a **Spring**, **Upthrust**, or **Turtle Soup** pattern) unfolds in three clear stages:

| Stage | Price Behavior on the Chart | Order-Flow Reality |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. The Setup Pool** | Clean, obvious horizontal high/low or "equal highs/lows" forms on the chart. | Retail stops and breakout orders accumulate just beyond the level. |
| **2. The Sweep / Probe** | Price accelerates past the obvious level (`₹1–₹8` depending on stock price), triggering stops and breakout entries. | Institutional algorithms absorb the triggered orders at favorable prices. |
| **3. The Snap-Back Close** | Lack of real follow-through causes price to **snap back inside the range**, closing back above the swept low (or below the swept high) and leaving a sharp wick. | Breakout traders are now trapped; their panic exits fuel the real move in the opposite direction. |

---

## 3. How to Trade Liquidity Sweeps Safely on NSE / BSE

Never guess a sweep *while* price is crashing through a low—wait for the **reclaim**:

1.  **Identify the Obvious Pool:** Mark a clean prior Swing Low (in an uptrend) or Swing High (in a downtrend) where stops naturally cluster.
2.  **Wait for the Sweep + Reclaim Close:** Do not buy as price breaks below the low. Wait for the candle (or the very next candle) to **close decisively back above the broken level**.
3.  **Enter on the Reclaim With Defined Risk:**
    *   **Entry:** On the close of the reclaim candle (or a shallow pullback inside it).
    *   **Stop-Loss:** Just below the **extreme tip of the sweep wick**. Because liquidity at that wick tip has already been harvested, price rarely revisits it if the sweep is genuine.

## 4. Practical Application on NSE / BSE

*   **Watch Equal Highs / Equal Lows:** When an NSE stock bounces twice off `₹650.00` to the exact rupee, almost every retail trader puts their stop at `₹648.50`. Expect a fast intraday probe to `₹646–₹647` followed by a sharp reclaim before any sustained rally.
*   **Combine With Higher-Timeframe Trend:** Bullish liquidity sweeps of pullback lows inside a **Weekly/Daily uptrend** carry the highest probability because you are aligning trap mechanics with dominant institutional flow.

---

## 5. Key Takeaways

*   **Liquidity pools** cluster just beyond obvious swing highs, swing lows, and equal highs/lows where stop-losses and breakout orders sit.
*   A **liquidity sweep** pokes past the level to trigger resting orders and **closes back inside the range**, leaving a sharp rejection wick.
*   Always wait for the **candle close back inside the level** before entering, and anchor your stop-loss just beyond the sweep wick extreme.
