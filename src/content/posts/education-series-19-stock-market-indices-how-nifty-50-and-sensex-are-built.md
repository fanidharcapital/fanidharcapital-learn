![Stock Market Indices — How Nifty 50 and Sensex Are Built](/images/lessons/lesson-19.png)

When headlines say *"Indian markets rose 1.2% today"* or *"benchmarks hit a fresh record,"* they are referring to **Stock Market Indices**—most commonly **Nifty 50** on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and **BSE Sensex** on the Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE).

With thousands of companies listed across Indian exchanges, checking each share price one by one would never give a clear picture of overall market direction. An index solves this by combining a representative basket of top companies into a single, real-time number.

---

## 1. What Is a Stock Market Index?

A **Stock Market Index** is a statistical barometer that tracks the collective price movement of a selected group of stocks listed on an exchange.

* **Measures Market Pulse:** Just as a thermometer measures room temperature without checking every air molecule, an index captures broad market sentiment and performance.
* **Not Directly Tradable:** You cannot buy a "share" of Nifty 50 or Sensex directly—because an index is a mathematical number. Instead, investors gain exposure through **Index Mutual Funds**, **Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs)**, or index derivatives.
* **Market-Cap Tiers & Sectors:** Beyond flagship large-cap benchmarks, exchanges also maintain broad indices (such as **Nifty 500**, **Nifty Midcap 150**, **Nifty Smallcap 250**) and sectoral indices (such as **Nifty Bank**, **Nifty IT**, **Nifty Auto**, and **Nifty Pharma**).

## 2. How Nifty 50 and Sensex Are Constructed

Both **Nifty 50** and **BSE Sensex** use the **Free-Float Market Capitalization** methodology rather than simple share-price averages.

### Step-by-Step Construction Logic
1. **Selection Criteria:** Companies must meet strict exchange norms on listing history, trading frequency, liquidity (low impact cost), and market-capitalization rank, while maintaining diversified representation across major Indian economic sectors.
2. **Free-Float Market Capitalization:**
   * **Full Market Cap** = $\text{Total Issued Shares} \times \text{Current Share Price}$.
   * **Free-Float Shares** exclude locked-in holdings held by promoters, founders, governments, or strategic entities that do not trade openly on the market.
   * **Free-Float Market Cap** = $\text{Free-Float Shares} \times \text{Current Share Price}$.
3. **Index Level Formula:**
   $$\text{Index Value} = \left( \frac{\text{Current Total Free-Float Market Cap of Constituents}}{\text{Base Period Free-Float Market Cap}} \right) \times \text{Base Index Value}$$
4. **Why Weighting Matters:** A heavyweight constituent with a large free-float market cap moves the index far more when its share price changes by 2% than a smaller constituent moving by 2%.

## 3. Nifty 50 vs. BSE Sensex at a Glance

| Parameter | BSE Sensex | Nifty 50 (NSE) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Exchange** | Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE) | National Stock Exchange (NSE) |
| **Number of Constituents** | **30** large, well-established companies | **50** large, liquid companies |
| **Sector Coverage** | Diversified across key Indian sectors | Broadly spans **13+ major sectors** (Financials, IT, Energy, Consumer, Auto, etc.) |
| **Base Year & Base Value** | **1978–79** (Base Value = **100**) | **November 3, 1995** (Base Value = **1,000**) |
| **Weighting Method** | Free-Float Market Capitalization | Free-Float Market Capitalization |
| **Rebalancing Cycle** | Semi-annual review (typically June & December) | Semi-annual review (typically March & September) |

## 4. Index Rebalancing & Corporate Action Adjustments

* **Periodic Rebalancing:** Index committees review eligible stocks semi-annually. Companies whose free-float market cap or liquidity falls below threshold rules are replaced by qualifying challengers—keeping the index aligned with India's evolving economy.
* **Corporate Action Neutrality:** When a constituent executes a **stock split** or **bonus issue**, its share price drops proportionally while its share count rises—so its free-float market cap remains unchanged and the index level does not artificially crash. Whenever constituents are swapped or rights/special adjustments occur, exchanges adjust the **base market-cap divisor** so the index level stays continuous.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **Free-float market cap drives index weight:** Nifty 50 (50 stocks) and Sensex (30 stocks) weight companies by publicly tradable market value—not raw share price.
* **Indices evolve automatically through rebalancing:** Underperforming or shrinking companies are periodically replaced by rising leaders, which is why flagship indices reflect India's long-term economic growth.
* **Use TRI when benchmarking funds:** Always compare mutual fund returns against the **Total Return Index (TRI)** version of an index (which includes reinvested dividends) rather than the Price Return Index alone.
