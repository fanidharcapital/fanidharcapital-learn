## Overview

Physical assets—particularly gold and real estate—command a dominant share of Indian household wealth. Comparing them rigorously against listed financial assets reveals sharp differences in entry ticket size, transaction costs, carrying expenses, and exit liquidity.

---

## Comparative Asset Matrix

| Feature | Physical Gold / Gold ETFs | Physical Real Estate / REITs | Listed Financial Markets |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Minimum Ticket Size** | Low (₹100+ via ETFs/funds) | Very High (₹50L–₹5Cr+ physical; low for REITs) | Low (single share / SIP) |
| **Transaction Friction** | 3% GST + making charges (physical); <0.5% (ETF) | 5%–8% stamp duty, registration, brokerage | ~0.1%–0.2% statutory & brokerage |
| **Carrying & Maintenance** | Locker fees (physical); TER (ETF) | Property tax, society maintenance, repairs, vacancy | Negligible (annual DP AMC) |
| **Cash Flow Generation** | Zero (except SGB coupon) | Rental yield (~2%–3% residential; 6%–8% commercial) | Dividends, buybacks, option premium |
| **Exit Liquidity** | Same-day / $T+1$ | Months to years (physical); $T+1$ (REITs) | Instant during market hours ($T+1$) |

---

## Critical Evaluation Factors

1. **Divisibility & Rebalancing:** You cannot sell a single bedroom of an apartment to raise ₹5 lakh during an emergency, whereas equities, gold ETFs, and REITs allow fractional liquidation.
2. **Macro Hedge Role of Gold:** Gold historically acts as a currency-debasement and geopolitical tail-risk hedge, often exhibiting low or negative correlation with equities during acute stress.
3. **Gross vs. Net Real Estate Yield:** Headline property appreciation frequently ignores stamp duty, interest on home loans, maintenance outflows, and illiquidity discounts during distressed sales.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **Prefer financialized wrappers:** Gold ETFs and listed REITs eliminate storage, purity, tenant, and title risks while preserving $T+1$ liquidity.
* **Account for all-in friction:** Always deduct stamp duty, GST, making charges, and maintenance costs when calculating historical returns on physical assets.
* **Avoid over-concentration in illiquid property:** Excessive allocation to a single real estate asset creates severe geographic and liquidity risk.
