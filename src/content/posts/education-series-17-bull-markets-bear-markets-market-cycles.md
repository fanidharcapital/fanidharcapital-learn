Stock markets never move in a straight line forever. Even over decades of long-term economic growth, indices like **Nifty 50** and **BSE Sensex** move in repeating waves of optimism, over-exuberance, fear, and recovery—known as **Market Cycles**.

Understanding where the market stands in a cycle helps you avoid buying blindly at euphoric tops or panic-selling quality businesses at cyclical bottoms.

---

## 1. Bull Market (Sustained Uptrend & Rising Confidence)

A **Bull Market** is a prolonged period in which stock prices rise broadly, supported by strong corporate earnings, economic expansion, and rising investor confidence.

* **Common Benchmark Rule:** Often defined broadly as a **20% or greater rise** from a recent major market bottom, sustained over months or years.
* **Key Characteristics:**
  * Strong GDP growth, healthy corporate profit expansion, and rising institutional/retail inflows (such as steady SIP participation and FII/DII buying).
  * High IPO activity, broad participation across large-, mid-, and small-cap stocks, and upbeat media headlines.
* **Hidden Risk for Beginners:** Late in a bull market, valuations stretch well ahead of actual earnings. Beginners often mistake a rising tide for personal skill and take excess leverage or buy weak businesses at peak multiples.

---

## 2. Correction vs. Bear Market (Pullbacks & Prolonged Downtrends)

Not every drop means the market is broken—and knowing the difference between a normal dip, a correction, and a bear market keeps perspective intact:

| Market Phase | Typical Decline from Peak | Duration & Nature | Common Triggers |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Routine Pullback / Dip** | **5% to 10%** | Days to a few weeks; normal part of any uptrend | Profit booking, minor global cues, short-term news |
| **Market Correction** | **10% to 20%** | Weeks to a few months; cools overheated valuations | Valuation resets, earnings misses, rate concerns |
| **Bear Market** | **20% or more** | Several months to 1+ years; broad pessimism & risk-off | Recession fears, liquidity crunches, macro/earnings shocks |

### Key Characteristics of a Bear Market
* Falling corporate earnings visibility, tight liquidity, and widespread risk aversion.
* Even fundamentally strong companies fall alongside weaker ones as investors raise cash.
* High-valuation, low-profit small-caps and thematic favorites typically fall much harder than established large-cap leaders.

---

## 3. The Four Phases of a Stock Market Cycle

Market cycles generally repeat through four recognizable stages:

1. **Accumulation Phase:**
   * Occurs after a prolonged downturn when valuations are cheap and public sentiment is still gloomy or indifferent.
   * Patient, long-term institutional and value-focused investors quietly accumulate quality stocks while headlines remain negative.
2. **Mark-Up Phase (Bull Trend):**
   * Earnings recover, economic indicators improve, and prices begin making higher highs and higher lows.
   * Media coverage turns positive, retail participation expands, and momentum builds across sectors—ending eventually in **euphoria** where valuations detach from fundamentals.
3. **Distribution Phase:**
   * Growth slows, interest rates or inflation may tighten, and early institutional buyers begin trimming positions into strong retail demand.
   * Indices move sideways with wider swings; rallies fail to hold highs as leadership narrows.
4. **Mark-Down Phase (Bear Trend):**
   * Selling pressure overtakes buying demand, key support levels break, and prices trend lower.
   * Late-stage panic or capitulation eventually washes out excess leverage and resets valuations—setting the stage for the next **Accumulation Phase**.

---

## 4. How Investors Navigate Full Market Cycles

* **Stick to Asset Allocation:** Keeping a deliberate balance between equities, debt/liquid funds, and emergency reserves prevents forced selling during a bear phase.
* **Continue Disciplined SIPs / Staggered Buying:** Falling prices during corrections and bear markets allow systematic investors to accumulate more units of quality funds or companies at lower valuations.
* **Separate Business Quality from Stock Price:** A temporary 25% drop in a debt-free, profitable market leader is very different from a permanent collapse in a weak, over-leveraged company.

---

## Key Takeaways

* **Cycles are permanent features of equity markets:** Bull markets build wealth, corrections cool excess valuation, and bear markets test discipline while creating future entry opportunities.
* **10% is a correction; 20%+ is a bear market:** Knowing standard thresholds stops you from treating every normal 6–8% pullback like a financial crisis.
* **Control emotion at extremes:** Avoid chasing overvalued stocks during late-stage bull euphoria, and avoid abandoning sound long-term plans during bear-market fear.
