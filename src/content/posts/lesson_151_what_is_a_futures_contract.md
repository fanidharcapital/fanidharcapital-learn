## 1. Core Mechanics of a Futures Contract

A **futures contract** is a legally binding agreement traded on a regulated exchange (such as NSE or BSE) to buy or sell a specified quantity of an underlying asset (an equity index or single stock) at a future date (expiry) at an agreed price:

*   **Standardization:** Unlike private forward contracts, exchange-traded futures standardize the **lot size**, **expiry date**, **tick size**, and **settlement rules** so every contract is liquid and fungible.
*   **Bilateral Obligation:** Every futures trade has a long (buyer) and a short (seller). Both sides are obligated—meaning both face symmetric mark-to-market profit or loss as price moves.
*   **Central Counterparty Clearing:** Clearing corporations guarantee settlement performance and collect upfront **margin** from participants to manage counterparty default risk.

---

## 2. Futures vs. Cash Equities vs. Options

| Feature | Cash / Spot Equities | Futures Contracts | Options Contracts |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Obligation** | Full delivery/payment upon settlement | **Binding obligation** for both buyer and seller until squared off or settled | Buyer has a **right (not obligation)**; seller has an obligation if assigned/exercised |
| **Capital Required** | Full share value (except intraday margin where offered) | **Margin deposit** (fraction of notional contract value) | Buyer pays **upfront premium**; seller posts **margin** |
| **Duration** | Perpetual (no expiry) | **Finite expiry** (near, mid, far month cycles) | **Finite expiry** (weekly/monthly cycles as applicable) |
| **P&L Behavior** | Linear with price; capped downside at zero | **Linear and leveraged** against initial margin | Non-linear (affected by time decay and volatility) |

## 3. Leverage & Price Discovery on NSE / BSE

Because you post **margin** rather than paying the full notional contract value upfront, futures provide **leverage**:

*   **Two-Sided Effect:** If a futures contract worth `₹10,00,000` requires `₹1,50,000` in margin (`~6.67×` notional exposure), a `2%` move in the underlying (`₹20,000`) equals roughly `13.3%` on your blocked margin *before* charges. Losses scale just as fast.
*   **Price Discovery Role:** Because futures attract high-frequency participants, institutions, hedgers, and index arbitragers with low friction, futures often react first to macro news, RBI/global cues, and institutional order flow—making futures charts a vital reference alongside spot and price-action structure.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

*   A futures contract is a **standardized, binding obligation** with a fixed expiry—not an optional right.
*   Margin creates **leverage**, magnifying percentage returns and losses relative to blocked capital.
*   Futures play a central role in **price discovery and hedging** on NSE and BSE.
