![Corporate Actions — Dividends, Bonus Shares, Splits & Rights Issues](/images/lessons/lesson-13.png)

Publicly listed companies on NSE and BSE do not just run businesses—they also make capital and shareholder-return decisions known as **Corporate Actions**. Whether a company shares profits via a **Dividend**, issues extra shares free via a **Bonus Issue**, divides existing shares via a **Stock Split**, or raises fresh capital from existing shareholders via a **Rights Issue**, every corporate action changes numbers you see in your portfolio.

Knowing how these events work—and how key dates govern eligibility—prevents panic when a stock price adjusts overnight or a share count suddenly multiplies.

---

## 1. Key Dates in Every Corporate Action (Under T+1 Settlement)

Before examining each corporate action, you need to know three dates:

* **Announcement / Board Meeting Date:** When the board proposes or approves the corporate action (subject to shareholder/regulatory approvals where required).
* **Record Date:** The cut-off date set by the company to check its register of shareholders eligible to receive the corporate action benefit.
* **Ex-Date (Ex-Dividend / Ex-Bonus / Ex-Split / Ex-Rights):** The first trading day on which a stock trades *without* the value of the upcoming corporate action. Under India's **T+1 settlement cycle**, the **Ex-Date is typically the same day as the Record Date** (or one trading day prior depending on the specific event mechanics)—meaning you must buy the shares **on or before the Ex-Date** so they settle in your Demat account by the Record Date.

---

## 2. Dividends

A **Dividend** is a cash distribution of a portion of a company's profits (or accumulated reserves) to its shareholders.

* **Types:** **Interim Dividend** (declared during the financial year by the Board) and **Final Dividend** (recommended by the Board and approved at the Annual General Meeting).
* **How It Is Quoted:** Often stated as a percentage of **Face Value** (e.g., 150% on a ₹2 face value share = ₹3 per share) or directly in rupees per share.
* **Price & Portfolio Impact:** On the **Ex-Dividend Date**, the stock price typically adjusts downward roughly by the per-share dividend amount, while the dividend cash is credited to your registered bank account (subject to TDS rules where applicable).

---

## 3. Bonus Shares

A **Bonus Issue** is the allotment of **additional free equity shares** to existing shareholders in a fixed ratio (such as **1:1**, **2:1**, or **1:5**) by capitalizing the company's free reserves or securities premium.

* **Example (1:1 Bonus):** If you hold **100 shares** at ₹1,000 each (₹1,00,000 total), a 1:1 bonus gives you **100 extra shares** free, making **200 shares** total.
* **Price & Portfolio Impact:**
  * Your **total share count increases**, while the **Face Value remains unchanged**.
  * On the **Ex-Bonus Date**, the market price adjusts downward proportionally (around ₹500 in the 1:1 example), so your **total investment value starts roughly the same** before normal market trading resumes.

---

## 4. Stock Splits

A **Stock Split** divides each existing share into multiple smaller shares by **reducing the Face Value** proportionally.

* **Example (1:5 Split on ₹10 Face Value):** One share with a ₹10 Face Value becomes **5 shares** with a **₹2 Face Value** each.
* **Why Companies Split Shares:** Lowering the per-share trading price can improve retail affordability and trading liquidity.
* **Bonus vs. Split Difference:** A **Bonus Issue** keeps Face Value unchanged and uses reserves; a **Stock Split** lowers Face Value and divides existing share capital without issuing "free" reserve shares.

---

## 5. Rights Issues

A **Rights Issue** is an offer by a listed company to **existing shareholders** to buy **additional shares directly from the company**, usually at a **discount to the prevailing market price**, in proportion to their current holding (for example, 1 rights share for every 4 shares held).

* **Key Feature:** Unlike a bonus issue, a rights issue is **not free**—you must pay the **Rights Issue Price** within the application window if you want the shares.
* **What If You Do Not Subscribe?** Depending on whether the rights issue is **renounceable** (as most listed rights issues are), you may either let your rights lapse or sell/renounce your rights entitlements (REs) on the exchange during the trading window, if applicable.

---

## 6. Side-by-Side Comparison

| Corporate Action | Cash / Shares to Investor | Payment Required? | Face Value Change? | Share Count Change? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dividend** | Cash paid to bank account | No | No | No |
| **Bonus Issue** | Additional equity shares | No (free) | No | Increases |
| **Stock Split** | Additional equity shares | No | Decreases proportionally | Increases |
| **Rights Issue** | Offer to buy new shares | Yes (issue price) | No (unless combined) | Increases if subscribed |

---

## Key Takeaways

* **Check the Ex-Date, not just the announcement:** Buying after the Ex-Date means you will not receive that corporate action benefit for those shares.
* **Bonus and splits do not create instant free wealth:** Your share count rises and per-share price adjusts proportionally on the ex-date; long-term wealth still depends on business fundamentals and earnings growth.
* **Read Rights Issue terms carefully:** Always check the issue price, entitlement ratio, record/ex-dates, and whether Rights Entitlements (REs) are tradable before deciding to subscribe, renounce, or ignore.
